A widowed mother sent every son she had to the war. Four went. These are the people behind the 272 letters.

The Hubbells farmed in Champlain, on New York's Canadian border. Frederick Hubbell died in 1853, leaving Frances to raise five children alone — and when the war came eight years later, she let all four of her sons enlist. What survives isn't a history written afterward. It's letters written during the war: from camps, hospitals, marches, and battlefields — and from a mother who read the casualty lists each morning with trembling hands.

Frances “Fannie” Hubbell
The eldest child and only daughter — a teacher who rose to preceptress of Champlain Academy. None of her own letters survive, but the brothers craved hers; she received at least 38, addressed “dear sister.”
