# Discrepancy: Henry's first letter dated "June 6, 1861" — but the 34th NY did not leave Albany until July 3

- **Date filed:** 2026-06-05
- **Severity:** notable
- **Status:** resolved
- **Persons involved:** `PER-hubbell-henry`
- **Letters involved:** `LTR-1861-06-06-001`
- **External sources involved:**
  - NY State Military Museum / Phisterer, 34th Infantry historical sketch — "The regiment left Albany for Washington on the 3d of July, 1861." (https://museum.dmna.ny.gov/unit-history/infantry/34th-infantry-regiment/historical-sketch)
  - Civil War Archive, 34th NY — "Left State for Washington, D.C., July 3, 1861." (https://www.civilwararchive.com/Unreghst/unnyinf3.htm)
  - NY AG roster — Henry "mustered in as private, Co. D, June 15, 1861." (https://dmna.ny.gov/historic/reghist/civil/rosters/Infantry/34th_Infantry_CW_Roster.pdf)

## What the family said
The first letter in the collection opens, in Henry's own hand: **"June 6th, 1861 / Washington, D.C. / Dear Mother — We arrived at Washington Friday eve about ten. We took the boat at Albany and went down the river..."** The letter is filed under id `LTR-1861-06-06-001`.

## What the record shows
The 34th New York was **mustered in June 15, 1861** at Albany and **did not leave Albany for Washington until July 3, 1861.** Henry could not have "arrived at Washington" and be writing from there on June 6 — his regiment was still in Albany (indeed, not yet mustered in). The events the letter describes (boat at Albany → down the Hudson → cars through Baltimore → Washington) are the regiment's **July** movement.

July 5, 1861 was a **Friday** — matching "arrived... Friday eve about ten" — which places the letter's writing on **Saturday, July 6, 1861.**

## What's likely true
The letter is **mis-dated by one month in its own heading**: Henry wrote "June" but meant **July 6, 1861.** This is a common slip in early-war soldier letters (the writer carrying the previous month). The collection had already flagged this independently — `all-letters.json` stores the corrected date `1861-07-06` with the note *"Retains original filing; correct date is July 6."* The external regimental-movement record now **independently corroborates** that correction: the July reading is not just plausible, it is the only one consistent with the regiment's documented departure.

**Confidence:** `external · clear · definite` on the regiment's July 3 departure; the corrected letter date is `inferred · clear · definite` (letter content + regimental movement + weekday alignment all converge).

## Why this matters
This is the **first letter in the entire collection** — the chronological anchor for everything that follows. Confirming July (not June) 1861 via an independent record:
1. Validates the project's existing date-correction discipline against external evidence — a clean methodology win for the press kit / paper ("we don't just transcribe; we verify against the record").
2. Tightens the collection's opening timeline by a month.
3. Demonstrates the cross-reference engine catching and *resolving* a dating question, not merely flagging it — exactly the kind of "filter, never a source" result Phase 3 promises.
