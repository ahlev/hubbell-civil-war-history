## What the family records said
The family genealogy (G03, "Emily's line") gives two **different** death dates for Alexander:
- **Dec 7, 1899**, with an official application #710.072 and certificate #658.842 (the certificate numbers lent this date apparent authority); and
- **1894** (a cemetery-stone transcription, G11), recorded elsewhere in the family materials.

The project's own README likewise carried "Died Nov 1894, Fonda, Iowa." So the conflict is *internal* to the family record: 1894 vs 1899.

## What the external record shows
The IAGenWeb Pocahontas County Civil War Soldiers Database — an independent compiled record keyed to the NY Adjutant-General report and a cemetery/marker record — gives, verbatim:

> Hubbell, Alexander F. … Buried: Cedar twp., Fonda … DOB: 28 Mar 1844 … **DOD: 7 Dec 1894** … Marker: **GAR star**

The DOB (28 Mar 1844) and burial (Cedar twp., Fonda) match the family genealogy exactly, anchoring this to *our* Alexander. Crucially, it pairs the family's **"Dec 7" day** with the **1894 year** — i.e., it reconciles the two family dates as a single event, **December 7, 1894**, implying the "1899" was a year transcription error.

## What's likely true
**Alexander died December 7, 1894.** The "Dec 7, 1899" in G03 most plausibly arose either as a transcription slip of the year, or by attaching a *pension* application/certificate number (which could post-date the death — e.g., a widow's claim or a bureau action) to the death line itself. A separate, suggestive corroboration: every Iowa newspaper "Hubbell" hit dated **1899** belongs to **Frederick M. Hubbell**, the unrelated Des Moines financier — so an 1899 "Hubbell" date is exactly the kind of thing that could be mis-imported into a family file.

**Confidence:** `external · n/a · likely` for Dec 7 1894. Two cheap handoffs would lift it to `definite`:
1. The **FindAGrave / WPA stone transcription** for Cedar Township Cemetery (the carved date governs).
2. The **widow's pension filing date** — if Lois filed as a widow in 1894–95, that independently times the death to 1894; the family's cert #658.842 can be checked there at the same time.
3. The **1900 census** as an absence test: if Alexander died in 1894 he is absent from 1900 and a widow-headed Lois household (with children Frederick/Affa/Wolcott/Helen) should appear.
