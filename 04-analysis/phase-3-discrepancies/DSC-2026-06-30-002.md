## What the site narrative says
James is labeled **"The Youngest"** throughout — `brother-james.html` (eyebrow + prose: *"the youngest of the four brothers"*, repeated ≥3×) and `who-they-were.html` (PEOPLE eyebrow `The Youngest`). This is the pervasive framing of James's identity on the site.

## What the resolved record shows
The sibling chronology already triangulated in **DSC-2026-06-30-001** (from roster ages + Glenwood gravestone + genealogy registers, with the register's *years* shown to be internally inconsistent) is:

> Fannie **Nov 1835** → Henry **~Nov 1837** → Charles **Apr 1840** → James **~Jan 1842/43** → Alexander (+ twin Julia) **Mar 28, 1844**

By that chronology the **youngest brother is Alexander (Mar 1844)**, not James (~Jan 1842/43). James was the *second*-youngest.

## Internal corroboration (already on the site)
The contradiction is already visible inside our own pages:
- `brother-alexander.html` prose states *"He was the youngest of the four brothers"* — which is **correct** by the resolved chronology.
- Alexander's own birth date on the site (**Mar 28, 1844**) is the latest of the four brothers.
So the site simultaneously calls **both** James and Alexander "the youngest." Alexander's claim matches the dates; James's does not.

## What's likely true
**Alexander was the youngest of the four soldier brothers.** James's "The Youngest" epithet is a narrative error — most likely inherited from the fact that James was the youngest *when he left for West Point* / the "boy at the academy" framing, conflated into a birth-order claim.

**Confidence:** `internal · clear · medium-high` — the chronology rests on the same roster/stone triangulation as DSC-001; the one open lever is the exact James vs. Alexander month gap, which a census pull would pin.
