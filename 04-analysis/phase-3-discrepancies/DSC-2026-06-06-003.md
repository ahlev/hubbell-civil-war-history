# Discrepancy: Charles's birth year — family "April 1840" vs. roster-implied "c. 1837"

- **Date filed:** 2026-06-06
- **Severity:** notable (a vital fact; affects his age and birth-order placement)
- **Status:** open — but **gravestone now corroborates the ~1837–38 (roster) side** (see update)

> **Update (2026-06-06, during the James sweep):** The Glenwood Champlain four-brothers memorial reads "Charles F. HUBBELL ... Died Mar. 21, 1875 / **AE. 37 Yrs.**" Age 37 at a March 1875 death → birth c. **1838** — which corroborates the roster's implied ~1837 over the genealogy's "April 1840." Two independent sources (roster age 25 in 1862; gravestone age 37 in 1875) now point to **c. 1837–1838**. The stone also gives Charles a PRECISE death date (Mar 21, 1875), refining the family's "March 1875." A census pull would finalize the exact year.

## What the family record says
The 9-generation family genealogy (G08) gives Charles Fullerton Hubbell **born April 1840**, Champlain.

## What the record shows
The NY Adjutant-General roster records, verbatim:
> HUBBELL, CHARLES F.—**Age, 25 years.** Enlisted, **August 30, 1862**, at Champlain...

Age 25 at enlistment in August 1862 implies a birth year of **c. 1836–1837** — roughly three years earlier than the family's "April 1840."

## What's likely true
This is an unresolved ~3-year discrepancy, and either side could be off:
- **Enlistment ages are frequently inexact** — men rounded, mis-stated, or were mis-recorded; a clerk's "25" is not a birth certificate.
- **The family genealogy** (compiled generations later) could equally carry a copied error; G08 gives only "April 1840" with no day.
- A third possibility: the "25" is correct and the genealogy's 1840 is the slip.

**No verdict is forced.** The cheapest resolutions are external and gated (handoffs):
1. **U.S. Census** — the 1850/1860 Champlain household (Charles as a child with his parents) gives an age that brackets the birth year; the 1870 Iowa census gives another.
2. **The pension file** — often records a stated birth date.
3. The **1840-vs-1837** question also lightly affects birth-order narrative (Charles relative to Henry, b. c. 1837–38 per Henry's own roster age 23 in 1861).

## Why this matters
1. It is a clean example of **record-vs-family vital-fact divergence** where, unlike the roster's authority on *service* facts, the roster's *age* field is itself only as good as what the enlistee said — so the engine flags rather than overrides.
2. It pairs with Henry's case (age 23 in May 1861 → b. c. 1837–38): if Charles was also born ~1837, the two brothers were nearly the same age, which the genealogy's "1840" obscures. The census will settle it.
