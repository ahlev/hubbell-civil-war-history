# Discrepancy: "Capt. Reich" (Henry's letters) vs. "Capt. Davis J. Rich" (the record)

- **Date filed:** 2026-06-05
- **Severity:** minor (orthographic) — but a clean, instructive resolution
- **Status:** resolved
- **Persons involved:** `PER-hubbell-henry`; the Co. D commander (Capt. Davis J. Rich)
- **Letters involved:** `LTR-1861-06-06-001`, `LTR-1861-07-21-001`, `LTR-1861-09-11-001`, `LTR-1861-09-22-001`, `LTR-1861-10-17-001`, `LTR-1862-01-09-001`, `LTR-1862-06-13-001`, `LTR-1862-06-22-002`, `LTR-1862-07-28-001` (9 letters)
- **External sources involved:**
  - NY State Military Museum / Phisterer, 34th Infantry historical sketch — "Company D | Champlain, Clinton co | Capt. Davis J. Rich" (https://museum.dmna.ny.gov/unit-history/infantry/34th-infantry-regiment/historical-sketch)
  - NY AG roster + Chapin (1903) — both list Co. D under Capt. Davis J. Rich; no officer named "Reich" appears anywhere in the 34th NY.

## What the family said
Across **nine** letters spanning June 1861–July 1862, Henry consistently names his company commander **"Capt. Reich"** — the officer who, in `LTR-1861-11-30-...`-era letters, pays five dollars for a captured deserter's knife and whose company Henry serves in (Co. D).

## What the record shows
The 34th NY's Company D — explicitly "recruited from Champlain, Clinton co." — was commanded by **Capt. Davis J. Rich** (surname **R-I-C-H**), accepted May 22, 1861, the same day Henry enlisted. No officer named Reich, Reichs, Roach, or Rouse exists in any 34th NY listing. There is exactly one Co. D captain by that founding date, and his name is **Rich**.

## What's likely true
Henry's **"Reich" is a phonetic spelling of "Rich."** Henry's letters are full of phonetic spellings (he asks his mother to "excuse bad spelling"), and "Rich" pronounced and re-rendered by ear easily becomes "Reich." This is not two different officers — it is one man, **Capt. Davis J. Rich of Co. D**, recorded by the record under his correct spelling and by Henry under an ear-spelling.

**Confidence:** `external · clear · definite` (the official roster and regimental history agree, and Co. D had a single founding captain). The identification of "Reich" = "Rich" is `inferred · clear · definite`.

## Why this matters
1. It demonstrates criterion **C7 (co-mention with known associates)** working in reverse — a named associate in the letters ("Capt. Reich") is *verified and corrected* against the record, strengthening the overall identity case for Henry himself.
2. It is a model **minor discrepancy**: not a contradiction, but a spelling the record resolves. It tells future readers that Henry's phonetic spellings of names should be treated as leads to verify, not as canonical.
