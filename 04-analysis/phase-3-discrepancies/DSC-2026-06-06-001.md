# Discrepancy: contemporary newspaper reports Henry "missing" — the official record says "killed"

- **Date filed:** 2026-06-06
- **Severity:** notable
- **Status:** resolved
- **Persons involved:** `PER-hubbell-henry`
- **Letters involved:** (none — this is record-vs-record, with letters as context)
- **External sources involved:**
  - The New York Herald, Sept 25, 1862, p.5 — 34th NY, Company D casualty list (https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83030313/1862-09-25/ed-1/?sp=5)
  - NY Adjutant-General roster + Chapin (1903) — "killed, September 17, 1862, at Antietam"
  - NY AG regimental Antietam tally: 32 killed / 109 wounded / **9 missing**

## What the immediate record said
Eight days after Antietam, the New York Herald printed the 34th NY's casualty list by company. In **Company D**, Henry Hubbell appears among the casualties **not as killed** — read from the page image as **MISSING** (the OCR ambiguously placed the jammed token "HenHubbell" in the Wounded run; the page image governs). Either way, the contemporary paper did **not** report him dead.

## What the settled record shows
The official NY Adjutant-General roster and Chapin's regimental history both record him **killed, September 17, 1862, at Antietam** — and Chapin's killed list (which folds in those who later died of wounds) names "Hubbell, Henry — D."

## What's likely true (resolution)
There is no contradiction — this is the **documentary signature of a soldier whose body was never recovered.** In the chaos after the West Woods fighting, men whose bodies could not be found or identified were first reported **missing** (Henry is almost certainly one of the **9 missing** in the regiment's own Antietam tally). Only after he failed to return was he reclassified **killed**. The newspaper preserves the interval the family lived through — the weeks of *not knowing* — that the final roster erases into a single word.

**Confidence:** `external · clear · definite` on both the newspaper "missing" reading (page image) and the final "killed" record; the missing→killed reconciliation is `inferred · clear · definite` (consistent with the cemetery null and the family tradition).

## Why this matters
1. It is the **closest-to-real-time trace of Henry** in any source — September 25, 1862.
2. It independently **corroborates "body never recovered"** from a third angle (newspaper "missing" + cemetery-roll absence + no grave).
3. It recovers something the bare roster cannot: the **human experience of the loss** — the family first learned Henry was *missing*, not dead. For the narrative/novel and the press kit, this is the emotional truth beneath the statistic.
4. It cross-binds the letters to the battle: the Co. D men listed beside Henry (John Green wounded, Peter Jolly killed) are the same comrades he named in his letters — see `letter-associates-crossref.json`.
