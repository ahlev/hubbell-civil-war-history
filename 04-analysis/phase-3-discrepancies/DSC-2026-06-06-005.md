## The conflict
- **NY AG roster:** "HUBBELL, JAMES.—**Age, 22 years.** Enlisted... mustered in... March 9, 1864" → birth c. **1842**.
- **Gravestone (1865 death):** "**AE. 27 Yrs.**" → birth c. **1838**.

A ~4–5-year gap.

## What's likely true
Unresolved; both sources have known failure modes:
- **Enlistment ages were frequently mis-stated** (rounded, or shaded to meet requirements). A clerk's "22" is not authoritative.
- **Gravestone ages** are supplied by grieving family and are also error-prone, sometimes by several years.
- Birth-order context lightly favors the *younger* reading: the family lists Alexander (b. 1844) as the youngest son and James just before him, so James born c. 1842 (roster) fits "second-youngest" better than c. 1838. But this is suggestive, not decisive.

**No verdict forced.** Resolution paths (handoffs): the **1850/1860 U.S. Census** Champlain household (James as a child) brackets the birth year; the **pension file** may state it.

## Why this matters
This is the **third** birth-year/age divergence the engine has surfaced across the brothers (cf. Charles, DSC-2026-06-06-003; Henry's age-23-in-1861). The pattern itself is a finding: **enlistment-age and gravestone-age fields are systematically soft**, and the family genealogy's birth years should be checked against censuses rather than assumed. A single census pull (the Champlain household pre-war) would likely resolve all of them at once.
