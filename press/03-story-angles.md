# Story Angles

*Each angle is a self-contained hook a journalist can run with: a framing + the specific proof points + which outlet types it fits. Adapted from `tasks/outreach-plan.md` Appendix A, plus the creator's verified Georgia connection.*

The first one is the strongest and most personal — use it as the default opening for local Georgia/Southern and Atlanta-Campaign outlets, and as a human frame everywhere.

### 1. "I live where my namesake marched" (the personal-Georgia angle) ★
A technology researcher in Athens, Georgia recovered his great-great-grandfather's Civil War letters with AI — and the ancestor he's named for, Alexander F. Hubbell of the 60th New York, fought the Atlanta Campaign across the same North Georgia ground the descendant now calls home. **Fits:** Athens/Atlanta/North-Georgia local; Atlanta-Campaign battlefield outlets; human-interest desks anywhere.

### 2. Local-family angle
"A Champlain, New York family's 273 Civil War letters — saved in a trunk for 160 years — are now an interactive archive." **Fits:** North Country / Champlain Valley / Plattsburgh local.

### 3. AI-and-archives angle
"What AI can do for the box of letters in everyone's attic — without making things up." **Fits:** tech, general-interest, genealogy how-to.

### 4. Methodology angle
"How one person did what used to take a research team — and the rule that kept the AI honest (*filter, never a source*)." **Fits:** digital-humanities, tech-craft, library/archives.

### 5. Discovery angle
"What the public record knew about these brothers that their own family never did." **Fits:** Civil War press, genealogy, history blogs. **Proof:** the four hero cross-references (cross-brother officer link; a soldier reported missing then confirmed killed; a verified West Point claim; an independently corroborated battle wound).

### 6. Human / brother-vs-brother angle
"Five people, one war, one return address — what four brothers in four theaters saw differently." **Fits:** narrative/feature desks.

### 7. Information-delay angle
"What they didn't know — letters as a study in the lag between event and news." **Fits:** features, data-journalism.

### 8. Money/economy angle
"What $X in 1862 bought — and worried — a soldier's family." **Fits:** economics/history.

### 9. Health/medicine angle
"Illness, hospital stays, recovery — Civil War medicine through one family's letters." **Fits:** history-of-medicine, general history.

### 10. Visualization/design angle
"Making 273 letters legible at a glance." **Fits:** design, data-viz, tech.

### 11. Genealogy how-to angle
"What I'd tell anyone with a similar box at home." **Fits:** genealogy press, podcasts.

### 12. Future-of-archives angle
"If every family did this, what would historians have in fifty years?" **Fits:** library/archives, digital-humanities, big-think tech.

---
*Each pitch picks ONE angle (occasionally a primary + secondary) weighted to the outlet — see each dossier's "way-in."*
