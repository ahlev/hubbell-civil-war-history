# Story Angles — v2 (asset-keyed, one story per audience)

*v2 principle: every pitch leads with exactly ONE asset chosen for the person — never with the project. The asset→best-home map lives in `targets-v2/SHORTLIST.md`. These are the stories those assets carry.*

## 1. The Antietam triptych → Antietam community, narrative desks
A mother in Champlain, NY reads the casualty lists "day after day... with trembling" and writes to her son Henry — who has been dead in the West Woods for four days, his body never recovered. His brother, also on that field, searches for him. Seven months later a third brother stands in Maryland asking who can show him where the 34th New York fought. *A family's disaster arriving at the speed of 1862 information, in their own hands.*

## 2. The July 4, 1863 letter → Gettysburg community, national CW media
An unpublished eyewitness letter written ON the Gettysburg battlefield the day after Culp's Hill: a 60th New York soldier counts his regiment's 52 casualties, names his company's dead and wounded, and reports "we are burying our and the rebel's dead today." *Primary evidence from the hill that saved the Union right — offered first to the people who've spent careers on it.*

## 3. The correction → genealogy, Civil War memory writers, public history
The family remembered James's death date for 160 years — and remembered it wrong, fused with the anniversary of his Cedar Creek wound. The archive built to honor the family's memory ended up correcting it, by triangulating muster rolls, a cemetery monument, and period newspapers. *What happens when a family's story meets the record — and the record wins, kindly.*

## 4. The information lag → data-driven essays, features
The archive computes the gap between when things happened and when each writer could know them — culminating in the mother writing to a son four days dead, and reading casualty lists "with trembling" while the answer was already fixed. *One family's war at the speed of the mail.* Dates are facts; this angle survives any scrutiny.

> **Retired as a standalone angle (2026-07-08, user call):** the quantified "asymmetry of register" finding (24% vs 2%, 17 matched pairs) is held back from press-facing use — not robust enough under scrutiny to lead with. The *qualitative* contrast between specific letters (James's plain-spoken wound account to his brother vs. reassurance in letters home) may still be used inside other stories, since it rests on verbatim documents, not codings.

## 5. The stewardship chain → genealogy, local press, human-interest
Frances saved the letters. Gladys typed all 272 on a typewriter, 1947–49. Fred wrote the introduction, 1996. Bruce scanned them, 2010s. Alexander structured and published them with AI, 2026. *"Every generation used the tools it had" — a 160-year family relay ending in an archive anyone can read.*

## 6. The discipline → AI/tech, digital humanities, archives, genealogy-AI
One person + AI did what used to take a research team — under a published rule ("the AI is a filter, never a source"), with curated identity tables instead of fuzzy matching, a confidence model, and a public registry of everything that didn't line up. *The counter-example to AI slop: receipts included.*

## 7. The resource → educators, institutions, historians
272 letters, both directions, all four years and after; four regiments across every major theater; searchable, visualized, free, citable (stable letter IDs, citation guide, documented method). *Not a story about the archive — the archive as infrastructure for other people's work.*

## 8. The homefront / the mother's war → women's history, religious history, features
Frances's letters are the war from the kitchen table: casualty lists, prayer, farm economics, four sons away. Her faith — "commit your all into His hands" — is the collection's connective tissue, and the brothers' memorial was raised by her church's Sabbath School. *The war as a mother lived it, in her own hand.*

## 9. The Athens angle → Georgia media, Atlanta-Campaign community
A technology researcher in Athens, Georgia recovers his great-great-grandfather's letters — and finds that the ancestor he's named for fought across the same North Georgia ground he now calls home, from Lookout Mountain (his wound corroborated in the records) to the fall of Atlanta. *Used sparingly and locally; one clause about being named at Gettysburg where natural, never forced.*
