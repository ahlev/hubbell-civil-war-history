## 3. Why them
Downey has spent ~30 years doing exactly what this project does, one battle deeper: collecting, structuring, and publishing soldier-level Civil War data online. He is the single most relevant person alive to the AOTW-meets-Hubbell story, and as an information-management person he will appreciate the data methodology, not just the sentiment.

## 4. Way-in (history first)
Lead: Henry Hubbell of the **34th New York Infantry** was killed at Antietam on September 17, 1862, and his body was never recovered. The public record first reported him **missing** before he was confirmed **killed** — the kind of record-correction AOTW does at scale. The supporting proof point: the family recovered and structured 273 of their Civil War letters (1861–1870) using modern AI, then cross-referenced the soldiers against the archival record. Offer Downey a soldier record (and any letter content) for the 34th NY at Antietam for AOTW.

## 5. Personalization hook
AOTW's ~2,000 soldier biographies and its long-running effort to correlate fragmentary, contradictory records into a single authoritative entry — cited: [AOTW About](https://antietam.aotw.org/exhibit.php?exhibit_id=3). The "missing → killed" discrepancy in Henry's record is precisely the type of correction AOTW exists to make.
