## 3. Why them
Clemens is a Hagerstown-rooted academic authority on the exact battle where Henry died and a leader who speaks to local audiences regularly. His credibility and reach in Washington County make him both a validator and a potential amplifier of a well-sourced discovery.

## 4. Way-in (history first)
Lead: Henry Hubbell, 34th NY, killed at Antietam Sept 17, 1862, body never recovered; record first **missing**, then **killed**. The 273 Hubbell letters (1861–1870), recovered and structured with modern AI and cross-referenced to the record, are offered as a primary source and a local-history hook. AI is the supporting proof point; the discovery and the preservation theme (a soldier with no grave) lead.

## 5. Personalization hook
Clemens spent years reconstructing Ezra Carman's painstaking Antietam record into a usable modern edition — cited: [Blue and Gray Education Society interview](https://blueandgrayeducation.org/2019/07/chatting-with-thomas-clemens-about-saving-antietam/). That is the same labor — turning fragmentary 1862 records into something legible — applied to one family here.
