**3. Why-them**
Eastman is the field's most established chronicler of genealogy-meets-technology. He routinely covers new tools and how researchers use them. A first-person case of using AI as a disciplined judge over a real family archive — with explicit guardrails — is exactly the kind of practical, tech-forward story his daily readers expect, and his coverage tends to set the agenda for what genealogists try next.

**4. Way-in (genealogy-first)**
Lead with what a descendant did with a box of family letters and what readers could replicate: the stewardship chain and the structured, cross-referenced archive. Then, for his tech-savvy audience, foreground the method as a model: AI used to transcribe, structure, and cross-reference against public archives (incl. Library of Congress newspapers), but strictly as a filter — "never a source" — with identity curated, never fuzzy-matched. Offer a guest write-up or an interview/news note.

**5. Personalization hook**
Eastman has long covered how new technology changes everyday genealogy and how to use it responsibly. A concrete, finished project that shows AI kept on a tight leash — and the family facts it helped verify against the public record — is a natural EOGN item. Source: https://www.familysearch.org/en/blog/dick-eastman-genealogy-newsletter
