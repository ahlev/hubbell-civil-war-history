**3. Why-them**
Family Tree's readers are exactly the people who have a shoebox (or attic, or scanned PDF) of family letters and wonder what to do with them. Koch commissions practical, replicable how-to content. A descendant who transcribed, structured, and cross-referenced 273 letters is a ready-made "here's how you could do this too" feature — with a real, finished archive to point at.

**4. Way-in (genealogy-first)**
Lead with what a descendant did with a box of family letters and what any family could replicate: a five-generation chain of stewardship (Gladys typed them 1947–49; Bruce digitized them; I structured and published them in 2026), then the method — AI used strictly as a disciplined filter to transcribe, structure, and check against public archives, with identity curated by hand, never fuzzy-matched. Frame it as a workflow readers can adapt. Secondary: the discipline ("filter, never a source") as a guardrail for anyone tempted to let AI invent.

**5. Personalization hook**
Koch's 2025 year-in-review podcast episode took stock of how AI and full-text search (e.g., FamilySearch's full-text search) are changing everyday genealogy. A first-person account of using AI as a careful judge — not an oracle — over a real family archive is a natural follow-up. Source: https://familytreemagazine.com/podcasts/reflecting-on-the-world-of-genealogy-in-2025/
