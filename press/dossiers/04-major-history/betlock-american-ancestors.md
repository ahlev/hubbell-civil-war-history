**3. Why-them**
NEHGS members are serious family historians who value provenance and sourcing — the exact audience for a methodology that treats AI as a disciplined judge rather than a generator. Betlock both edits and commissions, and the magazine explicitly seeks member stories. A first-person, rigorously sourced account of recovering a 19th-century New England/New York family archive fits the magazine's blend of the popular and the scholarly.

**4. Way-in (genealogy-first)**
Lead with what a descendant did with a box of family letters, and what any member could replicate: the five-generation stewardship chain and the structured, cross-referenced result. Then the rigor — public record as the source, AI strictly as a filter, identity curated by hand — pitched to readers who care about citation. Note the New England/New York provenance (Champlain, NY; brothers later in Iowa) as on-brand for "New England, New York, and Beyond."

**5. Personalization hook**
American Ancestors actively solicits members' research stories and "brick wall" narratives (submissions to magazine@nehgs.org). A documented case of using the public record to fill gaps the family itself never knew — soldier-brothers' service details surfaced from archives — is squarely the kind of member story the magazine runs. Source: https://www.americanancestors.org/browse/publications/american-ancestors-magazine/contribute
