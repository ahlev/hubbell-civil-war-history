**3. Why-them**
Appelbaum is a working historian who runs the Ideas section — uniquely positioned to see a family-archive project as both a human story and an argument about American history and memory. He cares about how the American past is recorded and mythologized, which is precisely the terrain a recovered Union family's correspondence occupies.

**4. Way-in (archives/future-of-archives)**
Lead with the idea: as digitization makes family papers structurable and checkable against the public record, the raw material of American history is quietly democratizing. Anchor in the project — 273 Hubbell letters cross-referenced against the Library of Congress's Chronicling America newspapers — and in the discipline (AI as filter, never source; curated identity). Frame for Ideas as an argument: what it means for democracy and memory when ordinary families can author and verify their own place in the record.

**5. Personalization hook**
Appelbaum's "Stuck" is about American mobility and opportunity — and the Hubbell brothers are a mobility story in microcosm: Champlain, NY enlistees who fought across multiple theaters and resettled as far as Iowa, their movements now traceable against the newspaper record. A natural Ideas hook on how ordinary American lives get (and don't get) written down. Source: https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/authors/2313272/yoni-appelbaum/
