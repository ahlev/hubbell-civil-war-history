## 3. Why them
Jordan's scholarly life is, almost precisely, the Hubbell story's subject: *Marching Home: Union Veterans and Their Unending Civil War* (Liveright/Norton, 2015; **2016 Pulitzer finalist**) is about the men who survived and the dead who didn't come home. A soldier reported missing then killed at Antietam — body never recovered — and a family's long postwar grief in 273 letters land directly in his expertise and his Monitor review desk.

## 4. Way-in (discovery + method first)
- **Henry Hubbell, 34th NY** — **missing → killed** at **Antietam**; body never recovered (his unrecovered death is the exact "unending Civil War" Jordan writes about).
- **Capt. Davis J. Rich** commanded two different Hubbell brothers in two different regiments — a cross-brother officer link.
- **A family West Point claim** verified by cross-binding independent records.
- **An independently corroborated Lookout Mountain wound** to Alexander F. Hubbell, 60th NY.
Method: *"filter, never a source"* — AI used only as a disciplined judge over real archival records; **never invents a record**. The 273 letters (1861–1870) carry the postwar grief through to 1870.

## 5. Personalization hook
*Marching Home* centers on Union veterans' unresolved grief and the war's missing and dead — cited: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Matthew_Jordan). The Hubbell letters give him a documented family case: a brother whose body was never recovered, and a mother and surviving brothers writing through the aftermath into 1870.
