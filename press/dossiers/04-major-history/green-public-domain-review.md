**3. Why-them**
PDR is built around bringing primary historical material to a wide, curious audience — and around the idea that opening archives changes what culture can see. A digitized, structured, cross-referenced family correspondence from the 1860s is precisely the kind of recovered primary source PDR champions, with a methodology story attached.

**4. Way-in (archives/future-of-archives)**
Lead with the future-of-archives idea PDR already embodies: every family archive that gets structured and cross-referenced enlarges the shared record. Connect to the Library of Congress's Chronicling America — the project cross-references 273 letters against digitized historical newspapers. Then the discipline that keeps it honest: AI strictly as a filter, never a source; curated, never fuzzy identity. Offer an essay on a specific thread from the letters, or on the method itself as a model for "domesticating" the archive.

**5. Personalization hook**
PDR's managing editor Hunter Dukes has written essays drawn from 19th-century ephemera and personal writing (e.g., Mary MacLane's scandalous 1902 journal). A Civil War family's recovered correspondence — with the editorial taste to pull out the strangest, most human threads — fits PDR's sensibility exactly. Source: https://publicdomainreview.org/contributor/hunter-dukes/
