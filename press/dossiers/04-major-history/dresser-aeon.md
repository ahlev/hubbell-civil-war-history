**3. Why-them**
Aeon publishes the "big idea behind the specific story" essay better than almost anyone, and Dresser is a long-tenured commissioning editor there. A family-letters project is the specific story; the future of memory and archives in an age of cheap digitization is the Aeon-sized idea behind it.

**4. Way-in (archives/future-of-archives)**
Lead with the idea, not the artifact: what happens to historical memory if every family can now structure, search, and cross-reference its own papers against the public record? Anchor it in the concrete project — 273 Hubbell letters cross-referenced against the Library of Congress's Chronicling America newspapers — and in the discipline that makes it trustworthy (AI as a filter, never a source; identity curated, never fuzzy). Offer a single Aeon essay on the future of the personal archive.

**5. Personalization hook**
Aeon's editorial line (per editorial director Brigid Hains, a trained environmental historian) is to let a vivid particular carry a serious idea. A descendant rebuilding a 19th-century family's record by hand-disciplined AI is a clean vehicle for an essay on memory, evidence, and what we owe the dead. Source: https://aeon.co/people
