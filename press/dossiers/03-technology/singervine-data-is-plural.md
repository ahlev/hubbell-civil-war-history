## 3. Why them
*Data Is Plural* exists to surface exactly one thing: a genuinely useful or curious **dataset**. This project converts 273 family letters into a structured, queryable corpus — with people and place registries, confidence scoring, and per-letter fields — which is precisely the kind of artifact a DIP entry is built around. It's curious (a 19th-century family in data form) and useful (a worked example of structuring a primary-source archive).

## 4. Way-in (tech-craft lead)
- **Lead — the dataset itself:** A three-layer schema (fact / editorial judgment / computed analysis) with a three-axis confidence model turns loose correspondence into a real dataset: 273 letters, 486 people, 353 places, all curated. That's a DIP-shaped object.
- **Secondary — the method:** curated-not-fuzzy identity resolution and "AI as filter, never a source" — a reproducible pattern others with a box of letters could copy.

## 5. Personalization hook
He founded the Data Liberation Project to "identify, obtain, document, and publish" datasets of public interest — the same documentation-first ethic this project applied to a private family archive. The pitch should frame the corpus as a small, fully-documented liberation of a primary source. (Cited: https://www.jsvine.com/)
