## 3. Why them
Thompson's enduring subject is what technology does to and for *people* — and his Smithsonian column is specifically about the history of technology. A descendant using modern AI to recover, structure, and humanize a 160-year-old family archive — carefully, without letting the model invent — is squarely his beat: a humane, skeptical, tech-meets-history story with a real person at the center.

## 4. Way-in (general-tech features lead)
- **Lead — humane applied AI:** the story of one person using AI as a disciplined assistant on a real personal archive, *without* hallucination — 273 letters recovered, transcribed, and made explorable, with the model kept out of the source-of-truth layer.
- **Secondary — history-of-technology resonance:** the letters themselves are a study in 1860s information delay (the lag between event and news), an irresistible counterpoint for a tech-history columnist.

## 5. Personalization hook
*Coders* and his Wired/Smithsonian columns repeatedly ask how tools change human understanding. The pitch should foreground the human frame: a namesake descendant, modern AI used responsibly, a family's war made legible. (Cited: https://www.clivethompson.net/bio)
