## 3. Why them
Thorp's argument in *Living in Data* is that data is most powerful when it's tied back to human lives and treated with care. This project is that thesis made literal: 273 letters from real people, turned into data without losing the people — a social network of 486 names, a health ledger of real illnesses, a money story of real worry. He's drawn to data that remembers it came from humans, which is the entire premise here.

## 4. Way-in (tech-craft / humanistic lead)
- **Lead — humanistic data art:** letters-as-data that keep their humanity — curated people and place registries, an emotion field per letter, a health ledger. Material for the kind of person-first data work he champions.
- **Secondary — provenance ethic:** display-layer enrichments never overwrite source data; AI is a filter, never a source — a "putting people (and provenance) first" stance that echoes his own.

## 5. Personalization hook
*Living in Data* and pieces like "A Sort of Joy" (his MoMA collaboration) center the human story inside the dataset. The pitch should lead with the people, not the pipeline: four brothers, a mother, a decade, rendered as data that still feels like a family. (Cited: https://www.jerthorp.me/living-in-data)
