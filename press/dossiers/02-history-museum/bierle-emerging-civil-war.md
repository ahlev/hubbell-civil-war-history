## 3. Why them
Bierle's stated mission — to surface "real people's everyday experiences" and civilian-military interaction — is the heart of the Hubbell corpus, where a mother (Frances) and four soldier sons write back and forth across nine years. Her home-front and women's-history interest is the natural entry to the project's most under-told thread: Frances Hubbell holding a family together while her sons scatter across two theaters. Her ABT education role also makes her an amplifier to teachers and students.

## 4. Way-in (history-first)
- **Lead — the discovery + the family:** 273 Hubbell letters (1861–1870), Champlain, NY; four brothers across both theaters; mother Frances at home. Henry (34th NY) killed at Antietam — reported missing, then confirmed killed. Alexander (60th NY) wounded at Lookout Mountain, then the Atlanta Campaign. Charles and James in the 153rd NY.
- **Home-front hook (tailored to Bierle):** the corpus is unusually rich in the civilian side — Frances's letters and the family's home-front economy and grief — the "everyday experience" she writes about.
- **Method as proof point:** AI used strictly as a disciplined judge over real records — "filter, never a source."

## 5. Personalization hook
Her bio says she writes about "real people's everyday experiences during challenging historical periods" and "civilian-military interactions." The hook: Frances Hubbell's correspondence is a sustained, datable record of exactly that — a mother's everyday wartime experience over nine years.
