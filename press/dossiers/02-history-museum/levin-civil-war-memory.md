## 3. Why them
Levin is the field's leading commentator on *how* we recover, present, and interpret Civil War evidence — exactly the meta-question the Hubbell project raises by using AI as a disciplined judge over real records. He prizes close readings of primary sources and is skeptical of shortcuts and myth, so the "filter, never a source" ethic is a natural fit for a thoughtful Civil War Memory post or a conversation piece (a format he runs often).

## 4. Way-in (history-first)
- **Lead — the discovery + the family:** 273 Hubbell letters (1861–1870), Champlain, NY. Henry (34th NY) killed at Antietam — reported missing, then confirmed killed. Alexander (60th NY) wounded at Lookout Mountain, then the Atlanta Campaign. Charles and James in the 153rd NY. Mother Frances at home.
- **Memory angle (tailored to Levin):** five generations of stewardship — Gladys typed the letters 1947–49, Bruce Levitt digitized them, and the namesake great-great-grandson published them in 2026 — a living case study in how a family remembers and transmits its war.
- **Method as proof point:** AI used strictly as a disciplined judge over real records — "filter, never a source."
