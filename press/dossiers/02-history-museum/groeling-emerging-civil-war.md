## 3. Why them
Groeling wrote *The Aftermath of Battle: The Burial of the Civil War Dead* (ECW Series) and is the field's go-to popular writer on what happens to soldiers' bodies, families, and graves after the fighting. That is the precise register of Henry Hubbell's story — 34th NY, reported missing at Antietam, then confirmed killed — and of the family's grief in the letters. Her Ellsworth work is also a New York thread, like the Hubbells.

## 4. Way-in (history-first)
- **Lead — the discovery + the family:** 273 Hubbell letters (1861–1870), Champlain, NY. Henry (34th NY) killed at Antietam — reported missing, then confirmed killed; the letters capture the family's search and grief. Alexander (60th NY) wounded at Lookout Mountain, then the Atlanta Campaign. Charles and James in the 153rd NY. Mother Frances at home.
- **Aftermath-of-battle hook (tailored to Groeling):** the corpus is a primary-source case study in exactly her subject — a family absorbing a battlefield death from a distance, with all the period's uncertainty about the missing and the dead.
- **Method as proof point:** AI used strictly as a disciplined judge over real records — "filter, never a source."
