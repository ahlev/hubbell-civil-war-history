## 3. Why them
Condon is a working public historian who has thought publicly about "the future of public history" (his Q&A with Kevin Levin) and who built a regional digital-history platform from scratch — kindred to the Hubbell dashboard. He champions soldier stories and accessible digital presentation, so an AI-assisted, sourced family archive sits squarely in his interests, both as content and as a public-history method case.

## 4. Way-in (history-first)
- **Lead — the discovery + the family:** 273 Hubbell letters (1861–1870), Champlain, NY. Henry (34th NY) killed at Antietam — reported missing, then confirmed killed. Alexander (60th NY) wounded at Lookout Mountain, then the Atlanta Campaign. Charles and James in the 153rd NY. Mother Frances at home.
- **Public-history method hook (tailored to Condon):** the dashboard is a small, replicable model of digital public history for a single family's records — the kind of project he discusses.
- **Method as proof point:** AI used strictly as a disciplined judge over real records — "filter, never a source."

## 5. Personalization hook
His Civil War Monitor essay "The Future of Public History" and his Levin Q&A center on how public history adapts and reaches people. The hook: a concrete, sourced example of new-tool public history — recovering 273 letters with AI as a disciplined judge — offered as both a story and a method to kick around. Cited: https://www.civilwarmonitor.com/what-is-public-history/ ; https://kevinmlevin.substack.com/p/the-future-of-public-history-a-conversation
