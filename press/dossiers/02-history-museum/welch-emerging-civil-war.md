## 3. Why them
Welch wrote a book on Antietam (*Ohio at Antietam*) and works the intersection of battle, memorialization, and cemetery history — the exact intersection where Henry Hubbell's story sits: a New York soldier killed at Antietam, first reported missing. His education background (NPS, Gettysburg Foundation, classroom teacher) also makes him an amplifier toward students and the visiting public.

## 4. Way-in (history-first)
- **Lead — the discovery + the family:** 273 Hubbell letters (1861–1870), Champlain, NY. Henry (34th NY) killed at Antietam — reported missing, then confirmed killed. Alexander (60th NY) wounded at Lookout Mountain, then the Atlanta Campaign. Charles and James in the 153rd NY. Mother Frances at home.
- **Antietam + memorialization hook (tailored to Welch):** Henry's missing-then-killed arc and the family's grief are a primary-source case in exactly his Antietam-and-memorialization wheelhouse.
- **Method as proof point:** AI used strictly as a disciplined judge over real records — "filter, never a source."
