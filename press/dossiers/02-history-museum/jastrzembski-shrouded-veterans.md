## 3. Why them
Jastrzembski's entire mission is recovering the names and resting places of forgotten soldiers — which maps directly onto Henry Hubbell, 34th NY, killed at Antietam after first being reported missing. The Hubbell letters contain the family's own search for what happened to Henry, the period's defining "missing soldier" anguish. That is precisely the human and documentary terrain Shrouded Veterans works in.

## 4. Way-in (history-first)
- **Lead — the discovery + the family:** Four Hubbell brothers, Champlain, NY; 273 letters (1861–1870). Henry (34th NY) reported missing at Antietam, then confirmed killed — the family's letters trace the dread and the confirmation. Alexander (60th NY) wounded at Lookout Mountain, then the Atlanta Campaign. Charles and James in the 153rd NY.
- **Grave/memory tie-in:** the corpus is a primary-source case of a 19th-century family chasing a missing soldier's fate — adjacent to the records Shrouded Veterans uses to recover such men.
- **Method as proof point:** AI used strictly as a disciplined judge over real records — "filter, never a source."

## 5. Personalization hook
His ECW bio describes Shrouded Veterans as "committed to rescuing the graves of 19th-century veterans...by identifying, marking, and restoring them." The hook: the Hubbell letters are a documentary mirror of that work — a family's real-time effort to learn whether Henry was missing or dead at Antietam — and an offer to share what the records show. Cited: https://emergingcivilwar.com/author-biographies/authors/frank-jastrzembski/
