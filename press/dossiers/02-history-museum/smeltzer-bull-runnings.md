## 3. Why them
Smeltzer's entire project is the disciplined digitization of soldier primary sources — exactly the Hubbell undertaking, one battle's worth at a time. He is also a Save Historic Antietam Foundation board member, which links straight to Henry Hubbell, killed at Antietam. He thinks publicly about *the process* of turning paper records into structured, searchable archives, so the method here will interest him as much as the content.

## 4. Way-in (history-first)
- **Lead — the discovery + the family:** Four Hubbell brothers of Champlain, NY served across both theaters; their mother Frances and the brothers left 273 letters (1861–1870). Henry (34th NY) was killed at Antietam — first reported missing, then confirmed killed. Alexander (60th NY) was wounded at Lookout Mountain and fought the Atlanta Campaign. Charles and James served in the 153rd NY.
- **Cross-brother officer link:** Davis J. Rich commanded companies of two different brothers in two different regiments — the kind of connective tissue Smeltzer's source-by-source work surfaces.
- **Method as proof point:** AI was used strictly as a disciplined judge over real records — "filter, never a source" — the same provenance ethic his project lives by.
