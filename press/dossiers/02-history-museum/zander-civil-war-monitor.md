## 3. Why them
Zander writes for the Monitor's blog about soldiers and the army as an institution, and she values primary-source-grounded argument (her book rests on close reading of the political record). A 273-letter enlisted-and-family corpus — ordinary New York soldiers across two theaters — is the kind of ground-level material that complements her institutional, top-down view of the army. She is also a frequent Q&A subject, a format that could surface the project.

## 4. Way-in (history-first)
- **Lead — the discovery + the family:** 273 Hubbell letters (1861–1870), Champlain, NY. Henry (34th NY) killed at Antietam — reported missing, then confirmed killed. Alexander (60th NY) wounded at Lookout Mountain, then the Atlanta Campaign. Charles and James in the 153rd NY. Mother Frances at home.
- **Soldier's-eye hook (tailored to Zander):** the letters are a sustained enlisted-and-home-front view of the volunteer army she studies from the policy level — the regular soldiers she argues were chronically undervalued.
- **Method as proof point:** AI used strictly as a disciplined judge over real records — "filter, never a source."

## 5. Personalization hook
*The Army Under Fire* argues that political leaders misunderstood the value of the regular soldiers fighting to reunify the country. The hook: 273 letters are a vivid, granular record of exactly those undervalued soldiers and their families — useful primary texture beneath her institutional argument. Cited: https://lsupress.org/9780807181409/the-army-under-fire/
