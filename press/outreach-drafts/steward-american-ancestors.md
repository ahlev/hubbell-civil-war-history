Hi Scott,

I've followed Vita Brevis as the place where staff and members put their own research out into the light — your "thousandth post" note caught the spirit of it.

I'd like to offer a guest essay from my own family. The Hubbells were a Champlain, New York family; four brothers enlisted in 1861, and they and their mother Frances wrote home across four theaters of the war. 273 letters survived — typed up by a relative in the late 1940s, digitized by my father, and this past year transcribed, structured, and published by me as an interactive archive. I'm the great-great-grandson and namesake of one of the brothers, and the line ended up in Iowa.

I'd write it genealogy-first: the family and the letters, then a candid note on method — identity resolved by curated alias tables, never fuzzy guesses; the tools organized and checked the record but never invented it. Mostly: what any family could do with a shoebox of letters.
