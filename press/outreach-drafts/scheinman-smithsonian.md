Dear Mr. Scheinman,

Your culture writing tends to find the human nerve inside a historical practice, so I wanted to bring you one I lived this year.

My family kept 273 Civil War letters: four Hubbell brothers and their mother, Frances, of Champlain, NY, 1861–1870. I transcribed, structured, and published the whole correspondence and cross-referenced it against the Library of Congress's digitized newspapers. The discipline mattered more than the technology: I used AI strictly as a filter, never a source, and identified every person by hand — so the tools could organize and check, but never invent an ancestor.

The piece I'd like to write is the cultural one: what it means that the tools of professional archives now reach every attic, and what we owe the dead when we use them. Grounded in a real, finished archive. One link first: [dashboard link]
