Your "From the age of dial-up" piece stuck with me — partly because my own project is about old tools meeting new ones, carefully.

The Hubbells were a Champlain, New York family. Four brothers enlisted in 1861; they and their mother Frances wrote home across four theaters of the war. 273 letters survived, were typed by a relative in the late 1940s, digitized by my father, and this past year I transcribed, structured, and published the whole correspondence as an interactive archive. I'm the great-great-grandson and namesake of one of the brothers.

What I think would interest you specifically: how the public military and pension record fit against the letters — a few things the archives knew about these men that the family never did — and how I kept identity honest using curated alias tables rather than fuzzy matching.
