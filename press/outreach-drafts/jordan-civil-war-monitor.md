**Subject:** A Union family's "unending Civil War," in 273 letters

Professor Jordan,

*Marching Home* has sat with me for years, so I wanted to bring you a documented case of exactly what it describes. Four Hubbell brothers from Champlain, New York, served in the Union army; with their mother Frances, they left 273 letters running from 1861 all the way to 1870 — well past Appomattox, into the grief.

Henry, of the 34th New York, was reported *missing* at Antietam and only later confirmed *killed*; his body was never recovered. You can watch the family absorb that, and keep writing, for years afterward. Working the letters against pension and muster records also surfaced a cross-brother link — one captain, Davis J. Rich, commanded two of the brothers in different regiments. I used AI only as a disciplined check over real documents; it never invents a record.
