Dear Ms. Betlock,

American Ancestors is one of the few magazines that keeps scholarship and readability in the same frame, so I'd like to offer it a family story built that way.

The Hubbells were a Champlain, NY family — four brothers who enlisted in 1861, and their mother, Frances, writing home across the war and after; two brothers later settled in Iowa. 273 of their letters survived. My great-grandmother typed them in the late 1940s; my father digitized them; this year I transcribed, structured, and published the full correspondence, cross-referencing it against public archives, including Library of Congress newspapers. The public record was always the source — I used AI only to organize and check it, never to invent, and identified every person by hand.

There are service details the archives knew that the family never did. I'd be glad to write it for your members, sourcing and all. A look first: [dashboard link]
