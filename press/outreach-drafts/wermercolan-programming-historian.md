**Subject:** Possible methods lesson: structuring a primary-source letter archive (honestly) with AI assistance

Alex — I've taught myself a fair amount from Programming Historian, so I wanted to ask whether the method behind a project of mine might fit as a lesson or case writeup, rather than pitch it as a story.

I recovered and structured 273 of my family's Civil War letters (1861–1870) into a queryable dataset. The reproducible part is the architecture: every letter is split into three layers — fact, editorial judgment, and computed analysis — with a three-axis confidence model on inferences, and identities resolved through curated alias tables rather than fuzzy matching (486 people, 353 places). AI is used strictly as a filter over real records, never as a source, so the historical layer stays clean.

It's essentially a worked answer to "I have a box of primary sources — how do I turn them into honest, queryable data?" Would a methods contribution along these lines be of interest? Happy to share the full writeup and the dataset.
