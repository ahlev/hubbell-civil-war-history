The Trust's whole argument is that the ground means something because of who fell on it — so I wanted to give you a name. Henry Hubbell, 34th New York, was killed at Antietam on September 17, 1862. The record first listed him *missing*; only later was he confirmed *killed*. His body was never recovered.

I know that because my family kept 273 letters that Henry, his three brothers, and their mother wrote home to Champlain, New York, between 1861 and 1870. One brother, my great-great-grandfather Alexander of the 60th New York, was wounded at Lookout Mountain — which I corroborated independently. I cross-checked everything against muster and pension records, using AI only as a disciplined check; it never invents a record.

If it's useful for *Hallowed Ground* or the Trust's storytelling, I'd gladly share a sourced profile and the letters. Dashboard: [dashboard link].
