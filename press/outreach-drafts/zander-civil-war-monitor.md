Dr. Zander — congratulations on the Wiley-Silver Prize. *The Army Under Fire* makes the case that the country's leaders undervalued the soldiers doing the actual fighting, which is why I thought you might find this corpus useful.

I'm the great-great-grandson and namesake of Alexander F. Hubbell, 60th New York, wounded at Lookout Mountain and through the Atlanta Campaign. He and his three brothers, from Champlain, New York, served across both theaters — Henry of the 34th New York was reported missing at Antietam, then confirmed killed; Charles and James were in the 153rd. The family left 273 letters, 1861 to 1870: a granular, datable record of exactly the ordinary volunteer soldiers your book argues were chronically misunderstood from above.

I transcribed and cross-referenced the corpus against pension and regimental records, using AI only as a check against documents. If a soldier's-eye source set is ever useful for your work or a Front Line piece, the archive is open to you.
