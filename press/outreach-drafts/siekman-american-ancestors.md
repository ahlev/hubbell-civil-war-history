Hi Meaghan,

Your work on compiling biographies of people left out of the written record is the closest thing I've found to what I've been doing — only mine started with people who were in the record, then went silent for eighty years.

The Hubbells were a Champlain, New York family. Four brothers enlisted in 1861; they and their mother Frances wrote home across four theaters of the war. 273 letters survived, were typed by a relative in 1947–49, digitized by my father, and this past year I transcribed, structured, and published them as an interactive archive. I'm the great-great-grandson and namesake of one of the brothers; the line settled in Iowa.

I'd write it method-first: the five-generation chain of custody, identity resolved by curated alias tables rather than fuzzy matching, and AI used only as a disciplined judge — a filter, never a source. And what the public record knew that the family never did.
