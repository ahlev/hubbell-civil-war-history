**Subject:** A records case study: 273 family letters, checked against the public archive

Hi Sunny,

You've spent years teaching people to find their families in the records they overlook, so I think you'll like what happened when I pointed that approach at my own family's letters.

The Hubbells of Champlain, NY left 273 letters — four soldier-brothers and their mother, Frances, 1861–1870. My great-grandmother typed them in the late 1940s; my father scanned them; this year I transcribed, structured, and published them, and cross-referenced the whole archive against public records, including Library of Congress newspapers. Service details and movements in the record corrected and filled what the family itself never knew. I leaned on AI to organize and check — but it was a filter, never a source, and I identified every person by hand.

I'd be glad to write it up as a records-and-method case study for Family Tree or NGS Magazine. A look first: [dashboard link]
