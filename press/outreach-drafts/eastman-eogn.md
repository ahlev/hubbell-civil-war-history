**Subject:** AI on a tight leash: how I recovered 273 family letters and checked them

Hi Dick,

For 24 years EOGN has been where genealogists go to see how new tools actually get used — responsibly — so here's a case I think your readers would want.

My family kept 273 Civil War letters: four Hubbell brothers and their mother, Frances, writing home to Champlain, NY, 1861–1870. My great-grandmother typed them in the late 1940s; my father digitized them; this year I transcribed, structured, and published the whole correspondence, and cross-referenced it against public archives, including Library of Congress newspapers. I used AI heavily — but as a filter, never a source. It organized and checked; it never invented; and every person was identified by hand, not fuzzy-matched. The public record ended up correcting and filling things the family never knew.
