**Subject:** Essay pitch: the future of the family archive

Dear Sam,

Aeon is unusually good at letting one vivid particular carry a serious idea, so here is mine — particular first.

My family kept 273 Civil War letters: four Hubbell brothers and their mother, Frances, of Champlain, NY, 1861–1870. This year I transcribed, structured, and published the whole correspondence and cross-referenced it against digitized historical newspapers, including the Library of Congress's Chronicling America. I used AI throughout, but strictly as a filter — never a source — and identified every person by hand.

The essay I'd like to write for Aeon is the idea behind that work: if any family can now structure and verify its own papers against the public record, what becomes of historical memory — and what do we owe the dead when their evidence is suddenly searchable? It's a piece about memory, proof, and discipline, grounded in a real archive. One link to see it: [dashboard link]
