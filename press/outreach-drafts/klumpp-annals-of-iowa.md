**Subject:** A New York Civil War family that became an Iowa family

Hi Andrew,

Congratulations on the new era at the *Annals*. I'm writing because my family's story ends where your journal lives — in Iowa.

The Hubbells were a Champlain, New York family. Four brothers enlisted in 1861; they and their mother Frances wrote home across four theaters of the war. 273 letters survived. My ancestor and namesake, Alexander F. Hubbell of the 60th New York, came home and settled in Fonda, Iowa — which is how a New York correspondence became an Iowa family's inheritance. The letters were typed by a relative in 1947–49, digitized by my father, and this past year I transcribed, structured, and published them as an interactive archive.

Given your work on 19th-century Midwest settlement, I'd be glad to write a scholarly note for the *Annals*: the corpus as a primary source for the New York-to-Iowa migration, with a candid methodology note — identity resolved by curated alias tables, AI used only as a disciplined judge, a filter never a source.
