**Subject:** The household economy behind four Union enlistments — 273 letters

Professor Thomson,

*Bonds of War* reframed how I read my own family's letters — so much of the correspondence is, quietly, about money. Four Hubbell brothers from Champlain, New York, served the Union; with their mother Frances they left 273 letters, 1861–1870, threaded with pay, bounty, and what a household spent to keep four sons in the field.

The harder discoveries came from cross-checking the letters against muster and pension records: Henry, of the 34th New York, was reported *missing* at Antietam and later confirmed *killed*, body never recovered; one captain, Davis J. Rich, commanded two of the brothers in two different regiments. I used AI strictly as a disciplined check over real documents — it never invents a record.

I'd welcome your read, for the Monitor or simply as a scholar of the war's finances. Archive: [dashboard link].
