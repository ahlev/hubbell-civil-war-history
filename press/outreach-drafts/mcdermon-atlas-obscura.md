**Subject:** A border-town soldier with no grave — and the archive that found him

Daniel,

Atlas Obscura is where the overlooked, place-rooted story belongs, so here's one from a town most people pass on the way to the Canadian border. Champlain, New York, sent four Hubbell brothers to the Civil War. One of them, Henry of the 34th New York, was killed at Antietam — first reported *missing*, then confirmed *killed*. His body was never recovered; he has no grave.

I know the details because my family kept 273 letters the brothers and their mother wrote home between 1861 and 1870. I recovered and verified them against muster and pension records using AI strictly as a checker — *filter, never a source* — so it never invents a record, only confirms one. That same process corroborated my great-great-grandfather's wound at Lookout Mountain.

There's a quietly haunting place-and-people story here. I'd love to share it. Archive: [dashboard link].
