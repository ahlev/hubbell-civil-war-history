**Subject:** A Union family that did write it down — 273 letters, checked against the newspapers

Hi Matthew,

Your JSTOR Daily piece on how the Union's victors failed to write their own history — leaving the vacuum the Lost Cause rushed to fill — stuck with me, because my family is a small counter-example.

The Hubbells of Champlain, NY left 273 letters: four brothers who enlisted in 1861, and their mother, Frances, writing across the war and after. This year I transcribed and structured the whole correspondence and cross-referenced it against digitized newspapers, including the Library of Congress's Chronicling America. The letters are a Union family writing itself down in real time; the newspapers date and corroborate them. I used AI throughout, but strictly as a filter — never a source — and identified everyone by hand.
