# Exemplar Pitches — the v2 Standard
*Three worked examples of the new bar. Each leads with a gift chosen for the person, opens inside their work at the level of ideas, and makes one format-matched ask. All facts verified against project data 2026-07-07. DRAFTS — user reviews, adjusts voice, and sends nothing until prerequisites (live URL, About page, methodology links) are met.*

## 1. John Banks — the Antietam triptych (email)

**To:** jbankstx@comcast.net
**Subject:** A Champlain NY mother read the casualty lists for a week — writing to a son already dead in the West Woods

John,

Your About page says descendants with a letter from the attic should give you a shout. This is that — I think it's the kind you mean when you say some stories should make a reader cry.

Henry Hubbell, Co. D, 34th New York, was killed in the West Woods on September 17, 1862. His body was never recovered. Two days later his brother Alexander — 60th New York, also on the field — wrote their mother: "he is missing... I feel very anxious about him, but hope for the best." On September 21, not knowing, she wrote to Henry: "I read the sad record day after day as it is published, with trembling." The family's letters trace the whole collapse, missing to hope to certainty, in their own hands.

I'm Alexander's great-great-grandson and namesake. Five generations of us kept 273 of the family's letters; I've transcribed and published all of them, free: [URL]

The story is yours first if you want it — happy to share images, the full sequence, and everything we verified against the service and pension records.

Alexander Hubbell Levitt
Creator, The Hubbell Civil War Letters · Athens, GA

*~175 words. Why it works: his own solicitation is the opening; the gift is a complete story in his exact genre; AI is never mentioned (verification is, in his records-first vocabulary); the ask is a true exclusive.*

## 2. Matt Callery — Addressing Gettysburg (contact form)

**Subject:** After your Culp's Hill episode with Charlie Fennell — an unpublished 60th NY letter written there, July 4, 1863

Matt,

Two weeks ago you and Charlie Fennell spent two hours on Culp's Hill. I have something written *on* that hill that I don't believe anyone outside my family has read in 160 years.

My great-great-grandfather Alexander Hubbell served in the 60th New York — Greene's Brigade. On July 4, 1863, "Near Gettysburg, Penna," he wrote his mother: the regiment's losses ("fifty-two — eleven killed and forty-some wounded"), his company's casualties by name — Milebus Ayres killed, Sergeant Brockway and Private John Tickle wounded — and this: "We are burying our and the rebel's dead today."

I've published all 273 of my family's war letters as a free archive: [URL]. There's a personal wrinkle your listeners might enjoy — my parents named me on that battlefield, after him.

Would you want to do an episode on it? First reading of the letter is yours — and if Charlie wanted to put the names against the muster rolls, I'd love nothing more.

Alexander Hubbell Levitt · Athens, GA

*~170 words. Why it works: pegged to an episode he aired two weeks ago; the named casualties are an invitation to do what guides love doing; the "named at Gettysburg" clause is one line, not a theme; the ask fits his format exactly and bundles Fennell.*

## The five craft rules these demonstrate
1. **The gift is chosen for the person** — triptych→Banks, Culp's Hill letter→Callery, dataset+discipline→Willison. None of these pitches could be sent to anyone else.
2. **Open inside their work** — an episode aired two weeks ago, a standing invitation on an About page, a January post — engaged at the level of what they do, not "I loved your piece."
3. **One vivid specific carries each pitch** — a quoted line, named soldiers, a corrected date.
4. **AI placement varies by audience** — absent (Banks), one implicit clause (Callery), the substance itself (Willison).
5. **The ask matches their format and costs them nothing** — an exclusive story, an episode with their recurring guest, a look at a dataset.
