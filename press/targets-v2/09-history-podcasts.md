# Broad History Podcasts, Broadcast & YouTube — Vetted Target List
**Vertical:** broad-audience history guest/feature formats (CW-specialist pods covered elsewhere) · **Researched:** 2026-07-07 · RESEARCH ONLY

## Ranked Finalists (6)

### 1. History Unplugged — Scott Rank, PhD (Parthenon Podcast Network)
1,100+ episodes since 2017, near-daily; top Apple History chart show. **Active:** July 2026 episodes verified (Jul 7, Jul 2, Jun 11 — [parthenonpodcast.com](https://www.parthenonpodcast.com/history-unplugged-podcast/)). Hour-long single-guest interviews; the highest-volume guest pipeline in the vertical; guests include independent/non-academic researchers.
- **Episode concept:** "Four brothers, 273 letters, one 160-year family error — what a descendant found when he cross-referenced his family's Civil War memory against the record." The methodology paper functions as the "book" that anchors a typical episode.
- **Booking (verified):** contact form at [scottrankphd.com/contact](https://www.scottrankphd.com/contact) ("To inquire about appearing on the show"). Secondary: Parthenon network contact.
- **Risk:** weeks-long queue; heavily publisher-pitched — one tight page. No pay-for-play observed; pitch direct, never via booking services.

### 2. The Road to Now — Bob Crawford (Avett Brothers) & Dr. Ben Sawyer
10 years, 372+ episodes; crossover audience. **Active:** Ep. #372 (Jun 22, 2026), #371, #370 ([theroadtonow.com](https://theroadtonow.com/)). Hosts "historians, politicians, journalists and artists" — and Crawford is himself a non-academic who did serious archival research: the closest host-persona match to our guest in the vertical.
- **Episode concept:** "A technology researcher inherits 273 family letters and rebuilds his family's Civil War — including correcting a death story they mis-remembered for 160 years — using AI as a filter, never a source."
- **Booking (verified):** contact email/form on theroadtonow.com.
- **Risk:** weekly single slot; often book-tied — lead with the story.

### 3. The History Guy — Lance Geiger (YouTube ~1.4M subs) — *story suggestion, not guest*
"History that deserves to be remembered" — 5–15 min scripted tellings of exactly this kind of story; M/W/F cadence; has a "Best of: American Civil War" compilation. **He explicitly solicits story suggestions.**
- **The pitch is the story, not the guest:** "The Hubbell brothers of Champlain, NY — Antietam's West Woods, Culp's Hill, Cedar Creek, and a homecoming death the family mis-remembered for 160 years," with the open archive as his source.
- **Channel (verified):** **Suggestions@thehistoryguy.net** ([thehistoryguy.com](https://thehistoryguy.com/)).
- **Risk:** no interview = no guest exposure; he may not feature the archive prominently; crowded inbox. Zero cost, perfect format match.

### 4. Vlogging Through History — Chris Mowery (YouTube ~514K subs + Parthenon podcast)
Reaction videos + historic-site tours driven by viewer-suggested material. **Key unlock: Mowery is a professional genealogist AND a Gettysburg Licensed Battlefield Guide** ([gettysburghistory.org/chrismowery](https://gettysburghistory.org/chrismowery/)) — genealogy-meets-Civil-War is his exact intersection, and Culp's Hill is his home turf.
- **Ask:** suggest he explore the interactive archive on camera ("a genealogist watches a 160-year family error get corrected"), or a Gettysburg-angle collab on the Culp's Hill brother.
- **Contact:** business email reported as business@chrismowrey.com (note spelling) — **UNCONFIRMED aggregator sourcing; verify on his YouTube About page before sending.** Fallbacks: linktr.ee/VTHistory, Parthenon contact.
- **Risk:** contact unverified; reaction format = possibly brief feature.

### 5. American History Hit — Don Wildman (History Hit network)
Twice-weekly, 398 episodes, professional production. **Active:** Ep. 398 (Jul 6, 2026); Ep. 394 "Victory in the Civil War" (Jun 22, 2026). Mostly professor-guests, but Ep. 397 (Jul 2, 2026) featured a non-academic shipwright — strong stories get non-credentialed guests booked. Wildman (ex-"Mysteries at the Museum") loves object/archive-driven stories.
- **Episode concept:** "The letters that corrected a family's Civil War memory: inside a 273-letter home archive from Antietam to Appomattox."
- **Booking (verified):** **podcasts@historyhit.com** ([historyhit.com/contact](https://www.historyhit.com/contact/)).
- **Risk:** UK network gatekeeping, academic/book-cycle skew — slower, lower hit rate.

### 6. C-SPAN American History TV — (conditional, two-step long game)
AHTV records *events* — talks at historical societies, roundtables, museums. Path: (1) land a public talk at a venue they cover (roundtables/societies — already in motion via the education vertical), then (2) suggest coverage: **americanhistorytv@c-span.org** ([c-span.org/ahtv](https://www.c-span.org/ahtv/)).
- **Risk:** lowest probability, longest timeline; near-zero cost once a talk exists. Sequence after a speaking slot is booked.

## Cross-cutting notes
- **Pay-for-play warning:** podcast-booking agencies monetize these exact shows; every finalist has a free, direct, verified door — paid booking would also undercut the "disciplined AI historian" credibility.
- **Diminishing returns:** beyond these six, broad-history is dominated by narrative/no-guest formats — the marginal 7th target is weaker than a second attempt at the top four. **Parthenon network is the highest-leverage single relationship (finalists #1 + #4).**
- **Register:** independents (#1–4) want the family saga with the correction hook; network/broadcast (#5–6) respond to methodology-plus-archive.
