## FINALISTS (ranked)

### 1. John Banks — John Banks' Civil War Blog + "The Antietam and Beyond Podcast" (w/ Tom McMillan) + Civil War Monitor digital columnist
The single best person-to-asset match in this vertical. De facto national storyteller of Antietam (*Connecticut Yankees at Antietam*; NYT, Civil War Times, Civil War Monitor); career journalist (Dallas Morning News, ESPN); has already written about a **60th New York private at Culp's Hill** — covering two of the brothers' exact grounds. Listed digital columnist at Civil War Monitor ([about-us](https://www.civilwarmonitor.com/about-us/)) — one relationship cascades into finalist #3.
- **Bridge:** the **Antietam triptych** (Henry, 34th NY, West Woods — the 34th's West Woods stand is core Banks territory). Backup: ["Desperation on Culp's Hill: A 60th New York private's story"](http://john-banks.blogspot.com/2016/07/desperation-on-culps-hill-60th-new-york.html) (2016) — the July 4, 1863 field letter is a natural sequel to his own piece.
- **Recent:** Clemens podcast post (Jun 1, 2026); "Tales from the road: The dead of Falling Waters" (Apr 17, 2026): *"The loss of such generous and determined spirits are not family but national losses."* Podcast hub: http://john-banks.blogspot.com/2025/02/the-antietam-and-beyond-podcast-hub.html
- **Contact (verified):** jbankstx@comcast.net; Facebook @johnbankscivilwar (42k).
- **Ask:** *Antietam and Beyond* episode on Henry's letters + the missing→killed sequence; secondary, blog feature. AI methodology only as provenance hygiene.
- **Risk:** may prefer to take the story to the Monitor — that's a feature, not a bug. Note his 2026 tour crossover with the Battle of Gettysburg Podcast ("Gettysburg Connections at Antietam") — one Banks relationship touches three platforms.

### 2. Emerging Civil War (Chris Mackowski, ed-in-chief) — guest-post channel
Highest-traffic enthusiast blog with a formal door for outside researchers; publishing daily 2026. Documented appetite: ["Book Review: Witness to War"](https://emergingcivilwar.com/2026/06/25/book-review-witness-to-war-the-story-of-the-civil-war-told-by-those-who-lived-it/) (Jun 25, 2026 — 432 unpublished letters).
- **Contributors on this beat:** **Sarah Kay Bierle** (family letters/diaries method; reviewed *The Civil War Letters of Sarah Kennedy*; now also ABT Education Dept — a side-door to ABT) and **Jon-Erik Gilot** (20-year professional archivist; writes on digitization/primary sources; the one ECW voice who can evaluate the methodology on its merits).
- **Bridge:** the **James death-date correction** as a research-detective guest post — "newly discovered information on significant events," exactly what their guidelines request.
- **Channel (verified):** **emergingcivilwar@gmail.com**, subject "Guest Post Submission," 800–1,500 words, Chicago footnotes, ≥1 image, double-blind review, 2–4 weeks ([guidelines](https://emergingcivilwar.com/join-us/submission-guidelines/)).
- **Risk:** **explicit no-generative-AI policy** — the post must be verifiably human-written; a methodology framing may qualify under the "specifically analyzing AI" carve-out, but the safe play is a human-written discovery narrative footnoting the methodology paper.

### 3. The Civil War Monitor (Terry A. Johnston Jr., publisher & editor-in-chief)
Premier surviving Civil War magazine (quarterly; [Spring 2026 issue](https://www.civilwarmonitor.com/issue/spring-2026/) live). **Spring 2026 cover story is one Vermont soldier's service told through his record** — structurally the Hubbell story. Web "Front Line" runs soldier-correspondence features (Peter Carmichael's ["Letters Home"](https://www.civilwarmonitor.com/letters-home-correspondence-from-men-at-war/)) with a standing "Firsthand Accounts" category.
- **Bridge:** the **asymmetry-of-register finding** (24% vs 2%, 17 matched pairs) — Carmichael's "Letters Home" is literally about the gap between what soldiers felt and what they wrote home; the Hubbell corpus supplies the first *quantified* single-family version. The Gettysburg July 4 letter anchors it.
- **Contact:** [contact form](https://www.civilwarmonitor.com/contact-us/) with "Editorial" option is the citable channel. terry@civilwarmonitor.com is *inferred from his author-page slug — NOT verified*. Warm path: John Banks is one of their six digital columnists.
- **Ask:** feature or Front Line web piece on the asymmetry finding.
- **Risk:** small shop, quarterly, no published freelance guidelines — cold pitch must be unusually complete; advisory board (Carmichael, Guelzo, Giesberg) means data claims get real scrutiny; methodology paper is the armor.

### 4. Kevin M. Levin — Civil War Memory (Substack)
Largest individual voice in Civil War public history; posting near-daily July 2026 ("The Enslaved Men of Gettysburg: A Recap," Jul 5, 2026). Newsletter promise: *"close readings of primary sources, deep dives into historiography."*
- **AI stance, assessed:** his AI posts target **AI-generated fake history** (fabricated images, Lost Cause chatbots) — media-literacy skepticism, not tool-rejection: *"another reminder of how easily we can be led astray when searching for online information."* **Verdict: great fit, not doomed — IF the pitch leads with the correction story and human verification and offers the methodology up for scrutiny.** The Hubbell project is the counter-example his AI coverage lacks.
- **Bridge:** the **James death-date correction** — a family's transmitted memory of its own Civil War dead being wrong for 160 years, fixed by documentary cross-reference, *is Civil War memory*. Secondary: the asymmetry finding (brothers curating what their mother could know).
- **Contact:** no public email; Substack DM/comments ([kevinmlevin.substack.com](https://kevinmlevin.substack.com/)), cwmemory.com contact page; responsive on Substack Notes/Bluesky.
- **Ask:** not "coverage" — offer the archive as a **teaching resource his educator-readers would use**, and/or invite him to interrogate the methodology. Treat him as a critic, not a megaphone. Explicit "tell us what we got wrong."
- **Risk:** medium-high — if it smells like AI-project promotion he may ignore it or use it as a foil.

### 5. Civil War Talk Radio (Gerald Prokopowicz, East Carolina University)
Longest-running interview institution in the field — weekly since 2004, ~700 hours; **Season 22 airing 2026 episodes** (Jan 21, 2026: Edwin Rutan; Jan 14, 2026: Matthew Pinsker). Interviews "historians, authors, enthusiasts" — not just book authors (has featured curators, editors, digital projects).
- **Bridge:** the **whole project as a body of work** — the only format that fits all of it; Prokopowicz (Ph.D. Harvard) will engage the verification method seriously — exactly the credentialing the positioning needs.
- **Contact (verified):** [Contact Us page](https://www.impedimentsofwar.org/contact-us.html) on impedimentsofwar.org.
- **Ask:** guest episode. Frame "descendant-built digital archive + published methodology," not "AI project."
- **Risk:** guest queue skews university-press authors; one-man production, slow replies.

### 6. Untold Civil War (Paul Hoza, independent podcast + YouTube)
Mission-statement fit: surfacing *"stories of the Civil War that remain forgotten."* Active (Jun 2, 2026: 1913 Gettysburg reunion episode). **Direct precedent:** "In Their Own Words: Civil War Letters" (Sep 21, 2025, w/ Jeff McArdle); unit-history episodes. Non-famous researchers are his normal guest.
- **Bridge:** **four brothers, one mother, one mail circuit** — the ensemble story, with the James correction as emotional payoff. Most pitch-receptive finalist; the proving ground before bigger asks.
- **Contact (verified):** **untoldcivilwar@gmail.com**; IG/FB @untoldcivilwar ([site](https://untoldcivilwar.squarespace.com/)).
- **Risk:** smaller audience; hobbyist-paced.

### 7. Muster — blog of the Journal of the Civil War Era (Hilary Green, Digital Media Editor)
The scholarly-legitimacy play; actively soliciting 2026 ([Jan 2026 call](https://www.journalofthecivilwarera.org/2026/01/muster-call-for-graduate-student-submissions/)). [Write for Us](https://www.journalofthecivilwarera.org/muster/write-for-us/) invites public historians: 1,100–1,300 words incl. *"short articles exploring intersections between the Civil War era and our present world."*
- **Bridge:** the **methodology** + **asymmetry finding** as a research note — the one venue here where the 17-pair empirical design is the headline.
- **Contact (verified):** **higreen@davidson.edu**, subject "Muster Submission" — re-verify the named editor at send time.
- **Risk:** academic venue; unaffiliated author judged on rigor; slow; unpaid.
