## A20. The Pudding — pitch (LAG story — date-computed, scrutiny-proof)
**Via:** pudding.cool/pitch (form) · **Working title:** "The Speed of Not Knowing"

On September 21, 1862, Frances Hubbell of Champlain, New York wrote to her son Henry, a private in the 34th New York — anxious, reading the newspapers' casualty lists "day after day... with trembling." Henry had been dead for four days, killed at Antietam. She wouldn't know for weeks; the family wouldn't be *certain* for months. Because all 273 of her family's surviving Civil War letters are transcribed, dated, and structured (my project — [URL]), we can compute the thing that letter never says: the gap between when things happened and when the people writing could know them. Four brothers, four theaters, four years of mail — every letter placed against the events it couldn't yet mention. The essay: one family's war at the speed of information, told through a timeline of what their mother knew and when, battle by battle, ending with the certainty that took the longest to arrive. Every date is documentary. I built the archive's nine visualizations myself and would build this end-to-end. — Alexander Hubbell Levitt, [EMAIL]
