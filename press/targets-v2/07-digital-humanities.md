# Digital Humanities, Public History & Archives — Vetted Target List
**Project:** The Hubbell Civil War Letters · **Date:** 2026-07-07 · RESEARCH ONLY — no contact made

**Vertical verdict:** the project's highest-legitimacy vertical, but it runs on *submission processes*, not pitches. Three fastest wins: a formal project review at **Reviews in Digital Humanities**, a **Project Showcase at NCPH History@Work** (direct precedent: a family-letters digital archive has been showcased before), and an **H-CivWar announcement**. The peer-reviewed play for the asymmetry-of-register finding is **Current Research in Digital History**.

## FINALISTS (ranked)

### 1. Reviews in Digital Humanities — Jennifer Guiliano & Roopika Risam, co-editors
The only venue whose entire purpose is formally peer-reviewing digital projects *as scholarship*. A published review = a citable, permanent legitimacy asset for every subsequent pitch in every other vertical.
- **Active:** Vol. 7 No. 5 ([May 2026 editors' note](https://reviewsindh.pubpub.org/pub/editors-note-may-2026/release/3)), Vol. 7 No. 4 (April 2026).
- **Appetite:** [About](https://reviewsindh.pubpub.org/about): accepts *"digital archives, multimedia or multimodal scholarship, digital exhibits, visualizations, digital games, and digital tools"* — Hubbell is three of these at once. Recent issues review digital public history and archives worldwide.
- **Process:** formal submission ([Submissions](https://reviewsindh.pubpub.org/submissions)); on acceptance the project director writes a 500-word overview published alongside the single-blind review.
- **Contact:** dhreviews@mit.edu (per [CELJ page](https://www.celj.org/featured-journals/reviewsdh); older sources list a gmail — **confirm current address on the submissions page before sending**).
- **Risk:** unknown queue; journal *particularly encourages* critical ethnic/postcolonial work — in scope but not their priority lane; reviewers will actually test identity-resolution and provenance claims — methodology paper must be linkable and airtight first.

### 2. NCPH History@Work — Project Showcase + Digital Projects Directory
Fast, low-barrier, practitioner audience, **direct precedent**: ["Project Showcase: Seward Family Digital Archive"](https://ncph.org/history-at-work/project-showcase-seward-family-digital-archive-community-project-achievement/) — a family-correspondence digital archive, the closest published comp found anywhere.
- **Active + timely:** NCPH is running a **2026 virtual program series on AI in public history** (ACE Mellon program): *"how artificial intelligence is reshaping public history work"* ([2026 Virtual Programs](https://ncph.org/conference/virtual-programs/2026-virtual-programs/)). The AI moment is institutionally live for them right now.
- **Process (verified):** [Contribute guidelines](https://ncph.org/history-at-work/guidelines/) — pitch = 1–3 paragraphs to **historyatwork@ncph.org** (~2-week response); Project Showcase = 300–500 words. Plus the [Digital Public History Directory](https://ncph.org/digital-public-history-directory/) — free listing, immediate.
- **Bridge:** frame as public-history practice — "an independent researcher used LLM cross-referencing to correct his own family's lore, and here's the replicable discipline." Cite the Seward showcase and AHA AI principles in the pitch.
- **Risk:** unpaid, practitioner reach — credibility infrastructure, not traffic.

### 3. H-Net — H-CivWar (announcement + DH resource page + interview series)
- **Appetite:** H-CivWar maintains a [Digital Humanities resource page](https://networks.h-net.org/node/4113/pages/6229991/digital-humanities) listing CW DH projects (e.g., *Private Voices*, [announced on H-CivWar](https://networks.h-net.org/node/4113/discussions/590230/new-online-letters-database-offers-insights-civil-wars-common)), and runs **"The Civil War Era & Digital Humanities Interview Series"** — video interviews with project directors; [Ep. 7](https://networks.h-net.org/node/4113/discussions/7631753/civil-war-era-digital-humanities-interview-series-ep-7) covered *"the Civil War Letters of Henry A. Allen Podcast"*. A four-brother 273-letter interactive archive is squarely their format.
- **Process:** free account → announcement (independent researchers accepted; moderated; [User's Guide](https://networks.h-net.org/group/905/pages/20000060/posting-announcements-users-guide)); separately request addition to the DH resource page + offer an interview episode.
- **Contact:** Ep. 7 lists resource editor Chase McCarter (chmccarter@unm.edu) — **likely stale** (he's moved to Mississippi State per [CWGK team page](https://discovery.civilwargovernors.org/page/team)); verify current editor on the H-CivWar staff page before writing.
- **Risk:** must read as scholarly resource launch, not publicity.

### 4. Current Research in Digital History (RRCHNM/George Mason) — Stephen Robertson & Lincoln Mullen, eds.
The right peer-reviewed home for the **asymmetry-of-register finding** — publishes short, argument-driven, data-backed digital-history *findings*.
- **Active:** Vol. 9 (2026); most recent article May 12, 2026 ([crdh.rrchnm.org](https://crdh.rrchnm.org/)). Rolling submissions.
- **Fit (verified):** [Submissions](https://crdh.rrchnm.org/submissions/): articles showing *"how digital methods have advanced specific interpretations of history"*, ≤3,000 words; *"welcome early findings"*; encourage submitting *"any associated data or code."* No affiliation restriction.
- **Contact:** online form; editors srober30@gmu.edu, lmullen@gmu.edu — short pre-submission scope query is normal practice.
- **Risk:** real peer review (months); the 17-pair sample will draw scrutiny (define matching + "extreme emotion" coding rigorously — the three-layer architecture helps defend the editorial-layer tags). **This consumes the finding's first-publication card — decide deliberately between CRDH and larger venues before submitting.**

### 5. AHA *Perspectives on History*
The discipline's trade magazine, actively covering AI-in-history right now.
- **Appetite (read):** ["A Disciplinary Approach to Generative AI in the History Classroom"](https://www.historians.org/perspectives-article/a-disciplinary-approach-to-generative-ai-in-the-history-classroom/) (Sept 2025); AHA ["Guiding Principles for AI in History Education"](https://www.historians.org/resource/guiding-principles-for-artificial-intelligence-in-history-education/) (July 2025) — cite these in the pitch. (The AHR's parallel [AI CFP](https://www.historians.org/news-publications/american-historical-review/how-to-submit/ai-in-historical-perspectives/) wants AI historicized as phenomenon — wrong genre for us.)
- **Bridge:** practice essay — "What 'AI is a filter, never a source' looks like in a real archive," with the James triangulation as narrative spine — an independent researcher's working answer to the AHA's own principles.
- **Process:** [Write for Us](https://www.historians.org/perspectives/submit); Perspectives Daily 800–1,000 words; journalistic tone, hyperlinks not footnotes; $100 honorarium (print).
- **Risk:** membership culture — nonmembers can write but editors favor insiders; don't double-submit anything intended for CRDH.

### 6. *Journal of American History* — Digital History Reviews (Jeffrey W. McClurken, contributing editor)
Most prestigious placement possible — a 500-word scholarly review in the flagship US history journal — with an explicit suggestion channel.
- **Process (verified):** [Digital History Reviews](https://www.oah.org/publications/jah/submit/digital-history-reviews/) — McClurken *"solicits, edits, and submits 5-6 500-word reviews every 3 months"* and *"welcomes suggestions"* at **jmcclurk@umw.edu**. His [review criteria](http://techist.mcclurken.org/digital-history/revised-digital-history-review-guidelines/) evaluate content, design, audience, AND digital methodology — the confidence model maps directly onto his rubric.
- **Ask:** one short email suggesting the project, linking site + methodology paper. Reviews are commissioned to third parties.
- **Risk:** quarterly, 5–6 slots, most reviewed projects institutionally hosted. Zero cost to try.

### 7. *The American Archivist* Reviews Portal (SAA) — reviewseditor@archivists.org
The archives profession's formal review channel — *"media and digital projects that engage with archives and archivists."*
- **Active:** 11 reviews 2025, 2 in 2026; ["Digital Archives and Projects" category](https://reviews.americanarchivist.org/category/resources/digital-archives-and-projects/) has 30 reviews.
- **Timely:** SAA's [Generative AI statement](https://www2.archivists.org/american-archivist/AI-statement) requires AI disclosure and prohibits purely-generated content — the Hubbell methodology is a working demonstration of the line SAA just drew.
- **Contact (verified):** reviewseditor@archivists.org (Rose Buchanan & Stephanie Luke, [contact page](https://reviews.americanarchivist.org/contact/)).
- **Risk:** archivists will probe long-term preservation of a Vercel-hosted archive — **have an answer ready (data exports + GitHub + Internet Archive deposit)**.

### 8. FromThePage blog — Ben & Sara Brumfield (relationship play, not a placement)
The community hub for AI + historical transcription practice; the most methodologically simpatico people in the vertical.
- **Active:** ["AI With Historical Instincts?"](https://content.fromthepage.com/ai-with-historical-instincts/) (Apr 1, 2026); ["Can Multi-Modal LLMs Transcribe Historic Documents?"](https://content.fromthepage.com/can-multi-modal-llms-transcribe-historic-documents/) — they are running and publishing the same experiments the methodology paper formalizes.
- **Approach:** not a pitch — a peer exchange referencing their April 2026 post. Contact: support@fromthepage.com (published channel).
- **Realistic outcomes:** blog mention, webinar/conference orbit invitation, or citation in their next benchmark post. Zero downside; upgrades every archivist-facing pitch elsewhere.
