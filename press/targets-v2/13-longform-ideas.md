# General-Interest / Longform / Ideas Publications — Vetted Target List
**Researched:** 2026-07-07 · RESEARCH ONLY — no one contacted.

**Headline finding:** two "obvious" targets are dead for this window — **Atlas Obscura has paused all freelance pitching indefinitely**, and **JSTOR Daily is closed until January 2027**. The surviving field is strong: 7 viable venues; **Public Domain Review and Narratively are the standouts** (both open NOW, each mapping to a different flagship asset).

## FINALISTS (ranked)

### 1. The Public Domain Review — bylined essay (+ possible Collections feature)
OPEN, publishing new essays through Jun 17, 2026; every two weeks; pays **$500 flat**. They accept pitches but *slightly prefer full drafts* — worth exploiting.
- **Bridge:** the letters as public-domain artifacts. Best pitch object: **the mother's Sept 21, 1862 letter to a son four days dead**, expanded into the missing→hope→certainty arc — an essay about how information moved (and failed to move) through a Civil War family, dovetailing with the "what they didn't know and when" viz.
- **Comparable (read):** "Lofty Only in Sound: Crossed Wires and Community in 19th-Century Dreams" (Puglionesi) — CW-era families processing separation/death through letters and dreams: *"People felt linked with imperilled loved ones through moments of clairvoyance, premonition, or dream communion."* Almost a sibling essay.
- **Ask:** ~2,500-word full draft to **submissions@publicdomainreview.org** ([guidelines](https://publicdomainreview.org/about/submissions/)); ~4-week response if interested. Then propose the letters as a Collections entry.
- **Risk:** slush volume; CC BY-SA on essays (aligns fine); the essay must stand on the 1860s material — archive linked as source, AI angle minimal.

### 2. Narratively — first-person memoir feature ("Hidden History")
OPEN year-round via Submittable; Memoir Prize each Oct–Dec ($3,000). Founder **Brendan Spiegel** actively publishes what-lands guidance; they sell a class literally called "Writing With Your Ancestors."
- **Bridge:** the memoir only this descendant can write — *"My family remembered his death date wrong for 160 years — until I cross-referenced the letters five generations saved."* James's correction is the spine; the namesake relationship is the close. Their memoir guideline — *"everything included should be 100% accurate"* — makes the project's quote-audit discipline a selling point.
- **Ask:** 2,500–4,000-word draft/detailed pitch via [Submittable](https://narratively.submittable.com/submit); hold a version for the Memoir Prize window (~Oct 2026).
- **Risk:** big slush pile; modest pay; AI framing = supporting detail, not hook.

### 3. Psyche — Ideas or "Turning Points" essay
Pitch portal opens **the first week of each month** (confirmed by Aeon's editor, Feb 2026). Pays; Ideas ~1,200–2,000 words.
- **Bridge:** the **asymmetry finding is a ready-made Psyche Ideas essay** — "What Civil War soldiers spared their mothers — and what it says about how families ration grief." Comparables: "Why you should talk to your dead ancestors"; "How I learned to hear the voice of my ancestors" (Turning Points).
- **Ask:** pitch via https://psyche.co/pitch during the first week of a month. ~3 months pitch→publication.
- **Risk:** narrow window; lead with "the descendant who measured his own family's emotional record," not AI. **⚠ Uses the asymmetry finding — coordinate with the first-publication decision (Pudding/Monitor/CRDH); a Psyche essay is angle-compatible with a data essay but don't promise the same "first" twice.**

### 4. Smithsonian magazine — reported history story
OPEN via online pitch form (250 words; requires 3 prior clips — a real gate).
- **Appetite proof:** "Newly Discovered Letters Bring New Insight Into the Life of a Civil War Soldier" ("wildly popular" per their own follow-up); Sarah Kuta's Jan 2025 National Archives cursive/AI story — *"these methods don't always work, and they aren't always accurate"* — exactly the beat the methodology answers. **Kuta is a byline worth knowing.**
- **Ask:** 250-word pitch via the [form](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/contact/smithsonian-magazine-online-article-submissions/); realistic paths: partner with a working freelancer, or accept slush odds.
- **Risk:** clips requirement; no named editor; variable pay.

### 5. Why Is This Interesting? (WITI) — guest edition
ACTIVE daily; built on rotating guest writers (Noah Brier & Colin Nagy; ed. Louis Cheslaw). No formal intake — guest editions happen by emailing the principals (Brier publicly reachable via noahbrier.com/LinkedIn).
- **Bridge:** "The Hubbell Letters Edition" — the information-lag viz + the asymmetry stat are tailor-made; Brier's professional obsession is AI-and-knowledge-work, making "filter, never a source" catnip. Comparables: The Archivist Edition (2020), The Old Map Mystery Edition (2024).
- **Ask:** offer to *write* a guest edition (~600–900 words, unpaid).
- **Risk:** relationship-dependent; smaller reach; near-zero effort to attempt.

### 6. The Browser — curated-link amplifier (not a pitch target)
11k+ paying subscribers; editors read publications **by RSS only** ([FAQ](https://thebrowser.com/frequently-asked-questions/)). **Action:** submit the project's blog/essay RSS feed at thebrowser.com/submissions; nominate published essays. Force-multiplier on placements, not a primary target.

### 7. Zócalo Public Square — ideas essay (lower confidence)
OPEN ("writers of all levels"); pitch support@zocalopublicsquare.org. 2026 Book Prize theme is literally "community and connection." Comparable: "Finding My Family's History in the Tulsa Race Massacre's Destruction" (2021, descendant-as-researcher).
- **Risk:** generic intake; comparables are not recent; modest audience. Background bet.
