# FAQ — anticipated journalist questions

**How accurate are the AI transcriptions?**
Every letter was transcribed from Gladys Hubbell's 1947–49 typescripts (and scans of surviving originals) using AI vision, then reviewed by a human. The working rule, documented in the project's methodology paper, is that the AI is a filter, never a source: it proposes, people verify. Identity matching uses curated alias tables — never fuzzy name-matching — and factual claims about the soldiers were cross-checked against muster rolls, pension indexes, newspapers, and cemetery records. The project maintains a public discrepancy registry of everything that didn't line up, including errors it found in the family's own history.

**What did the AI actually do, and what did humans do?**
AI did the labor: reading handwriting and typescript, structuring 273 letters into a consistent schema, surfacing candidate matches in public records. Humans made every judgment: confirming transcriptions, resolving identities, adjudicating discrepancies, writing the editorial layer. The data architecture keeps these separate — source facts, editorial judgment, and computed analysis live in distinct layers, so a reader can always tell which is which.

**Are you a professional historian?**
No — an amateur historian and a technology researcher, and the great-great-grandson of the writers. The methodology is published precisely so professionals can check the work, and corrections are welcomed.
