## Full Transcription

> August 25, 1863
> Ellis Ford
>
> Dear Sister Frances:
>
> I presume you would like to pull my hair right lustily — (rather a difficult matter, by the way, as it is not over an inch or two in length) for not writing you before. I plead guilty and promise all sorts of good things if you will let me off this time. Let me know immediately as I am afraid that my cranium (through fear of the pulling) will soon be in as critical a position as that of a certain ancient philosopher whose head a certain ill omened bird mistaking for a stone dropped an enormous turtle on it. How the old fellow must have hopped, eh! Well, now, Miss Fannie, if you do not wish me to hop as lustily as he did, write immediately. (That's a good way to get a letter!)
>
> All is quiet today along the lines of the Rappahannock, but we expect stirring times before many weeks. Shouldn't wonder if Lee paid us a visit. But a truce to war.
>
> How does Mr. Simonds flourish and that lame wrist of his? Hope it is not so bad but what he can use the rod to castigate the "young ideas" of Champlain. If this should be the case, please recommend for me a very popular mode of punishment we have done here, i.e., hanging a man up by his thumbs and allowing only his toes to touch the ground.
>
> How do Cook and the other returned soldiers flourish? Hope they have good times. My regards please give to all that you know. I believe that I never told you that during the march in Maryland, I passed over a part of the battle ground of Antietam, where we were at one time during the day and about half a mile from where we and the 34th fought. Oh! how I longed to leave the ranks and hunt for some trace of Henry, but it was impossible. Not far from there we halted and loaded and formed our line of battle. Skirmishers were thrown out and we marched a short distance when we halted and commenced throwing up intrenchments, etc., etc.
>
> I believe I also forgot to mention that Sergt. Howes of my Co. has been reduced to the ranks for drunkenness and that Amos Luther has been promoted to his place. This occurred some time since. It slipped my mind when I wrote before. Tell Mother that I sent her fifteen dollars by express a few days since. Wish I could have sent more. Tell her to use it to pay taxes or whatever else she pleases. Love to all.
>
> Your aff. Brother,
>
> Alexander F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

"Oh! how I longed to leave the ranks and hunt for some trace of Henry, but it was impossible." Alexander marched within half a mile of where his brother was killed at Antietam — eleven months earlier — and could not step out of formation to look. The army's discipline held him in line while his brother's grave lay just beyond reach. He mentions it almost casually, buried between jokes about hair-pulling and a passage about drunkenness.

The letter's tonal whiplash — from classical jokes about philosophers and turtles to the Antietam passage — is characteristic of Alexander's writing. He is the most literary of the Hubbell brothers, and the most capable of hiding deep feeling inside playful prose.
