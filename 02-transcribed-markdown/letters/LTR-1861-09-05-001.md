## Full Transcription

> Sept. 5th, 1861
> Washington, D.C.
>
> Dear Sister —
>
> I received your letter this morning. Very glad to know that you are all well. As for myself, I have not been well, have not been able to drill for three days, but am some better now. The Dr. gave me medicine. I think it is very unhealthy sleeping on the ground. I have got so now I don't mind it much. This is a great place for fever and ague. I have got to be careful xx or I shall come down with it.
>
> I can hardly realize that it is now fall. The summer has passed quickly away to me. It is nearly three months since I left home. It is now raining — shall probably have a wet day of it. We have a very fine tent to keepout the rain. I suppose you would like to know bout matters and things in our Camp. I shall write a Journal as soon as pay day comes around when I can get paper. When we get paid off again we shall probably have $24 or $26. I have now a five dollar gold piece. I shall send it all home except one or two dollars, by the Capt. as he expects to go to Champlain this month. Now I shall expect, of course, you and Mother will use what money you want. You must not hesitate, I put that down as a command. Times are hard and you will need it bad enough.
>
> Our Regt. is stationed here as guards to keep the rebels from crossing the river. The pickets go out by Co. Ours has been out once. There are ten thousand of our troops three miles from here so that if we are attacked we can fall back and have a strong force. I have heard the opinion expressed that our Regt. may never see a battle, that we will always be stationed here as guards, but we can not tell. I for one am willing to go. I don't care where.
>
> A sad accident happened here last Sabbath morning. A private from Co. H. was shot while on guard by another person. There were playing with their guns when one of them went off. He lived about fifteen minutes after he was shot. This makes three that have lost their lives by carelessness in this Regt.
>
> I have received all the papers that have been sent from home. A short time ago I got two papers from Charles — New York Times. It is some time since I received any Observers. I don't doubt but what they all come, as the direction is correct. I have heard that we will have a band to our Regt. I hope it will be so, heretofore we had fife and drum. We have a very good drummer.
>
> I hope you and Mother will take rides with our horse. He is very gentle if only managed right. I don't suppose he could run away if he should try. I hope the boys will get a chance to go to Middleburg. It is better than soldiering. Please write often.
>
> Your aff Brother
>
> Henry
>
> Please let me know if my old clothes have been received. I sent them in a box with others that live in Champlain. I had mine directed to Mother.

## Editorial Notes

Henry's health issues (fever and ague risk, unable to drill) foreshadow the illness that plagued Civil War camps. His insistence that the family use his pay — "I put that down as a command" — reveals the financial pressure on the home front. The accidental shooting death (third in the regiment) underscores the danger of untrained soldiers handling weapons in camp. Henry's statement "I for one am willing to go. I don't care where" shows eagerness for action after months of guard duty.
