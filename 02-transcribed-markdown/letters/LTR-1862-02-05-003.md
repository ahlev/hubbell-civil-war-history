# Letter: Henry Hubbell to Mother — February 5, 1862 (Camp Gorman, Bolivar Heights)

> Confidence notation: `basis · legibility · source-certainty`. See [CONFIDENCE-GUIDE.md](../CONFIDENCE-GUIDE.md).

> **⚠ CRITICAL DATING DISCREPANCY:** This letter is dated "Feb. 5th, 1862" and locates Henry at "Camp Gorman / Bolivar Heights." However, contemporaneous letters show Henry at **Camp McClellan on Feb. 11–13, 1862.** A soldier cannot be at Bolivar Heights on Feb. 5 and back at Camp McClellan 6 days later unless movement was extremely rapid. The Feb. 5 Bolivar Heights letters (this letter and LTR-1862-02-05-001, LTR-1862-02-05-002) likely have **incorrect dates** — possibly **Feb. 25 or Mar. 5, 1862** instead of Feb. 5. Recommend flagging and rechecking source documents against regimental movement records. Date retained as filed for traceability; **status: requires field verification**.

## Full Transcription

> Feb. 5th, 1862
> Camp Gorman
> Bolivar Heights
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I received your letter last evening and was very glad to hear from you. Nothing gives me greater pleasure than to receive a letter from home, although I wrote a short time ago I know you will be anxious to hear from me often. You must excuse this writing as I have no bench or table to write on, have to write the best way I can.
>
> I received the Observer last night. Very glad to get that any time it is convenient to send it. Our Regt. are now in large bell tents. I do not know how long we shall remain here, probably not a great while.
>
> You wished to know if I had written about Lieut. Scott having been put under arrest. I have not for I did not think it worth while. In regard to him having communicated with the rebels, it is not so. Some one must have written that that did not like him very well. He was put under arrest for being asleep when he was officer of the guard. There are some in the Co. that do not like him. I like him very well myself. Each of our Co. have had a present of Testament and Psalms. I prize it very much.
>
> It is very mild today. I am very glad if you like the picture that I have had taken, the one taken standing. I hope you will put it away off upstairs. I have nothing more to write this time. Direct your letters the same as before except the Camp.
>
> I remain your aff. Son,
>
> Henry
>
> P.S. I left home the first of June, I think. Swore in to the United States Service on the 15th of June. Have been in the service a little over nine months.

## Editorial Notes

**⚠ DATING DISCREPANCY — RESOLVED**

This letter and two others dated Feb. 5 at Camp Gorman, Bolivar Heights have **incorrect dates**. **RESOLUTION (from LTR-1862-02-28-001):** Henry's Feb 28 letter confirms "Our Regt. Left Camp McClellan on the 26th." LTR-1862-02-24-001 shows Henry at Camp McClellan on Feb 24 with march orders. The departure was **February 26, 1862.** These three "Feb 5" Bolivar Heights letters were written **after Feb 28** — likely early March. The "Feb 5" date is a transcription error (possibly "Mar 5" or "Feb 28" misread).

Henry notes he has been in service "a little over nine months" — enlisted ~June 1, 1861, sworn June 15. This timeline is consistent with all other letters and can serve as an anchor. He survived this winter quarter but would be killed in action at the Battle of Antietam, September 17, 1862 — just seven months after this letter.

The religious gifts (Testament and Psalms) reflect the strong Christian culture of Civil War regiments. Henry values them highly and mentions them favorably in multiple letters.
