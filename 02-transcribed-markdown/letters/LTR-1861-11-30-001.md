## Full Transcription

> Nov. 30, 1861
> Camp McClellan
>
> Dear James:
>
> I was very glad to get a letter from you. I should like it if you would write often. I must tell you what news I can. When our company was on picket a secesh or reble came across the river, near our post. He came over as a deserter, had a very small boat. It was most full of water when he got over. He had a very fine gun and a large knife with him. We heard him paddling the water some time before he came across. There were twelve of us on post. We all had our guns loaded and our bayonets fixed for we did not know but what there was a large force of them. When he was nearly over we halted him and demanded the countersign, but he called us all sons of biches. He had been taking a drop before he came over. The rebles, next morning, wanted him to go back, but he would not. We can see the rebles quite plainly in the day time. They have fires all along the river at night. It is against the orders to talk with the rebles across the river.
>
> I have got two secesh caps, those that they use for "Guns". Lt. Scott bought a large knife that the rebles brought over. He bought it for five dollars. He bought it for a curiosity to show when he goes home what the rebles use in war. No more this time.
>
> I remain, your aff. Brother,
>
> Henry.

## Editorial Notes

A short, vivid letter — the drunk Confederate deserter paddling across the Potomac in a leaky boat, cursing the Union pickets when challenged, then refusing to go back when the Confederates called for him the next morning. The detail of seeing rebel fires along the river at night and the prohibition on cross-river conversation creates a portrait of two armies in close, uneasy proximity. Henry's souvenir gun caps and Lt. Scott's purchased knife show the informal economy of the Potomac picket line.
