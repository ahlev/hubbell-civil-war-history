## Full Transcription

> Feb. 7th, 1862
> Baltimore, Md.
>
> My Dear Mother:
>
> Enclosed I send Capt. Ransom's photograph. The men (most all) were determined to have them and volunteered to pay fifteen cents for each one (the price of them.) The Capt. would not allow this, but very generously gave us enough to supply nearly all the Company. Please keep it carefully for me.
>
> I received a letter from home and one from Poolesville this morning. What do you think I found in my 'Poolesville letter'? Whay 'A Valentine' with a 'raw recruit' represented on it. I need not tell you that I had not much reason to be flattered. I'll show them that Baltimore can do as much as the '34th'.
>
> I wrote Charles a day or two since, also Henry. I have written Henry again today. I feel very anxious about James. It will not be him alone that will suffer if he fails. The whole family will spur him up, encourage him. Tell him to persevere. Love to all.
>
> Your affectionate son,
>
> Alexander
>
> P./S. I shall write Frances soon.

## Editorial Notes

**FAMILY DYNAMICS & INTER-REGIMENTAL COMMUNICATION**

Alexander mentions writing to "Charles a day or two since, also Henry" and writes Henry again "today" — showing active correspondence among brothers in different regiments. This is significant for tracing how information flowed within the Hubbell family during the war. Henry was in Co. D, 34th NY; Alexander was in Co. H, 60th NY; Charles and James appear to be at home or in academic settings at this point.

**VALENTINE & POOLESVILLE CONNECTION**

The "Valentine" from Poolesville with a "raw recruit" representation is intriguing. Poolesville, Maryland was a supply and garrison area during the war. The sender's identity is unclear — could be a civilian admirer, a fellow soldier (if it was a joke from barracks), or a family friend. The "raw recruit" might be self-deprecating humor or a taunt. Alexander's reply ("I'll show them that Baltimore can do as much as the '34th'") suggests competitive unit pride — his 60th NY vs. Henry's 34th NY.

**CAPTAIN RANSOM'S GENEROSITY**

Capt. Ransom paid for 15-cent photographs to distribute to his company rather than charging the men — a notable act of officer kindness that Alexander felt worth reporting. This contrasts with some accounts of officer-soldier relations and reflects the morale-building efforts some officers employed.

**JAMES'S SITUATION (PRE-WEST POINT)**

By Feb. 7, Alexander is "very anxious about James" and warns he must "persevere." Four days later (Feb. 11), in `LTR-1862-02-11-001`, Alexander celebrates James's West Point appointment as *confirmed*. So between Feb. 7 and Feb. 11, James's appointment was finalized. This letter shows the family's anxiety in the days before that confirmation.

## Event: Valentine Receipt & Unit Comparison

On the morning of Feb. 7, Alexander receives both a letter from home and one from Poolesville containing a Valentine with a "raw recruit" representation. His playful response — "I'll show them that Baltimore can do as much as the '34th'" — indicates his 60th NY (or Baltimore garrison) is in friendly competition with his brother Henry's 34th NY. This may reflect rivalry between units or simply brotherly banter.
