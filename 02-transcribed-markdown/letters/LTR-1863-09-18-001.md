## Full Transcription

> Sept. 18, 1863
> Raccoon Ford
> Rapidan River, Va.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I have just witnessed the execution of two deserters. They belonged to this Brigade. Every man that was not on duty was obliged to be there. One of the poor fellows was obliged to shoot three times before they killed him. They sat upon their coffins beside their graves where they were shot. After the execution we were all marched by the coffins that we might see them and learn a lesson. I think these examples will have a very salutary effect upon the army. Five others were shot today in other parts of the army.
>
> Company H. presented a sword to Captain Delaney today. Did you receive the fifteen dollars I sent you not long since? We are being paid again today. I shall send twenty dollars this time. I received your letter of the 12 inst. last night. It does me good to hear from you. Please write often. Tell Frances I want longer letters from her. I shall write her soon if possible. Yesterday was the anniversary of the battle of Antietam. I presume I shall be in another battle soon. I am in God's hands and the thought gives me comfort — does it not you? I trust that my life will be spared to see this unholy rebellion crushed and the country restored to prosperity and happiness again. What joyful days those will be!
>
> Love to all. I have received James' photograph. It does not look very natural to me. He has changed very much, I think.
>
> Your aff. son,
>
> Alexander Hubbell
>
> P.S. I send the express receipt I received for the fifteen dollars, peradventure you have not received the money.

### Historical Significance: `major`

> A brigade execution of two deserters, witnessed up close: one man took three volleys to die, and both sat upon their own coffins beside open graves while every off-duty soldier was marched past to "learn a lesson." Alexander calls them "poor fellows" yet judges the killings "salutary," with five more men shot elsewhere that day. He notes it was the anniversary of Antietam, and braces for battle: "I am in God's hands and the thought gives me comfort."

## Editorial Notes

"One of the poor fellows was obliged to shoot three times before they killed him." Alexander calls them "poor fellows" — then endorses the executions as "salutary." The ambivalence is revealing: he pities the deserters but supports the punishment. The mandatory attendance — every man not on duty — was designed to make the lesson unforgettable.

The Antietam anniversary falls between the execution and the expectation of another battle. Alexander doesn't mention Henry by name, but "Yesterday was the anniversary of the battle of Antietam" carries the weight of everything unsaid. One year since Henry died on that field — the same field Alexander passed within half a mile of in August and couldn't stop to visit.
