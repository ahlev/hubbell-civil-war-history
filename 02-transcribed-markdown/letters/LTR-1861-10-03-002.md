## Full Transcription

> Oct. 3, 1861
> Camp Wheeler
> Ogdensburgh
>
> My dear Brother:
>
> I presume you will be glad to hear from Camp Wheeler. Well, here I am — been here almost two weeks. I like the life of the soldier very well so far. Our Camp is situated a mile and a half from the town. We use the car factory building as barracks. The fare is excellent, also the sleeping accomodations. Capt. Ransom says th t our uniforms will be here on Saturday. I don't know whether I shall have a Corporals berth or not. I hope I shall. The Captain is very kind to me. I don't believe a better officered company than our has gone to the war.
>
> I had a bath in the Lawrence yesterday. I enjoyed it famously. The Regiment now numbers about eight hundred men and will soon have its thousand. We have now between sixty and seventy men in our Company. Our Company when full will number one hundred and one. We shall soon have it. I must write home to have them get me a pass from Mr. Hoyle. The Captain has promised to give me a furlough as soon as I get my uniform. Don't you envy me. I have enjoyed excellent health ever since I have been here. Write soon.
>
> Your affectionate Brother,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

Alexander's cheerful tone here contrasts sharply with his moral distress just six days earlier (LTR-1861-09-27-002). He's settled in, likes the food, enjoys the Captain's favor, and hopes for promotion to Corporal. The regiment numbers — 800 of 1,000, company at 60-70 of 101 — place the unit in mid-formation. The "car factory building" as barracks locates the camp precisely within Ogdensburg's industrial infrastructure. The "Dear Brother" ambiguity is the first instance of the pattern noted in our QA protocol where Hubbell brothers address letters to unnamed siblings.
