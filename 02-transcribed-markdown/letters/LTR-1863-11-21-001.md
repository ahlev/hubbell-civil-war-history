## Full Transcription

> Nov. 21st, 1863
> Wauhatchie, East Tennessee
>
> Dear Fannie:
>
> So you defy me, eh! I believe I put a flea in your ear in the last letter I wrote home. Didn't I now! Don't you wish you knew who sent those books? I know, but I'll be bound if I'll tell. How did you like "Harry Lorrequer?" (Ins) Maud the Knight of Dettine worth reading? Please let me know how you like them. I'll just tell you what I think. I think your curiosity is pretty well excited. Am I not right? Enough of this. Now for local matters, etc., etc.
>
> We were ordered to fall in last night under arms with knapsacks packed in ten minutes. We marched over to Division headquarters and a — and marched back again. Interesting, wasn't it? I don't believe General Geary had any other purpose than to see how quickly he could move troops from one part of the line to the other. Other regiments were also ordered out. It was rather a dry joke, considering the rain and mud.
>
> So Professor Bass is flourishing, eh! I like the old chap better than any teacher I ever had. I trust the time is not far distant when I shall be able to battle for drunk and lath on him again. Three years is a pretty big slice off a man's life, isn't it? I shall only have to work all the harder to make up for it. Capt. Delaney called me into his tent the other day and said he had received a letter from Capt. Ransom asking him to use his influence in getting me a discharge. He had talked with the surgeon and colonel, but it was not in their power to do anything in regard to it. I knew this well long ago, but Mother seems averse to giving up the idea. Secretary Stanton is the man to apply to, or else the President.
>
> I have now only about eleven months more to serve, but if Mother wishes for my discharge I shall not oppose her.
>
> Say Frances, I wrote in my last letter for Mother to send me by express some shirts, socks, and a pair of boots and said I could pay for them at the Jan. payment. Now if this will inconvenience you at all, don't send them. If you do send them, direct to Nashville, Tennessee. Be very particular in putting on the Regt. Brigade, Division and Corps. Also put on Army of the Cumberland. Also send me a list of the prices of each and the express charges. Love to all. Please write soon.
>
> Your Aff. Brother,
>
> Alexander
>
> I borrowed this sheet, envelope and stamp.

## Editorial Notes

The discharge subplot reaches its clearest articulation. Mother is pulling every string she can — the Colonel, Capt. Ransom, Capt. Delaney — and Alexander indulges her while knowing it's hopeless. "Secretary Stanton is the man to apply to, or else the President." The gap between a Champlain mother's influence and the Secretary of War's attention is immeasurable.

Alexander has 11 months left to serve. In nine days, he will be wounded at Lookout Mountain. The discharge question will become moot.
