## Full Transcription

> Feb. 17, 1864
> Capitol Hill
> Washington, D.C.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> Military movements are sudden so you must not be surprised to hear that we are to leave this place tomorrow morning at 7 o'clock for Alexandria. It was un-expected, our being relieved from duty last night. Today we received our pay for two months which has been due us some time. It is said that we must report at Alexandria tomorrow at 7:00 A.M. I don't know whether we will go on foot or in a steamboat to Alexandria. Neither do I know whether we will stay there or go to Texas or the Army of the Potomac. Some think we are going to Texas. Our Col. has just come back to the regt, and we are all glad of it as he is a good Col. The men cheered him roundly last night. We are all in good spirits about leaving, although I think the most of us had rather stay here than leave.
>
> Enclosed is a $20 check and $10.60 in greenbacks. Please let me know soon when you receive it. Since last pay day I sold my large double cased watch for $17.50 and have just been paid most of it.
>
> Today it is very cold; the coldest day we have had this winter. Hope it will be warmer tomorrow. The most of the men think we are going to Texas and had rather go there than to go to the other armies.
>
> I am expecting a letter from you. Should like to hear about Jas. and Alex. I think Alex's photograph looks very well. I hope you will not worry about us boys. Everything will come out all right. The best way is to make the best of everything. Please write soon. Must close as I am in haste.
>
> From your aff. son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

The comfortable world Charles has known for seven months — Capitol Hill barracks, prison guard duty, Congressional debates, love feasts — is ending overnight. "Military movements are sudden." He sells his watch, collects his back pay, sends money home, and writes a hasty note. The Col. who was under court martial two letters ago returns to cheers — the men's loyalty won out over the charges.

"I hope you will not worry about us boys. Everything will come out all right." Three sons in the army, destination unknown, and Charles offers the reassurance that soldiers always offer mothers.
