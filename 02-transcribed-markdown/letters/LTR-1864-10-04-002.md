## Full Transcription

> Oct. 4, 1864
> Harrisonburg
>
> As the mail leaves soon I will write a few lines. I rejoined the regt. the last day of Sept. There were too many clerks there in the commissary so they told two of us to go to our regts. I am quite fat and am in good health. James is very well. I have no gun yet, as I turned in my gun when I left the regt. Expect to have one soon.
>
> A few days since a recruit from Fresseville came to our Co. He rec'd. $1202 bounty. Enlisted for one year. We have been here three days. I don't know which way we will move from here. Have chased the rebs a long way. It is about ninety miles from here to Harper's Ferry. The cavalry have been as far as Stanton and have burned barns and grist mills. We advanced to the next town about 8 miles from here where we got a lot of flour and came back here the next day. The troops have suffered some from want of rations. Rations are wagoned here from Harper's Ferry. On the march the troops we foraged a great deal. Pigs, sheep, chickens, apples, butter, etc. There is a Provost Marshall to stop such doings but it is done for all that. This is a rich valley — a fine farming country. There are a great many wounded rebs in the hospital here. We have also passed other towns where the rebs were in hospitals. They were sent this way after the fight near Bunker Hill.
>
> Recruits are coming to this army fast. Quite a number have joined our regt. We have burned all the fences for a long ways around this place except where there is a safe guard. These oak rails make a good fire. This is a bad place to camp as water and wood are scarce. Have to go quite a distance for them. Do you know a soldier's daily duty? It is to cook, clean his gun and look over his clothes and pick off the vermin. On a march the soldiers get pretty lousy as they have no kettles to heat water to boil their clothes. It is a common saying that a man is no soldier unless he is lousy. Some of the men think that we are going to Louisiana to spend the winter. I hope not.
>
> Have you rec'd seventy-five dollars from Albany — James' state bounty? James rec'd two or three days since two democratic newspapers (Journal of Commerce). We expect to receive a letter from you next mail. It is some time since we received one.
>
> Charles

## Editorial Notes

Charles's Shenandoah description is pure quartermaster's eye: $1,202 bounties, rations wagoned 90 miles, fences burned for fuel, foraging despite the Provost Marshall. "Pigs, sheep, chickens, apples, butter, etc." — the inventory of a rich valley being consumed by an army.

"Do you know a soldier's daily duty? It is to cook, clean his gun and look over his clothes and pick off the vermin." This is Charles at his most characteristic — the practical observer who describes war as housekeeping. "A man is no soldier unless he is lousy." Alexander would never write that sentence, but Charles can, because Charles sees the army as work, not drama.
