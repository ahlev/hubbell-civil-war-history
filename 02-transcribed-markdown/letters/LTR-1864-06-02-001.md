## Full Transcription

> June 2, 1864
> Camp on the Bank of the
> Mississippi River
> Morganza Bend,
> State of La.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> Have just heard that a steamer leaves in a short time for New Orleans so will write you a few lines. As I have to get ready for inspection I cannot write much. This morning I had the pleasure of receiving a letter from you dated May 7th, also two newspapers which are very acceptable. Two or three days since I received four letters which were directed to H. Qtrs. N.O. They were dated April 12th, 20th, 26th & May 5d. The letter I rec'd this morning was directed to the regiment and was directed right.
>
> All the men here have built a framework over the tents & have covered the framework with bushes which makes it shady and cool for us. A shelter from the sun is made in the same way for the horses. We do not expect to go out of this department but cannot tell anything about it. I think we will lie in camp till cool weather.
>
> I have got pretty well rested after our march and now have a good appetite. On the march we too tired to have much appetite. Each man in our Co. does his own cooking. We draw all the rations raw. Instead of hard tack for one day we drew flour and last night and this morning I made pancakes. I mixed the flour with water, put in a little salt and fried the cakes in a frying pan. They were heavy, but good. We also have bean soup. It is said that we are to have Co. cooks. I hope we will for it takes about all the time to cook and wash.
>
> James has been made Lance Corporal, that is, acts as corporal. He has to drill with the recruits but don't know how long a time he will have to. It is very pleasant for us to be together. He has his bounty with him and will send it home when he can find an express agent. His trunk he gave away. Says he was about two weeks in Albany at a hotel, paid three dollars a day for board. I guess he never showed his papers from the W.P. officers. James says he told the recruiting officer when asked his place of residence that it was Champlain, Clinton Co., N.Y. He gave his occupation as a farmer. He rec'd $860 bounty and was told that he would receive $975 at his regt. Is he not entitled to Clinton Co. bounty? I think it would be well to inquire about it. He enlisted March 9th in New York City. I hope Alex is all right. Please write soon and give Alex's address. Is his corps the 12th? Is 20th Corps right? Love to all.
>
> From your aff. son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

James Hubbell gave his occupation as "farmer" when he enlisted — the West Point cadet who accumulated demerits now a private in his brother's regiment, hiding his academy background from the recruiting officer. Already promoted to Lance Corporal, drilling recruits. The youngest brother has found his footing fast.

Charles making pancakes in a frying pan — flour, water, salt — "heavy, but good." The domestic soldier cooking for himself while his brother commands recruits nearby. The war has given them this unexpected gift: the same tent, the same rations, the same camp on the Mississippi.
