## Full Transcription

> Dec. 11th, 1861
> Camp Jackson
>
> My dear Mother:
>
> Here is my "picture." Luther and I were at Baltimore yesterday and had them taken. I was badly cheated on account of my inexperience in such things. Those two "pictures" cost thirteen shillings. That which id dearly bought is apt to be prized. I shall profit by this experience. The picture in which Luther and I are taken cost ten shillings. When that picture was given to me I felt like making a bayonet charge upon the one who took it. I said to myself "I cannot and will not send home that picture. I will have another taken if it costs me fifty dollars." I went to another gallery and had the other taken which cost three shillings. If you think it is worth it you had better have it put in a case as it is easily spoilt. The other picture I want you to put safely away in some drawer and show it to no one. The only reason why I send it is I want to keep it as a memorial of our soldiering together.
>
> My dear Mother, will you be so kind as to send me some postage stamps. I will refund the money to you next pay day. I feel bad to think I have spent so much money. I want to be very economical. I am enjoying excellent health. The weather has been very warm and pleasant. Love to all.
>
> Your affectionate son,
>
> Alexander.

## Editorial Notes

Alexander's photography adventure captures a universal Civil War experience — soldiers getting portraits taken before (potentially) going into battle. His fury at the first gallery ("I felt like making a bayonet charge upon the one who took it") and his determination to get a better picture at a second gallery show his pride. The joint photo with Luther, intended as a private "memorial of our soldiering together," documents the deepening friendship between these two guard companions from Camp Wheeler through Camp Jackson.
