## Full Transcription

> Dec. 20th, 1861
> Camp McClellan
>
> Dear Frances:
>
> I send this letter by B. Miner. He starts tomorrow morning for Champlain. I gave him my other letter also. The reason why I did not write to you before is because I had nothing to write about. Everything goes on here about the same from day to day. I have got an accordian. Bill Robers offered to learn me how to play on it. I have not made much progress yet, but I will after a while. You know how I play the violin. I have not continued my journal any since it has been so cold. That is one thing I shall be sorry for if I ever get home. I heard through one of the boys from Champlain that they had a sing school there, I should like to attend if I was at home. Albert has been home and had a fine time sleigh riding as I heard. To day eight rebels came across to our side as deserters. They say the rebels have nothing to eat. We went to church today — Capt. Rich preached. Poolesville is about three miles from our camp. Write often.
>
> I am your aff. Brother,
>
> Henry

## Editorial Notes

Henry's admission that "I had nothing to write about" and "everything goes on here about the same from day to day" captures winter quarters monotony. His accordion lessons from Bill Robers and regret about abandoning his journal show a young man trying to fill time. The eight rebel deserters reporting starvation is intelligence-level detail — Confederate supply shortages along the Potomac were real by December 1861. The mention of B. Miner carrying letters directly to Champlain shows the parallel mail system soldiers used: official U.S. mail plus men traveling home on furlough or discharge serving as private couriers. Henry's wistfulness about the sing school at home is a quiet homesickness marker.
