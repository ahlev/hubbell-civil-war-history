## Full Transcription

> Feb. 5th, 1862
> Camp Gormon — Bolivor Heights
>
> Dear Frances:
>
> You will no doubt be a little surprised when you receive this letter to know that our Regt. and Brigade are now in Camp two miles from Harpers Ferry, but so it is, and here we are. Shall not probably remain long. We left Camp McClellan on the 25th of Feb. very early in the morning with our knapsacks and two days rations with us. The first day we marched about twenty miles. You may be sure that I felt tired at night and was thankful to get a hard cracker and cold water, salt junk, for that was all we could have.
>
> The first night we camped out with nothing but the sky for a covering except our blankets. Sleep was of course out of the question for it was very cold. I was up next morning at three, had cup of coffee and hard cracker, and was off at day light. This is what I call soldier life, but we may see hard times yet. We marched till about noon when we arrived at Adams Town. We took the cars there for Harpers Ferry, a ride of thirty miles in a freight train. Anything will do for soldiers you know. That is what I think.
>
> Harpers Ferry is a perfect wreck. In some places large splendid buildings burnt, nothing but the walls remain. Also the bridge that was burnt by the rebels. They have about ruined the place. I saw a large locomotive in the river which was the work of the rebels. Harpers Ferry is a large place, but not as large as Montreal is. There is one thing very evident. The Rebels will never have the chance to go into that place again and do what damage or as much more as they have done without a little skirmishing. That is as long as the 3rd remains anywhere *(34th) around. When this Regt. crossed over the River on a bridge of boats, or a floating bridge, it was called the best looking Regt. that passed over. The Rebels must look out for the 34th. I have become so accustomed to it now that I can march 19 or 20 miles a day with my knapsack and not be the worst for it the next day. Nothing like getting used to anything.
>
> (Contd.)
>
> Feb. 5th 1862 — Contd.
>
> I received a letter from home last night with yours. They are all well at home. I hope you will excuse this miserable scrall. I have to write the best way I can. I have not seen Albert or Miner for some time. Write as often as you can.
>
> I remain your aff. Brother,
>
> Henry
>
> Feb. 6th: I saw Miner and Cook this morn. Only had time to shake hands with them. They have gone on to Charleston for signal core. It is very mild today. I would mail this dirrect to Montreal but I have no postage stamps. Albert is very anxious to know who it was that shed tears or felt so bad among the young ladys when he left home last. He is very anxious to know.

### ⚠ DATING DISCREPANCY — RESOLVED

Henry says "We left Camp McClellan on the 25th of Feb." but the letter is dated Feb 5 from Bolivar Heights. This is internally contradictory.

**RESOLUTION (from LTR-1862-02-28-001):** Henry's Feb 28 letter from Harpers Ferry states unambiguously: "Our Regt. Left Camp McClellan on the 26th." Combined with LTR-1862-02-24-001 (Henry at Camp McClellan on Feb 24, orders read at dress parade to pack knapsacks), the departure date is confirmed as **February 26, 1862.**

**Conclusion:** This letter's "Feb 5" date is a transcription error. The letter was written **after Feb 28** — likely early March. The "25th of Feb" departure date mentioned in the text is approximately correct (off by one day from the Feb 26 confirmed in the Feb 28 letter). The original 1947-49 transcriber likely misread Henry's handwriting — "Feb 5" may have been "Mar 5" or another date.

## Editorial Notes

Henry's first march narrative is vivid: twenty miles the first day, sleeping under the sky in freezing cold, up at three for coffee and hardtack, then a freight train ride through the Shenandoah Valley to the iconic wreckage of Harpers Ferry. His description of the destruction — burnt armory buildings, the bridge destroyed, a locomotive dumped in the Potomac — is an eyewitness account of Confederate scorched-earth tactics. The pontoon bridge crossing and the regiment being called "the best looking" to cross shows unit pride solidifying through shared hardship. The dating discrepancy ("25th of Feb" in a Feb 5 letter) is a significant QA issue — the transcriber or Henry himself confused the dates. Tier-2 verification against the 34th NY regimental movement records should resolve this.
