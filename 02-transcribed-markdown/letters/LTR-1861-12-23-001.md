## Full Transcription

> Dec. 23, 1861
> Baltimore
>
> My Dear Mother:
>
> I have received a letter from you today and very glad I was to hear that you are all so well. Your letter contains some strange news — very strange. Champlain again to be occupied by United States troops — well — well — well. What next? "Stranger things have happened." I will not tell you something about as strange. I may be home a little sooner than you expected. Preston King is here it is said trying to have the Regiment sent North to guard the frontiers.
>
> Dec. 25. Christmas is it? So it is. I a soldier hundreds of miles from home! How funny, eh? Luther and I were out in the City yesterday. Baltimore is quite a city. There is no lack of "darkies" here. I wonder what you are doing up there today! How I should like to be with you all today, but then there is no use in wishing. It cannot be. I feel rather lonely today. You have no idea how much good your letters did me. I was particularly pleased to hear that the "white cow" had a little "bossy". I should indeed like to be at home to enjoy the "new milk cow". I should like to return and see you all, nevertheless I am very well contented where I am.
>
> Dec. 23, 1861 — Contd.
>
> You tell James that I do not doubt his good intentions, but if he waits until vacation to write I am afraid that letter never will be forthcoming.
>
> Dec. 26. Luther, Orderly Dickinson, I and three others were out in the city. We went into an eating saloon and got dinner. It cost me a quarter. I find the only place to get a Christmas dinner is at home. I also find that it does not pay to get dinner in these eating saloons. I am on guard duty today. At one oclock I have a relief of seven men to take out to relieve the pickets along the line (about two miles.) Now you have some idea of the duties of a Corporal.
>
> Love to all. Give my respects to Mr. Copeland. I have written grandmother McNiel.
>
> Your affectionate son,
>
> Alexander.
>
> P.S. It is reported that Mason and Stidell have been released.

## Editorial Notes

Alexander's Christmas 1861 is spent hundreds of miles from home, but his letter is more wistful than despairing. The detail about the white cow's calf ("bossy") grounding him in farm life at home is touching — a corporal guarding the B&O Railroad still delighted by a new milk cow. Preston King's presence in Baltimore lobbying for northern frontier defense is historically significant: it shows how the Trent Affair with Britain created real political pressure to redeploy troops to the Canadian border, especially from New York regiments raised near that border. Alexander — from Champlain, a few miles from Canada — would have been a natural fit. The P.S. about Mason and "Stidell" being released is remarkably timely: Seward formally agreed to release them on Dec 26, the same day Alexander wrote his final entry. The Trent Affair arc across Alexander's letters (Dec 16 → Dec 23-26) captures the crisis from war scare to resolution.
