## Full Transcription

> Feb. 21, 1864
> Stephenson, Ala.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> It is a most beautiful Sabbath morning. The sun is shining warm and pleasant, the birds are singing, and every thing is hushed to an unusual quiet. It is such a day as is seldom vouchsafed to us soldiers. Nevertheless all are on the alert. The horse thief Morgan is on the move with a force of some eight thousand men and there is no telling upon what particular part he will fall. All the troops stationed here, with the exception of our regt., were under arms last night. All his motions are watched and I am afraid he will find it hard to come upon our troops unprepared.
>
> We reached here night before last and — most wonderful to relate — found a Soldier's Home established here where we were provided with an excellent supper and good sleeping accommodations. Yesterday we established our camp and commenced carrying timber to build our shanties. Today most of us are resting from our labors. My three tent mates and myself had a confab together and came to the conclusion that we would not work upon our shanty today as soldiers above all needed God's blessing — and Sabbath breaking would not be very likely to get it. My tent mates are Corporal Kelly, Oliver Lafountain, and George Bully. Two of them you have seen.
>
> Opposite our camp a large number of soldiers are buried — victims of Chickamauga. After that battle the General Hospital was established here and these men died from the effects of their wounds.
>
> The veterans of 64 attached to the 60th N.Y. had a warm welcome from the old 3rd Brigade when they reached here. (A gentleman attached to the Christian Com., I cannot, just gave me a paper entitled "the Parlor Visitor." So you see I have something good to read today.) The Brigade Band gave them a grand serenade accompanied by lusty cheers from the men of the Brigade present which were promptly responded to by the Veterans. "The Old Sixtieth" is of some account down here, you see.
>
> I have talked with a number of rebs since I came here. They all tell the same story of want and discouragement in the rebel ranks. I was talking with a rebel Capt. of Cavalry yesterday. "Yes," said he in conclusion to what he was saying in regard to the rebel conscription, "they send a whole regt. of cavalry to guard half a dozen conscripts." One of the rebs told me that it was generally believed in the rebel army that this war will terminate about June. He says Johnson's (formerly Bragg's) army swear that they will not stand in the next battle.
>
> Gen'l. Slocum made a speech here not long since in which he prophesied a speedy termination of the war. There does not seem to be any doubt of the war's terminating this year.
>
> I have done my best to go into minute details. I am afraid you will get sick of it. If you do let me know. Tell Frances I shall certainly write her soon if it be possible. Love to all.
>
> Your aff. Son,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell
>
> Gave you heard from James yet? Be particular when you direct your letters to put on the Brigade, Division of Corps, also Army of the Cumberland. The bugles are blowing the dinner call. I must hurry up. One of the rebs said, "You think you took a great many prisoners at the last fight, and so you did, but wait until the next fight and you will be prisoners."

## Editorial Notes

Alexander's Sabbath morning at Stephenson is one of the most lyrical passages in the collection — "the sun is shining warm and pleasant, the birds are singing, and every thing is hushed to an unusual quiet." The tent mates' decision not to work on Sunday because "soldiers above all needed God's blessing" is a rare moment of collective piety.

The rebel captain's remark about conscription — "they send a whole regt. of cavalry to guard half a dozen conscripts" — is a devastating indictment of Confederate manpower collapse from inside. The postscript rebel's defiant retort — "wait until the next fight and you will be prisoners" — provides the other side. Alexander includes both voices.

The Chickamauga dead buried opposite camp are a silent commentary on where this war has been. Alexander walked past them to build his shanty.
