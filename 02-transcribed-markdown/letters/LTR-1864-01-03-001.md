## Full Transcription

> Jan. 3, 1864
> Capitol Hill
> Washington, D.C.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I wish you and Frances a happy New Year. Did either of you pass a pleasant New Year's day? I was on guard but passed it well enough. On that day we had boiled flour pudding for supper. It was new business for our cooks and they put the puddings on the bottom of the kettles; one burst and burst the bag; the other two were then raised from the bottom of the kettles by a string tied to them and fastened to the floor above. The pudding was not very good; the raisins were the best part of it. On Christmas we had for dinner 8 turkeys — price $11.50; 2 kegs of lager beer, price $5.50; and a baked bread pudding with raisins in it. The dinner was bought with Co. Funds. I do not like beer so did not drink any. A good many of the boys got quite happy on it.
>
> Christmas morning an Irishman belonging to our Co. was found at the side of the road dead drunk by one of our men. He shouldered him and brought him into camp as if he had been a dead pig. The Irishman says he was having his Christmas.
>
> A good many of the old regts. are reenlisting for three years or the war. They get a 30 days furlough and a large bounty. Their reenlistment is dated from the time they get their furlough and not from next summer when their time is out. In this way the soldier gains several months time. I think Alex had better not reenlist although he can get $1,000 bounty.
>
> Two or three nights since our regt. and the 14th New Hampshire received marching orders with three days rations, but the order was countermanded next day. We were to go to "Hardsburger" or Harper's Ferry and some back in a few days, but they say that if we had once left here we would not have come back. A Brigade from Meade's Army went, so we did not have to go. When we hear about marching orders we don't believe it because it is an old story. We still believe we are going when the knapsacks are on our backs.
>
> Our regt. has done a good deal in the stealing line lately taking turkeys, chickens, etc. A woman came running into our guard house for a guard, saying a soldier stole a turkey and a piece of meat right before her face. Of course she did not get a guard, for the soldier was not going to stay there and be arrested. A guard could not have found him. Most of our regt. are good men but there are some great rascals in it.
>
> Who surveyed the wood land and how much did it cost? I think from what you wrote the surveyors found the true line. Enclosed I send you a letter from Alex that I received about a week since. I forgot to tell you in my other letters that I saw Geo. Dodds some time ago. He came from the hospital to see us Champlainers. He had been sick with a fever and was not then entirely well. Please write soon.
>
> From your aff. son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell
