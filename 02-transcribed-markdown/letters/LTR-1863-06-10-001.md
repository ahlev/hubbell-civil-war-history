## Full Transcription

> June 10, 1863
> Alexandria
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I received your welcome letter of June 2nd yesterday and was glad to hear from you. Enclosed is a few lines that I received from Alex. Received two more letters from him dated the 7th this morning. He wrote me the first letter that I received today because he wanted to buy a gold ring for a man, and he wrote the second because he forgot to put the measure of the ring in the first, and as he forgot to put it in the second and was on duty he requested Amos Luther to write to me and enclose the measure, which he has done, so that I read three letters or notes from Alex and Amos this morning. Amos says his father has been there to visit him and stayed two days. He went to Falmouth. Amos' letter was dated June 8th and says they expect to move soon.
>
> One of the magazines attached to Fort Lyons exploded yesterday killing twenty and wounding about the same number. It made a great noise. This fort is a mile or two from this city and is in sight of our reg't. camp. But I guess I have told you this before. I have had some good swims in the Potomac. My health is good and I hope that you and Frances are enjoying good health.
>
> When I visited Alex I staid over night with him; staid about one day. He was well. I think it would be pleasant for you and Frances to make a visit together to Charlotte while M. Smith is there. Can't Frances get some one to take her two classes while she is gone and put on her French till she gets back? It would be a pleasant visit for you both. How do Charlotte affairs come on? Have not heard anything about them for a long time.
>
> I think that you ought to apply at once for Henry's back pay and bounty. For if those who apply first receive the money you will have to wait a good while. He was killed, without a doubt.
>
> We have an hours drill every morning before breakfast and drill a while after supper, also I have plenty of reading here, more than I can read. I see the daily papers quite often. Welky in our tent, has got the History of Rome, also of France, also of United States. Small volumes, each of them. I have not read them, but intend to do so.
>
> I must close as I am in haste.
>
> Your Aff. Son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

"He was killed, without a doubt." Five words that close the Henry investigation. Charles has said it before, but never this flatly, never as a prelude to "apply at once for Henry's back pay and bounty." The transition from mourning to bureaucracy is complete. Henry is dead; the family needs his money; file the paperwork. This is not coldness — it is the pragmatism of a family that has been waiting eight months and cannot afford to wait any longer.
