## Full Transcription

> Dec. 19th, 1861
> Camp McClellan
>
> Dear Chas:
>
> I received a letter from you and also two papers. We are having very fine weather for this time of the year. Our Co. came off from picket yesterday. The probability is now that our Regt. will stay here for winter quarters to guard the Potomac. That is what I heard. The Minnesota Regts. are fixing up for winter, building log houses. Our Regt. is also building a hospital. This looks somewhat like staying.
>
> You wished to know who tents with me. They ar strangers to me. E. Graves from Chasy and John Green and A. Perkins. Pleasant fellows, but I would rather tent with some of the Champlain boys, but the tents are all full. I get very lonesome now and then. I go on line guard every week. We have three drills every day besides dress parade. We have also three Brigade drills every week. Three Regts. besides our march in line of battle, etc., etc. The old sutler was drumed out of Camp yesterday for selling licor, good for him, but some of the boys don't like it. We have one sutler in our Camp now. I have not got any tickets from him since pay day nor do I intend to. He charges to much. The boys cannot get anything to drink but cider now.
>
> I am much oblige to you for sending me some of my money. I am not shure that I will get a pr. of boots. If I do not I think I will not use the money. In regard to sending money home — I paid 31 cts. freight. After this I shall send home my money in a letter. It is about as safe. I have not heard from Alexander yet. I have written him, but it seems that the direction is not correct. I shall write him soon. So far as I know the boys from Champlain like soldiering very well. Col. L--- has resigned so I have heard. Good many of the boys thought he was a coward. He has a very good voice. xxxxxxxxxxxxxxxxx A good many of the boys pass away there time by playing cards, but I have not learned yet. If they are caught at it they are severely punished. Some of our Regt. were caught gambling the other night. They will have to stand on a barrel six hours without any food and four dollars taken off their wages. Write often. I am your aff. Brother,
>
> Henry

## Editorial Notes

Henry's loneliness is quietly present — strangers for tentmates, lonesome "now and then," unable to reach Alexander despite both being in the Maryland/Virginia theater. The sutler being drummed out for selling liquor and the barrel-standing punishment for gamblers show ongoing discipline enforcement. The Col. L--- resignation is notable — the men thought him a coward, which suggests possible Ball's Bluff-related tensions (Ball's Bluff was Oct 21, in this area). Henry's financial pragmatism (31 cents freight too much; will mail money in letters instead) shows how privates managed their $13/month.
