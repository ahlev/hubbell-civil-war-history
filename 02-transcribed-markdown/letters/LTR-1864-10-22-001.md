## Full Transcription

> Oct. 22, 1864
> Camp near Strasburg, Va.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> James and I rec'd a lot of newspapers Oct. 18th and were anticipating a good deal of pleasure next day in reading them. Well, next morning I was awakened by a sharp volley of musketry. James exclaimed, "The Rebs are here!" We jumped up and put on our accoutrements and fell in behind our breastworks, but in a few minutes were ordered to pack up. We took our tents down in a jiffy. By that time the rebs were close to us. It was a surprise attack. They got behind our breastworks before we knew it. Our Brigade was ordered to leave our breastworks and form on the other side of a deep ravine. We jumped over the breastworks very quick as the Rebs were firing into us and we were moving up the hill the bullets came very thick. A good many of our men were shot here. James was wounded near this place. A Sergeant of our Co. says that as he was going to the rear with a wounded man he saw James; that James rec'd. a flesh wound in the thigh or hip and walked about five miles to the hospital. Says James had made a bandage of his drawers. He went with James to the hospital and says James is all right. James Lucas was reported killed, but I have since learned that he was taken to the hospital shot through the head and arm. Sergeant Pelkier (pronounced Pelky) from Corbeau of our town was shot through the leg. Alex Douglas has been missing about ten or twelve days. While on the march here, he and another man of our Co. were either captured or killed. It is supposed that they got to the rear and were taken prisoners by guerillas. Every one is very sorry about Douglas as he was very much liked. He and the other man were mounted. James Lucas wrote Mr. Douglas about Alex. Lieut. Col. Strain was wounded in the arm in the fight on the 19th of Oct. Our Regt. had about 50 wounded and 8 killed. Our Co. had 7 wounded and 2 killed.
>
> The Rebs drove us out of our camp in the morning when we lost a great many men, but we rallied and drove them, recapturing all our artillery and all of theirs. We drove them out of our camp and beyond to Fisher's Hill. The Rebs came into our camp by the flank where the 8th Corps lay. I am very thankful that I did not get scratched. I think that I have received all your letters but am sorry that you have not received mine. I wrote two from Harrisburg. Am very sorry about Lucas. He rec'd a letter from home the night before the fight and intended to write the next day. Did you receive a letter from Dr. Sweeney some time since? He inquired of me if you did. Please write soon and send me a few stamps.
>
> From your aff. Son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

"James exclaimed, 'The Rebs are here!'" — the Battle of Cedar Creek begins in the Hubbell tent with a shout. Charles tells the story of surprise and chaos: breastworks overrun, brigade ordered across a ravine, bullets thick on the hill, James hit. The bandage made from drawers — the youngest brother's first wound, improvised from underwear.

Cedar Creek was Early's last great gamble — a dawn surprise attack that rolled up the Union left (the 8th Corps) and drove Sheridan's army from its camp. Then Sheridan rode from Winchester ("Sheridan's Ride" — the famous poem) and rallied the troops for a crushing counterattack. Charles tells none of the famous story — no Sheridan, no ride, no poetry. He tells the soldier's story: "We jumped up and put on our accoutrements." "The bullets came very thick." "I am very thankful that I did not get scratched."

The casualty list is the Hubbell family's worst day: James wounded, Lucas shot through the head, Pelky through the leg, Douglas still missing, Strain's arm, 7 wounded and 2 killed in the company alone. And the newspapers from October 18 that James and Charles were looking forward to reading — never read.
