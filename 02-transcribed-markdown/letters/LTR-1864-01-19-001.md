## Full Transcription

> Jan. 19, 1864
> Capitol Hill
> Washington, D.C.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I rec'd. your welcome letter of Jan. 11th a few days since and would have answered it before but I have been on guard and we have had general inspection. Am sorry that Alex has reenlisted, but it may be all for the best. You did not say a word about James in your last letter so I infer that he passed the examination. How does he get along? Have not heard from him for a long time. Just before I rec'd. Alex's letter dated in Tennessee, I wrote him. Has he rec'd the letter? I hope Alex will pass a pleasant furlough. Think it would be the best thing he could do to try to improve his penmanship. He can write after copy slates at home; also look over his arithmetic. I wish he would devote himself for a while to these two branches.
>
> Corporal Trombly of our Co. and from the town of Ghash has passed the examining board in this city and has got a commission as 2nd Lieut. in a colored regt. He has reported for duty.
>
> Our regt. is in a queer state. The Col. and Adjutant are under arrest. Ex-Lieut. Colonel Armstrong, our Ex Col., also ex Lieut. Col. Prentoff, also ex-quartermaster, and several others have got to give an account of themselves. It seems there was a fraud in our being mustered at Fonda. Some men belonging to one Co. would go to another Co. to fill it up, the same men being mustered two or three times. The Col. is being tried by a court martial for husking and gagging the men; also for trying to take the mark U.S. off a government horse. Some time since Capt. Spalding was also missed from our regt. for incompetency. In revenge he has preferred charges against our Col. It is he that is making the trouble. The men like Col. Davis and he is a good military officer and would be sorry to lose him. They hope he will not be broke.
>
> In the false muster, bounty was drawn from the state for men that were not in the regt.
>
> Yesterday it rained all day and the mud is deep, but today it is pleasant. Two days since I saw George and Chas. Dodds. They both look healthy. Chas. told me that he had reenlisted. Tell Alex that Mr. Lafountain was here with Chas. Dodds. Lafountain says his brother in your Co. did not reenlist. How many men of your regt. were injured by an accident on the Ogdensburg railroad. Lafountain said that about 4 of your regt. were injured. Is this true? He and Lablue, also Trudell, are in the 6th Cavalry. Lafountain was visiting this city on a five days furlough. Says he will not reenlist.
>
> Perhaps you do not know what bucking and gagging is. A stick is placed in the mouth and tied with a string, the hands are fastened together at the wrist and placed under the knees and over the arms so that the culprit cannot draw his arms up. The man that is bucked sits on the floor and when it is done cannot move. Niggers are punished in this way in the South.
>
> I hope you and Alex will write soon. Will you send me James' letter if you have received any I tely, and I will return it. How does Frances get to school this bad weather? It would be well to hire a sleigh to carry her back and forth to school. Has Mr. Bass a large school this term?
>
> I wrote to Mary McNeill a few days since. I had owed her a letter a long time and before I answered it rec'd another from her. Then I wrote. Enclosed is the letter. Will close by wishing you and Frances a pleasant visit with Alex.
>
> From your aff. son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

The false muster scandal reveals systematic corruption: men mustered in multiple companies to inflate bounty payments from the state. The Colonel is under court martial for bucking and gagging — a punishment Charles describes with the clinical precision of an observer who has seen it done. "Niggers are punished in this way in the South" — the comparison between military discipline and slavery's violence is made without irony.

Trombly's commission in a colored regiment is quietly historic. A corporal from a small New York town, passing an examining board in Washington and becoming an officer commanding Black troops — the kind of social revolution happening inside the army while the larger revolution was fought on battlefields.
