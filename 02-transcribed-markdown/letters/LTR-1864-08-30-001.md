## Full Transcription

> Aug. 30/64
> 2d Div. Hospital, 20th A.C.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> Now that I am in a hospital I know you will be anxious to hear from me often and consequently I shall endeavor to be very regular in my correspondence. I am getting along as well as can be expected, though I expect will take some little time to get this scurvy out of my blood. I have very good care taken of me so you need not be troubled on that score.
>
> I received your letter of Aug 16th day before yesterday and am glad to hear that you at home are well, and very, very sorry to hear that James is sick in hospital and Charley likely to be soon. Please excuse me, Mother, but I did have a hearty laugh when I took in the idea of your three hopeful sons being in the hospital at the one and same time. I suppose you think it is something that should not be laughed at. Well I agree with you on that point — it is not quite so funny as it might be. Low diet and doctor's medicines are not quite as agreeable as some things.
>
> A truce to the blues and now for something lively. Our Corps has fallen back from Atlanta and is now holding a line along the Chattahoochee. The rest of the army I expect has gone on a grand flanking excursion, heading toward Macon. It is reported here today that the rebs have left Atlanta and gone after Sherman. I expect stirring things will happen in a day or two. General Slocum now commands our 6th Corps. 1st Lieut. P. H. Brookway (formerly Orderly Sergt. of Co. H.) now commands our Co. Capt. Delaney, as you are aware, is at home. There is very little of interest to write. I hope the chronic diarrhea will not get hold of James and Charles. It is a terrible disease. Love to all. Please send some stamps.
>
> Your aff. son,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

Three brothers in three hospitals at the same time — and Alexander laughs. "Please excuse me, Mother, but I did have a hearty laugh when I took in the idea of your three hopeful sons being in the hospital at the one and same time." The dark humor of a veteran. "Low diet and doctor's medicines are not quite as agreeable as some things."

The military news is enormous: Sherman's army has swung south toward Macon in the "grand flanking excursion" that will force Hood to evacuate Atlanta. The 20th Corps holds the Chattahoochee line — and on September 2, they will march into the empty city. Alexander, in the hospital with scurvy, will miss the moment his corps has been fighting toward for four months.
