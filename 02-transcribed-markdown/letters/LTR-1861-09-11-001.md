## Full Transcription

> Sept. 11, 1861
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I received your letter of Sept. 5th and also two papers from home day before yesterday. Very glad to know that matters and things are going on well at home. I received two letters from you while in Charlotte, also two or three from home while you were away, and several papers. I don't doubt but what they all came, as the direction is correct. After this I shall acknowledge all that I receive.
>
> I have come off from picket today. There were six of us on one post with Corporal Kellogg, two on guard at a time, as it is more safe. We had a very fine time while on picket, lived very well.
>
> I never heard of the taking of Fort Hatteras and others till you wrote. That was indeed a noble achievement. A few such battles and the South will have to give in. It is my belief that the war can not last two years, still we can not tell. Time can only tell how long it will continue.
>
> You write that Alexander is very anxious to join Ransoms Company. My opinion and advise is he had better stay at home, and I think I am wright. If he can get a good chance to go to College, he had better go. If not he can make himself xxxxxx yousefull at home. I don't know as I have spelt that long word right. At all events Alex can do better than going to war. I hope he will think the matter over the right way.
>
> Sept. 12th
> We have not received our pay yet. Expect to have it in a few days. I shall send all I have coming except two or three dollars which I may need. Reich will not go to Champlain till pay day comes around. I have now a five dollar gold piece.
>
> We have two brass cannon with our Regt. Fired some xxxx bomb shells over into Virginia last Sabbath morning. They will carry over three miles, but they would not return the favor by fireing back. I have heard this morning that we shall probably winter in Kentucky. Please write often.
>
> Your aff. Son,
>
> Henry
>
> P.S. I heard the Capt. say the other day that if Ransom went to Albany without a full Company it would probably be scattered as they will not receive part of a Co. I suppose that must be so.

## Editorial Notes

This two-part letter is crucial for Alexander's enlistment timeline. Henry explicitly advises Alexander to go to college or stay home rather than join "Ransoms Company." The P.S. adds practical intelligence — if Ransom can't raise a full company, it will be "scattered." Yet Alexander's first letter from Ogdensburgh is dated just 9 days after this one, showing he enlisted despite Henry's counsel. The connection between "Ransoms Company" here and Alexander later addressing letters "care Capt. Ransom" at Camp Wheeler confirms this is the same unit.

The cannon fire into Virginia on Sept 8 and the enemy's refusal to return fire illustrates the static, low-intensity phase of the Washington defenses in late summer 1861.
