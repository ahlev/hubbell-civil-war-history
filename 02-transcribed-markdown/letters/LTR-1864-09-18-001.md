## Full Transcription

> Sept. 18, 1864
> U. S. Field Hospital No. 2
> Chattanooga, Tenn.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> Much to my gratification a day or two since the 60th N.Y. Vets. were sent to this place from the front to escort the Paymaster of the 20th Corps down, and of course they brought what mail there was for me at the Regt. (5 letters.) You can rest assured I was well pleased for I had not heard from you in a long, long, time, and I did not dare have the mail sent here for I was liable to be sent off to Nashville or some other place any time. Direct your letters to the regt. as usual. There is too much uncertainty to have them directed here. I am getting along pretty well, but how soon I shall be fit for duty I am unable to say. I may be well soon and it may take a long time to get this misnamed scurvy out of my blood. However I guess I will be all right before long. The man that gives out the medicine has just been around and given me a gay old drink of lime water — something very similar to lemonade. Then I say lime I don't mean lime such as they make mortar of, but limes, a fruit like lemons.
>
> Luther is with the regt. He has been up to see me twice. A number of the boys have been up to see me. It is pleasant to have them here. The regt. is still here unless they have left this morning. I am sorry I shall not be able to draw pay with the regt. this time. Government owes me some two hundred and forty dollars. I may draw a little pay here, sufficient for my wants but no more.
>
> What do the people of C. think of the draft. We all like it down here. There is no way to put down this rebellion but by force of arms. A few more vigorous pushes will shove them into the Atlantic Ocean.
>
> When you speak in your letters of the tomatoes you are having, and the new potatoes, etc., etc. in such abundance, I long to be there to enjoy these good things with you, and I hope I shall before many months roll around. The letters I received from you were of Aug. 24th and 29th. The other letter was from Jack Ingram. I received six stamps in one of your letters which will do for some little time. I received the money and everything you sent except the tea, I think. Perhaps you did not put on enough postage for the tea. Tell Fannie I will try and write her before a great while. Love to all. Remember me to Mrs. B. If any body wants to know my political principles you tell them I am a "war man." That's enough. Please write often.
>
> Your aff. Son,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell
>
> I am glad to see the copper heads don't like McClellan. It speaks well for him.

## Editorial Notes

"I am a 'war man.'" — Alexander's political identity has found its final form. Not Republican, not Democrat — a war man. The draft is good. Force of arms is the only way. And the postscript is pure Alexander: "I am glad to see the copper heads don't like McClellan. It speaks well for him." He still admires McClellan personally but defines himself by the war, not by party.

The lime water for scurvy — "I don't mean lime such as they make mortar of, but limes, a fruit like lemons" — is Alexander explaining the exotic to his mother in Champlain, New York. The same instinct that described Atlanta's spires from a ridge now describes a citrus fruit.

$240 owed by the government — nearly a year of a sergeant's pay. The army fights on credit.
