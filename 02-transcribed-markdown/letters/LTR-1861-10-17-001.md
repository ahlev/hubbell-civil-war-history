## Full Transcription

> Oct. 17, 1861
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I received your letter while on picket. Very glad that you are all well at home. I have dreamt of being at home twice since I have been in Camp, but my dreams are not quite long enough for on wakening in the morning I find that I am not quite there yet. There is not a day that passes but what I think of you all. I know that matters and things can not go on as well at the farm as when I was there, as James is in school, but I shall be home one of these days.
>
> Rich has not returned yet. We expect him tonight.
>
> A sad occurrence happened here the other night about twelve o'clock. A man was stabbed by another person that was drunk. He lived till morning. Rum is not allowed in Camp. If any one is found with it he is severely punished. I don't know how that man came to get it, but the gallows will not be to hard for him.
>
> Brigadier General Gormon was here today to inspect our Regt. of there knapsacks and everything we got from the State. We made a very fine appearance. Our Regt. is thought a good deal of by him. I see in the papers we are called the sharp shooters. Quite flattering, so the boys think.
>
> One of those prisoners that was taken by the rebels in that skirmish when Kellogg was taken, has returned. He had two guards placed over him. He managed his escape very cunningly. He pretended that he was sick for some time. They gave him opium every day. When he got enough of it he drugged the guard's coffee and thus got away. He had to pass through three Regts. of Rebels. Said he was very well treated while prisoner. They told him they were going to put him in a cage and take him around for a show, but he outwited them. He said that Kellogg is safe in Richmond a prisoner, was not wounded. This man that escaped had a ball glance by his face the time he was taken.
>
> The report is now that our Regt. will go to Poolsville for winter, to be there as guards. Shall probably go before a great while. Do not know how soon. There is nothing more to write at present. Please write often.
>
> I remain your aff. Son,
>
> Henry

## Editorial Notes

This letter is dense with significant events. The POW escape story — feigning illness to accumulate opium doses, then drugging the guard's coffee — is the most dramatic narrative in the collection after the Bull Run letters. It also confirms Kellogg's status as a prisoner in Richmond, linking back to the skirmish mentioned in the Sept 27 letter. The stabbing murder by a drunk soldier (the second violent death Henry has reported, after the accidental shooting in Sept) shows the dark side of camp life. Brig. Gen. Gormon's inspection and the "sharp shooters" newspaper nickname suggest the 34th NY was well-regarded. Henry's comment about the farm struggling with him gone and James in school reveals the family's economic reality — this was a working farm that needed hands.
