## Full Transcription

> May 16, 1863
> Aquia Landing
>
> Dear Charley:
>
> I received your letter of the 3rd just after we returned from the front and was pleased to hear of your safety. I feared you had been obliged to leave Alexandria as it was reported here that the army had received strong reinforcements from Wash. and Alexandria. I hope you may never be called away from there.
>
> Our reg't. was pretty roughly handled at Chancellorsville, May 3rd. We lost seventy-three, nine killed, forty three wounded and twenty-one missing. Co. H lost six men. Lester Bond (Co. B) was shot through the forehead and instantly killed while the reg't. was making a bayonet charge. Our Orderly was sent Fittingly a few feet in the air, by a rebel shell, but without doing him much damage. The reg't. charged three times upon the rebels, driving them back each time in great disorder. The eleventh corps broke and ran in a most cowardly manner and let the brunt come upon the twelfth. At one time, the 60th and 102nd N.Y. were left unsupported by the rest of the troops and for a long time held the enemy in check.
>
> I received a letter from home today. They are all well. When I sent my money home I asked Mother to send me a watch. She tried to get one, but Scott had none much as I sent. Mother says she received a letter from you saying that you could get one, but you see that will do no good. I have my money home and so have you. (I sent forty-three dollars and ninety cents). The watch I used very much as you well know, and I must have it. Will you, Charley, if you have but two stamps, write Mother to send the watch without delay. Now Charley, if you do this, you will do me the greatest favor imaginable. Send it as soon as you can, for I need it very, very, very much. Give my regards to all the boys from Champlain.
>
> Your aff. Brother,
>
> Alexander F. Hubbell
>
> P.S. We are camping in the same place as when you were here.
>
> ..... 30 .....
>
> April 13th — I could not mail this letter for want of stamps. About that watch. I wish you would send it as soon as possible. You had better send it by express. The mesh of one gloves me sometimes in a very awkward position. When on guard duty I am obliged to guess at the time my reliefs have been on post. My portfolio with my papers, pens, postage stamps, etc., etc. (on acct of my being on duty) Luther carried in his knapsack were lost in the late battle, owing to the fact that Luther was obliged to throw away his knapsack.
>
> It is reported that the rebels are in full retreat. Our corps suffered severely in the late battles. A large number of reg'ts. have gone and are about going home so that the 10th A.C. is considerably reduced in numbers. If there is truth in the report that Hooker has crossed the river (and I think there is) I think the reason that we are left behind is to keep open the avenue of supplies to the army — though we may be ordered on any moment.
>
> I will endeavor to write soon. My regards to all friends.
>
> Monday Morn. — May 14th —— The rebels commenced shelling the hospitals and the supply and ammunition trains, but did not keep it up long for our cavalry made a charge and captured the two rebel guns (breach loaders). I think I have great reason to thank God for my safe preservation. The same day the trains were moved farther back from the river.
>
> Please let the Capt. know the loss of the company — L. Bond, killed; G. Ploof, wounded; H. Myers, wounded; P. Brookway, ditto; I. Stone, missing; B. Brame, Orderly Sergeant Brookway is still with the company. Luther was uninjured.
>
> Your aff. son,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

A letter written in fragments over several days, during and after the Chancellorsville aftermath. The casualty list is clinical — names, dispositions — but behind each entry is a man from Champlain or the surrounding towns. Bond's death is detailed (forehead, bayonet charge); the rest get single words (wounded, missing). The watch obsession running through the letter is almost comic against the backdrop of shelling and cavalry charges — Alexander needs to know the time to relieve his guards, and without a watch he's guessing.
