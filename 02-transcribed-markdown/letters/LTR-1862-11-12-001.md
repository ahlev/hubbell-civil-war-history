## Full Transcription

> Nov. 12th, 1862
> Harpers Ferry
>
> My Dear Mother:
>
> It is a long, long time since I last wrote you. I know you must feel very anxious about me, but I do not think you will blame me when you know all. A day or two after I wrote you my last letter we marched shifted our Camp and continued changing until at length we settled down on Bolivar Heights. I have been indisposed for some time and there my indisposition took a permanent form in the shape of Jaundice. I had very little disposition to write and did not wish to until I had something more favorable to write about concerning myself.
>
> We left the Heights a day or two to guard Government property in this place where we now are. To "cap the Climax" I took cold and had my face break out in a way not to be envied. I suffered a great deal with my eyes and was sometimes afraid I should lose them, but they are better now though still very weak. I will write you a good long letter as soon as my eyes are stronger. I received your welcome letter of the 4th inst. last night. Will try and write soon. Capt. Ransom is very, very kind. Love to all.
>
> Your affectionate son,
> Alex. F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

A letter held together by willpower. Alexander has been so sick he couldn't write for six weeks, and even now his eyes are barely functional. The cascade of illnesses — diarrhea → jaundice → cold → facial eruption → near-blindness — is the body breaking down under sustained deprivation. He says nothing about Henry, which is notable: either he has no new information, or his eyes won't let him write more, or he doesn't want to address it in a short letter. The promise of "a good long letter as soon as my eyes are stronger" signals that he has much to say. That letter arrives a week later (Nov 19) and contains the extraordinary Henry meeting flashback.
