## Full Transcription

> Sept. 9, 1862
> U.S. Barracks
> Plattsburgh, N.Y.
>
> Dear Mother —
>
> As Mr. Averill leaves soon I will send a few lines by him. I was examined by Dr. Foote yesterday & again today. That is my eyes were, nothing else. Some of the fellows were told to strip, but quick as I stood up he caught hold of my eyes & asked me if I did not have something on my eye. I told him no, but that I was a little near sighted. He then told me to look out of the window on the lake shore & tell him what I saw. He was satisfied that I was near sighted. Mr. Averill talked to him, told him that I had two brothers in the Army & one at West Point & that he must pass me. He said he would examine again. This afternoon I was sent for to see the Dr. He told me his instructions were perfect vision. Mr. Averill told him to try me again again. The doctor told me to look out of the window. I did & the doctor said he would sign my papers as I could see pretty well. Mr. Averill told me I was too honest, to tell the Dr. that I was near sighted.
>
> Sept. 15 — I think I had better finish this letter. We have drilled every day since we have been here except the first day & Sunday. I have been to P. village twice. Attended Mr. Youngs church Sunday morning. Pet Stacia & I were shown to the front slip near the door. The Pulpit is between the doors, the seats all facing the doors. We had a fine view of the persons coming into the church — I guess there are about 150 men here now. Mr. Armstrong has appointed me 1st Corporal. I don't know as it is made permanent yet, but I suppose it will be. A Corporal has this advantage, he does not have to stand guard but merely to change the guard, etc. We Non Commissioned officers drill by ourselves & need not drill with the company unless we wish. The drill sargent keeps us ahead of the Co. as we are to drill squads of the men. I have not been on duty yet but expect to be on tomorrow.
>
> The stone barrack is a very comfortable building. There are fireplaces to the different rooms in which we have fires. The bunks are made of rough boards, are broad enough for two to sleep in & are 4 bunks high. The day of our arrival we were told to fill our ticks with straw in the barn. Have two blankets, sleep very well.
>
> We have plenty of pork (boiled), some salt beef, Potatoes, bakers bread, beans. Sometimes, Pork hash & bean soup. This is our diet. Not all of these in one day though.
>
> I have had a good time enough since I have been here. Nearly every man swears. There are a good many hard cases here. There are quite a number of decent men here also.
>
> Have you heard from the boys? I should like to hear about them. How does Frances get along with her French class? How does the farming come on. I don't know when I can have a furlough as we are wanted to drill. When I do get one it can be only for a short one. I wish you would send me an undershirt & shirt by any one coming here. This is all I need. I can get my washing done here.
>
> Affectionate Son
>
> C. F. Hubbell.

## Editorial Notes

Charles F. Hubbell enters the collection with a letter that perfectly captures his personality: methodical, honest, unadorned. The eye exam scene is wonderful — Dr. Foote catches his near-sightedness immediately; Mr. Averill lobbies hard ("he has two brothers in the Army and one at West Point — he must pass me"); Charles admits his weakness honestly; the doctor relents. Averill's verdict: "I was too honest." This is recruitment in 1862: the army needs bodies, a connected advocate helps, and a doctor bends the rules. The Plattsburgh barracks are practically home — Charles goes to Mr. Youngs church with Pet Stacia, asks about the farming. The 1st Corporal appointment comes fast (within a week), suggesting either prior reputation or visible competence. His food list and barracks description are the most detailed domestic account in the collection — four-high bunks, straw ticks, boiled pork and bean soup. "Nearly every man swears. There are a good many hard cases" — but also "quite a number of decent men." Charles sees the world in steady categories. He is the quiet brother.
