## Full Transcription

> January 1st, 1862
> Baltimore
>
> A happy New Year to you all:
>
> The holidays are great days down here. The Baltimoreans made quite a racket last night. I was on guard duty and heard it all. I just write this to let you know that I am in the land of the living. I am enjoying excellent health and spirits. I received my warrant today. Each non-commissioned is obliged to have one of these. It is the same to them as a commissioned officers Commission. Mine runs in this wise "Know Ye That reposing special trust and confidence in the patriotism, valor, fidelity and abilities of Alexander F. Hubbell I do hereby appoint him, etc."
>
> I Have received all the letters and papers you have sent me I think. Those postage stamps were especially welcome. Now you must excuse this note. I feel really ashamed of it.
>
> I again wish you a happy New Year. I do not feel very bright after my yesterdays guard duty. Ask Mr. Copeland to write me. I feel almost afraid to write him first. I promise I will answer promptly. I should like to hear from my Minister very much. Please excuse this.
>
> Yours affectionately,
>
> Alexander

## Editorial Notes

A brief, tired New Year's Day note — Alexander was on guard duty the night before and "does not feel very bright." The NCO warrant language is a valuable primary source, showing the quasi-official dignity of the corporal's appointment. The asymmetry between the grandiose commission language ("reposing special trust and confidence in the patriotism, valor, fidelity and abilities") and the exhausted young man writing a note he's "really ashamed of" captures something essential about the gap between military ceremony and daily experience.
