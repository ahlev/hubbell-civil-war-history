## Full Transcription

> Oct. 3, 1861
> Camp Wheeler
> Ogdensburgh
>
> My dear Mother:
>
> Our uniforms will be here Saturday and the Captain has promised me a furlough as soon as I get mine. Will you be so kind as to get a pass for me from Mr. Hoyle as it would cost me three dollars to go and come and I can not afford that. If you can get it please send it immediately. I am enjoying excellent health.
>
> Your affectionate son
>
> Alex F. Hubbell
>
> P.S. Please to send the enclosed letter to Henry as I have probably hardly enough money to pay my washing bill. I shall soon have a dollar or two from the State.

## Editorial Notes

A short, practical letter revealing Alexander's financial situation — nearly broke, unable to afford the $3 fare home, routing a letter to Henry through Mother to save postage. The promise of a furlough upon receiving uniforms suggests he's been in camp about two weeks without one. Mr. Hoyle likely provides railroad passes — Ogdensburg to Champlain would require travel along the rail line.
