## Full Transcription

> Dec. 12, 1864
> Camp near Newtown, Va.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I wrote you a letter two days since requesting you to send me a pair of boots. Write now to say that I do not want the boots as I bought a pair of government boots last night. My letter of the 10th contained fifty dollars. Please acknowledge as soon as possible.
>
> The law allows four pounds of woolen or cotton goods to be sent by mail. I would like a pair of mittens — mittens with one finger are the best, but given it is hardly worth while to send any. I have a good pair of boots and paid only five dollars for them. Sutlers are selling boots from ten to fifteen dollars. My boots are cavalry boots.
>
> There is about six inches of snow on the ground and it is very cold. A hard west wind is blowing and the snow is drifting. The weather is the cause of our being here now. Expect to move soon, down to the railroad and guarding that will be our duty. Must close as the mail leaves soon.
>
> From your Aff. Son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell
