## Full Transcription

> February 4th, 1862
> Camp McClellan
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I received a letter from you, brought by Mr. Hoyle. We are having very pleasant weather now. We have had a slight fall of snow, but it is almost all off. I think the heart of the winter is broke here. Wood is very scarce in and around our camp.
>
> I have collected most of the money for the stove, all except $1.00. I shall get that next pay day. In regard to the Sutler's: I mean to keep clear of them the next two months and come out with $26.00 on next pay day. I can get any quantity of beer or Cider, but as I have promised not to drink I shall not touch it unless it is a glass of cider now and then for my health. I have had my picture taken twice. I do not know as you will call the one that is taken standing very good. The other one you may wish to know who it is that is with me. It is one of my tent-mates. His name is Graves. The two pictures cost $2.25. I think it would be a good plan to have them framed if it would not cost to much.
>
> Feb. 6th: I have delayed mailing this letter, expecting to receive an answer from the money that I sent, $15.00. I have nothing more to write at present. One of our Co. has deserted. He got into a drunken scrape and was put into the guard house and from there ran away. This makes two that has deserted. I forgot to mention that one ran away when the Capt. was in Champlain. He went out to cut wood, and has not returned yet. There is a good deal of gambling going on in this Regt., and some in our Co. but I keep away from it. I have not learned how to play cards yet. There is also lottery going on in our Co. for watches, but I keep away from them all.
>
> Today is quite mild, but muddy. We shall not probably move till the roads are better. Nothing more at present. Please excuse this writing, as I have lost all my pens. Write often.
>
> I remain your aff. Son,
>
> Henry

## Editorial Notes

The Feb 6 date on this letter creates a timeline problem: Henry writes from Camp McClellan on Feb 6 saying they won't move till roads improve, but the next two letters (to Frances and Charles) are dated Feb 5 from Bolivar Heights/Harpers Ferry. Either: (1) the Feb 6 addendum was actually written before the sudden march orders, (2) the Camp McClellan letter was actually written earlier than Feb 4, or (3) one set of dates is a transcription error. The most likely explanation: the Feb 5 Harpers Ferry letters may actually be from a later date (perhaps Feb 25?), or the Camp McClellan letter's "Feb 6" addendum was actually written earlier. This will be flagged as a ⚠ dating discrepancy requiring Tier-2 verification.
