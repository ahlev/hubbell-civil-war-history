## Full Transcription

> August 16, 1863
> Capitol Hill Barracks
> Washington, D.C.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> It is a long time since I wrote you and the reason is we are all very busy. We are on guard from one to two days at a time. Still I might write while on guard if I could carry the writing materials with me. I have received no letter from you for a long time also, but I suppose I can't say anything as I have not written you.
>
> Our Reg't. guards two prisons and has a guard at the R.R. Depot, also has a camp guard, and besides a large number of men go down to the provost marshals every night and sleep there on the floor to be ready if wanted. The provost marshal's is over a mile from here. We have a good many men go every day to the city hall where the draft has been taken place and they are now examining the drafted men or their substitutes. Most of them get substitutes. Every night the men that the examining board are listed under a strong guard and taken to a barracks that is strongly guarded. A good many citizens are caught at the R.R. Depot trying to run away from the draft. Yesterday they arrested, while I was at the depot, a young fellow I should think about nineteen years old. He was dressed as a captain, only his coat was too large for him. He may have stolen or bought the coat. I have not heard how he came out.
>
> I received a letter from Mary McNeill a few days since. Guess I will answer this letter soon. I waited so long before answering the other that I thought she would not answer it. Have not heard from Alex yet; wish he would write. Have you heard from him lately; if you have I will write to him again. I think James is not getting along so badly as you said in your last letter. I expect a letter from him soon. Did you receive that $20.00 check that I sent you two or three weeks since by mail? You said nothing about it in your last letter. As we can get plenty of reading here you need not send me the Observer; send only the Tribune once in a while.
>
> They are now giving furloughs in this reg't. I think I told you that James Lucas was reduced to the ranks and loses a month's pay for being concerned in stealing rations. A corporal here has an easier time than a private, as he only changes the guard, and does not stand guard. Also he does not come on duty as often as a private. Capt. Burdick has resigned and is going home very soon, I think. Every day a small quantity of whiskey is given to each man that wants it. Some kind of medicine is in it; I think it is quinine. They only commenced giving it out a day or two ago. All in the Co. that want it are marched to the hospital to get it from an open window. Some would drink the whiskey, no matter what was in it, for the sake of the spirits. I write in haste so I must close.
>
> From your Aff. Son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell
>
> P.S. Please write soon.
