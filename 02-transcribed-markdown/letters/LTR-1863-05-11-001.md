## Full Transcription

> May 11, 1863
> Aquia Landing, Va.
>
> Dear Mother and Sister:
>
> I presume you have long ere this heard of the battle of Chancellorsville in which my regiment was employed. I am sorry to say I was not in the battle, being in command of the guard at the Headquarters trains of the 12th Brigade. I am now relieved from that duty and am again with the reg't. The exact loss of the reg't. is seventy three — nine killed, forty three wounded, and twenty-one missing. Company H lost six men — one killed dead, Lester Bond. He was shot through the forehead and instantly killed while the reg't. was making a bayonet charge. Our orderly was sent Fittingly a few feet in the air, by a rebel shell, but without doing him much damage. The reg't. charged three times upon the rebels, driving them back each time in great disorder. The eleventh corps broke and ran in a most cowardly manner and let the brunt come upon the twelfth. At one time, the 60th and 102nd N.Y. were left unsupported by the rest of the troops and for a long time held the enemy in check.
>
> I received a letter from home today. They are all well. When I sent my money home I asked Mother to send me a watch. She tried to get one, but Scott had none much as I sent. Mother says she received a letter from you saying that you could get one, but we have no money home and so have you. (I sent forty-three dollars and ninety cents). The watch I used very much as you well know, and I must have it. Will you, Charley, if you have but two stamps, write Mother to send the watch without delay. Now Charley, if you do this, you will do me the greatest favor imaginable. Send it as soon as you
>
> [page break]
>
> can, for I need it very, very, very much. Give my regards to all the boys from Champlain.
>
> Your aff. Brother,
>
> Alexander F. Hubbell
>
> P.S. We are camping in the same place as when you were here.

## Significance Flags

### Historical Significance: `major`

> At Chancellorsville the 60th N.Y. lost seventy-three men — nine killed, forty-three wounded, twenty-one missing — yet Alexander writes that he missed the fight entirely, posted to guard the brigade trains while his regiment charged three times and drove the rebels back. Company H's one dead is named plainly: Lester Bond, shot through the forehead. He scorns the Eleventh Corps, which "broke and ran in a most cowardly manner," leaving the 60th unsupported.

### Emotional Intensity: `moderate`

> Alexander's frustration at missing the battle is palpable — "I am sorry to say I was not in the battle." The watch urgency is almost frantic — "very, very, very much."

## Editorial Notes

`★ Insight ─────────────────────────────────────`
The letter is actually written by Alexander to Charles (signed "Your aff. Brother"), not by Charles to Mother — the metadata label in the PDF header ("Dear Mother and Sister") is from a different letter on the same page. Alexander is writing to Charles asking him to relay a message about a watch. This is one of the few brother-to-brother letters in the collection.
`─────────────────────────────────────────────────`

Alexander's Chancellorsville report is notable for what he missed: he was on guard duty while his regiment charged three times. The man who refused to seek safety was kept from battle by a routine assignment. The 11th Corps rout — one of the most dramatic moments of the war, when Jackson's corps smashed through an unprepared flank — is dismissed in a sentence: "broke and ran in a most cowardly manner." The 60th NY's defense, unsupported after the rout, was genuinely heroic.
