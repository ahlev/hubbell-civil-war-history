## Full Transcription

> Aug. 26, 1864
> Camp near Harper's Ferry
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I received a letter from you last night dated Aug. 20th, was glad to hear from you, but am very sorry you are so anxious about James and me. James, I think, is all right. When around for a couple of days near Frederick James was sent to the hospital at that place, and Dr. Sweeney told me that he would send me as soon as he could but would not at that time very well. James was thin in flesh and had the diarrhea. If he has been very sick since then the doctors have made him so with their medicine. But I think James is having a good time in some hospital or convalescent camp — that is, a good time compared with marching. I have not heard from James nor have I written him. It is useless to be anxious about him because he has not written. You know, he doesn't seem to care about letter writing. Perhaps he is waiting till he rejoins the regt. As for myself I am well and have been nearly two weeks in the convalescent dept. As there are more clerks here than are necessary I may go back to the regt. any day, and then I may not. There is a good deal of work to do but then we live well. I have flatted in considerably since I have been here because I eat all the sugar I want, for one thing. You have been sending me recipes for the diarrhea, because I suppose as it is an army complaint you think I must have it. I have been troubled very little with it. I know a man with whom it has become chronic, who is a perfect skeleton. He has a good appetite and does duty all the time.
>
> I am sorry you wasted $5 telegraphing about James. Dr. Sweeney told me at the time James was sent to the hospital that he would be sent on to Washington probably, and as I did not know his address I have not written him. James had no chevrons on his coat and I guess in his paper for admission to the Hospital Corporal was not written. I hope you will not worry about us boys, for if there is anything the matter with us you will hear of it. You must call no news good news. I am entirely out of money and postage stamps. When you would send me a couple of dollars and some stamps. You direct the letters me right. I have recd 2 or 3 letters lately from you, also two newspapers. Have not written because I have not had the material or time. We have been on the move nearly all the time, have been here two days. Our Army is composed of the 19th and the 6th Corps — also the 8th — all under the command of General Sheridan who has a large force of cavalry with him. We have not had a sight of the rebs yet, although the cavalry has fought with them some; also a few of the infantry. Please write often. It is not convenient for me to write as I have no place to carry writing materials and not much time, but I will write as often as I can. Give my love to Frances. Also don't forget yourself.
>
> From your aff. Son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

Charles is in the convalescent department as a clerk, "flatted in considerably" on sugar and good food, while James is in a hospital at Frederick with diarrhea. Mother has spent $5 on a telegram about James — a significant sum — and Charles reassures her it was wasted. "Call no news good news."

Sheridan's army is forming around Harper's Ferry — the 19th, 6th, and 8th Corps with cavalry. The Shenandoah Valley campaign that will produce Third Winchester, Fisher's Hill, and Cedar Creek is about to begin. Charles and James are in it. Alexander is in a hospital near Atlanta with scurvy. Three brothers, three theaters, three hospitals.
