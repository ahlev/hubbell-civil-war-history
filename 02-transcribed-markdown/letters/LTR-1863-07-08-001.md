## Full Transcription

> July 8th, 1863
> Near Crampton's Gap, Md.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I wrote you immediately after the battle of Gettysburg (through which I came unscratched) but am somewhat afraid you may never get the letter and accordingly I now write you again to let you know of my welfare.
>
> We beat the rebels gloriously in that battle. The old 60th took two stands of colors from the enemy. We stretched seven rebel officers out in a row in front of the company. We will doubtless fight tomorrow or next day. My life is in God's hands. His will be done.
>
> We are only seven or eight miles from the Antietam battle ground. If possible, I shall visit it and endeavor to find out something about Henry. Love to all.
>
> Your Aff. Son,
>
> Alexander F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

A brief, electric letter. Alexander writes from Crampton's Gap — almost exactly where the army passed before Antietam — and the proximity to Henry's grave is on his mind. "If possible, I shall visit it and endeavor to find out something about Henry." Ten months after Henry's death, Alexander may finally stand at his brother's grave.

"We stretched seven rebel officers out in a row in front of the company." The language is blunt, almost boastful — the adrenaline of Gettysburg hasn't worn off. But the next sentence is pure fatalism: "We will doubtless fight tomorrow or next day. My life is in God's hands." Alexander has survived Antietam, Chancellorsville, and now Gettysburg. He knows the mathematics of survival.
