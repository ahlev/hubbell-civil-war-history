## Full Transcription

> Nov. 19, 1863
> Capitol Hill
> Washington, D.C.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I rec'd your letter of Nov. 7th yesterday and was glad to hear from you. Mr. Roberts from C. is here visiting his cousin. He says he is going to Meade's army and is to receive $48 a month in the photograph business.
>
> Capt. Rich has got back. I saw his last night at a distance. The other night all our reg't. not on duty were up all night at the depot here waiting for rebel prisoners. They came near morning, 1,846 of them, and our reg't., with some cavalry took them to the Old Capitol prison. Next day there was a heavy detail from our reg't. to take part of them to Point Lookout at the mouth of the Potomac. I was lucky not to be up that night nor yet to go off with the prisoners. A newspaper here said a few days since that all the troops around this place were to be relieved by the Invalid Corps. But for all that I guess we will still stay here this winter. A good many of the I. Corps are on duty in this city now. They are divided into two classes. One class carries rifles and the other carries a sword and colts revolver. Our Co. has gone to N.Y. York on 13 days leave of absence. Major Strain (or Strange) the command while he is gone.
>
> Those who went home to vote, want free, their fare being paid by the Union party of N.Y. I did not know this till too late. I rec'd. two pieces of cheese (it is very good), but have not seen any homeruns or shirts/sleevings; perhaps they are under the currents in the case. At any the apples were sent because our Co. draw from. Have you heard from James lately? How is he getting along and how did he dispose of all his photographs? I think James will get along well enough, but don't expect he will graduate very high. Has Hiram I. got his appointment yet?
>
> If you are offered what the house is worth I think it would be well to sell it. I am glad that you are going to settle with the Savages become the interest runs up fast. How do you direct Alex's letters? I have not written him since I saw him. Do you keep an acct. of the money he sends home? If not, I wish you would be particular about doing so. I sent that Goddard's letter to James, but forgot to tell him to return it to you. I think James will pass the coming examinations well enough and goes to the marks, he has only two months to be marked so he will fall short of 100 reports for all but the two months. You have his reports for all but the two months.
>
> That is Uncle John's visit for! Is it to see about hit share of the estate? Jim Lucas has just told me that Pet Steais is going to get his discharge. A lot of rebel officers from the old Capitol prison were sent to Sandusky, Ohio, two or three days since. I see by today's paper that there is a plot to free them and burn Buffalo and Ogdensburgh. But I guess it will not amount to much.
>
> Give my thanks to those that sent me socks, etc. But do you think it best to trouble so many people about me? Don't forget to take a good many thanks for yourself for that box. Those cookies were first rate and so was that pie.
>
> Please write often. Frances' letter was good, only it was short, but then she is not to blame as she has so much to do.
>
> From your aff. son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell

## Cross-Reference Notes

### Connections to Other Letters in Collection
- **Selling the house:** Charles advises selling if offered what it's worth. The family's Champlain property is becoming a burden — no male labor, farm rented out, Mother possibly leaving for Charlotte.
- **Photograph business:** Mr. Roberts heading to Meade's army as a photographer at $48/month — one of the itinerant photographers who documented the war.
- **Sandusky plot:** The Lake Erie conspiracy — Confederate agents attempted to free prisoners at Johnson's Island, Sandusky. Charles dismisses it: "it will not amount to much."

## Editorial Notes

Note: The dates of this letter (Nov. 19) and the previous letter (Nov. 20-23) overlap oddly in the PDF ordering. This may be a typist sequencing issue, or the Nov. 20 letter's opening may have been on a page that precedes where we pick it up.
