## Full Transcription

> Feb. 18, 1864
> Nashville, Tenn.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I wrote you a day or two at on from Louisville, Ky., but you recollect I promised to write you as often as possible — and as I have plenty of leisure at present, I don't know as I can better occupy it than by writing you. You agree with me perfectly, I know.
>
> We left Louisville early yesterday morning and reached here a little before dark last night. Strange to say, we had comfortable passenger cars instead of the cold freight cars we expected. To leave here at three this afternoon. I was out in the city this morning on a pass. I went to hospital No. 8 and saw one of our wounded boys, Thos. Small, who lost his leg at Ringgold. He was doing well and seemed very cheerful.
>
> Luther has spent the greater part of his state bounty — and I don't see as he is any the better for it. Mr. Marshall Dunn said the state bounty yet "please give him five dollars as a reward for his services." He allows the boys to give him what they will. All who have received the bounty have given him five dollars and I would not be "behind hand."
>
> How does Frances and her school flourish? Mrs. Rider and Miss Riley? Have Frances, Miss Averill, and others been down in the ravine to have a slide yet? Tell Frances I shall endeavor to write her soon. All the boys from Champlain are well. Direct your letters in future to Nashville, Tenn., Army of the Cumberland. They will reach me if you direct to Washington, but will come sooner, I think, by directing the other way, to Nashville. Love to all.
>
> Your aff. son,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell
