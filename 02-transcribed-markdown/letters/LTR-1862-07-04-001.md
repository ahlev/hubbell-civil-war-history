## Full Transcription

> July 4th, 1862
> Camp Goodrich
> Near Strausburgh, Va.
>
> My Dear Mother:
>
> I received a letter from you a day or two since and was very much disappointed not to hear more definitely from James. I feel very anxious about him. I hope and believe that he will succeed. Please write and let me know as soon as possible.
>
> I received the letter you directed to the 78th Regt. Now, Mother, I shall keep you posted as far as possible as to my whereabouts, ie., ie. and you must not believe everything every old fogy tells you, for they will surely lead you astray. We have had orders to be ready to move at a moment's notice, and I think we shall leave tomorrow. Where we are to go to, I do not know. Our Orderly said to me that he thought we should go to Richmond and I am pretty near of the same opinion. "At any rate" we are to go somewhere, and a prettly long march to.
>
> In the way of celebrating the "fourth" all the batteries have made considerable noise and the brigade marched down to the camp of the first and were harrangued by Gen. Cooper.
>
> Please write often — very often.
>
> Your aff. son
>
> Alexander

## Editorial Notes

A brief Fourth of July letter with orders to march. Alexander's Orderly's prediction of Richmond would prove wrong — the 60th NY would head toward Second Bull Run, not Richmond. Gen. Cooper's harangue to the brigade on Independence Day was standard wartime morale-building. Alexander's frustration with Mother directing mail to the 78th Regiment ("don't believe everything every old fogy tells you") shows the confusion of families trying to track soldiers across a shifting war.
