## Full Transcription

> July 27th, 1865
> Camp Wheeler
> Ogdensburgh, N.Y.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> We reached here last Sat. and have been waiting for the Paymaster ever since. He has not come yet and I have some doubts whether I shall be able to be home this week. I am very anxious to see you all again. Luther will be down with me and spend a short time at "our house." I got this paper from one of the boys who I guess does not like writing much.
>
> Love to all,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell
>
> P.S. We had a grand reception. I will tell you all about it at the tea table.

## Editorial Notes

This is Alexander's last letter. After nearly four years — from September 20, 1861 to July 27, 1865 — the boy who enlisted at 17 writes his final words from the same town where he first put on the uniform. Camp Wheeler, Ogdensburgh. Waiting for the Paymaster.

The letter is the shortest he ever wrote. The man who described Chancellorsville's "howling wilderness," who wrote a valedictory about treason walking the halls of Congress, who catalogued every battle and every march — has nothing left to say on paper. "I will tell you all about it at the tea table."

The stationery is a printed poem called "Soldier's Letter" — borrowed from a boy who doesn't like writing. Alexander, who loved writing, doesn't need it anymore. He's going home.
