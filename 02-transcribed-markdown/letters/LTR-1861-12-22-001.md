## Full Transcription

> Dec. 22, 1861
> Baltimore
>
> My dear Brother:
>
> You perceive by the heading of my letter that we have moved camp. Colonel Hayward being dissatisfied with the conduct of Company C and as Company H had the reputation of being the best, or at least one of the best "companies" in the Regt. he ordered us to take their place. We are quartered in a three story brick building on the outskirts of the City and are guarding a branch railway about three miles in length running from the main road (Baltimore and Ohio) to another end of the city. The depot is called the Camden Station. Luther and I have a first rate room. Five others "tent" with us. One of us are singing — others writing. We make music. It is almost time to get supper. Now I suppose you would like to know how we will go to work to "get it". Well, in the first place we fill our "mess pan" with water. In the next place we put it on the stove (we have the Captains), in the third we put some tea or coffe in it and let it boil, and in the fourth place we put in some sugar and drink it. We sometimes cook beef or port. One of our men says he will get some cabbage tonight for soup tomorrow.
>
> Now, Master James, you must excuse this wandering, disconnected letter. We have been singing and sometimes when I was in the midst of a sentence I would "pitch in". You know that two things can not be done at once and be done well. Don't you think my excuse good — eh? I have been holding an indignation meeting with myself. Why don't you all write me more often. I — a solider — with barely any conveniences write twice to your once. Now — I feel really neglected — yes, neglected. Ask Mother and Frances and Charles and yourself if you have forgotten me. I received two papers yesterday. Don't forget to send some postage stamps. I have risen three grades. When I left the North I was seventh Corporal. I am now fourth. Luther has also risen three grades. He was eighth and is now fifth. Love to all.
>
> Your affectionate Brother,
>
> Alexander
>
> P.S. Has Mother received that fifteen dollars I sent her and my miniature?

## Editorial Notes

This is a pivotal letter: Alexander's company has been singled out as the best (or one of the best) in the regiment and moved to Baltimore proper to guard Camden Station — one of the most strategically significant railroad depots in the border state theater. The April 19, 1861 Baltimore riot had occurred at this very station months before Alexander arrived. The promotion from 7th to 4th Corporal in roughly three months shows Alexander's rapid advancement, and Luther rising in parallel suggests they were recognized as a pair. The domestic scene — seven men in a brick room, singing, writing, making tea on the Captain's stove — is the warmest camp-life description in the collection. Alexander's complaint about being neglected ("I — a soldier — with barely any conveniences write twice to your once") is playful but real — a recurring theme across his letters.
