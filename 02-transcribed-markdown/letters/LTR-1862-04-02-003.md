## Full Transcription

> April 2, 1862
> Hampton, Va.
>
> Dear Brother Charles:
>
> I received your letter and was very glad to hear from you. It is some time since I have written to you. The weather here is quite mild. We are in camp in a large field — green grass all around here. The nights are very cold. There are about twentyfive thousand troops in Camp around here with one brigade. We had rather a crowded time coming on the steam-boat. We were two days and half on the boat. Our steamer ran aground by mistake coming ashore so that we had to go on to another boat to get ashore. The rebels leave their tracks every where they go. They will do all the damage they can if nothing more. I suppose we will keep advancing all the while till the rebels get tired of us. Then I hope the war will close. To tell the truth I would rather be making sugar this Spring than soldiering. I am not the only one, but I shall make the best of every thing.
>
> You wished to know whether we have newspapers. We can have them by paying the small sum of five cents which I do not happen to have. If our Regt. ever comes to bayonet charge, all I have got to say is that I pity the rebels. We have drilled in the bayonet exercise a good deal last Summer. I understand it very well now. Today is very cloudy and rather chilley. I suppose the weather is pretty cold up home. I would not let out the sugar bush unless you can have an honest man. Write all the news.
>
> I remain your aff. Brother
>
> Henry
>
> P.S. If it would be convenient could you send me a fine comb in a letter. I cannot have a chance to buy one here.

## Editorial Notes

Henry wrote three letters on April 2 — to Mother, Mother again (carried by Cooper), and Charles. Each contains slightly different details: the Charles letter adds the steamer running aground, the 5¢ newspaper cost, and the fine comb request (a euphemism for lice management). His sugar-making homesickness is the most persistent domestic yearning in Henry's letters — the maple syrup season was the defining spring ritual of Clinton County farm life.
