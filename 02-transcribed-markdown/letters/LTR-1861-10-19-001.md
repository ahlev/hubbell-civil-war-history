## Full Transcription

> Oct. 19, 1861
>
> Dear Francis:
>
> I am very much oblige for the portfolio that you sent me, and also for the paper and pens, pen holder and ink. I would not have believed that you made it had you not written about it. Very nicely made. I thought it came form the store. Was nice as could be. A good many of the boys have them here, but thy won't come up to the one you made. I am writing on it now. Find it very convenient — more so to keep my paper in and letters. I shall enjoy reading the papers very much. I will give Brink that paper as soon as I can get a chance. He has left from where he was formerly. I do not remember the name of the place he has gone to.
>
> I have not much more to write. I hope to get your picture one of these days, the sooner the better. Perhaps I shall get mine taken one of these days better than the other one. I wish I was going to be at home when you have company and could try my voice in singing. I shall be one of these days. Please write often.
>
> Your aff. Brother, Henry.
>
> P.S. I got a letter from Nellie with a promise of her picture.

### Historical Significance: `routine`

## Editorial Notes

A short, warm letter mostly about the handmade portfolio Frances sent. Henry's genuine surprise and delight ("I would not have believed that you made it... I thought it came form the store") is touching. The mention of Nellie — who writes separately and promises her picture — suggests a romantic interest. The Brink connection may link back to Mrs. Brink/Brinkhoff mentioned in Alexander's and Henry's earlier letters, suggesting the Brinks are a family with connections both at home and in the army.
