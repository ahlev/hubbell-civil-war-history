## Full Transcription

> July 8th, 1862
> Fort Royal, Va.
>
> My Dear Mother—
>
> Here I am safe and sound. We left Camp Goodrich July 5th at 5 P.M. and reached here at 10 A.M. the next day. We were greatly delayed on the way by the baggage trains of the troops in advance. We leave tomorrow morning at 4 oclock for Manassas Gap. I hope we are going to Richmond and think we are.
>
> Please write often. I feel very anxious to hear from James. I hope he will write me, but I am afraid. He is a poor correspondent. I think a little knapsack drill would do him good. Tell Charles to write. I don't know but I owe him a letter. I have but little opportunity of writing. When will Frances return? I hope she will write me a good long letter. I have had nothing from her but a little scribble on a little scrap of paper for a long time. I shall have to pull her ears when I return. How I should like to see you all, but then, I do not wish to return until this war is over. I hope James will succeed and then we shall have one educated soldier in the family.
>
> Write soon. Remember me to Mrs. B.
>
> Your affectionate son
>
> Alex F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

"Fort Royal" is Alexander's spelling of Front Royal, at the northern entrance to the Shenandoah Valley. The march through Manassas Gap — the pass through the Blue Ridge Mountains — marks the 60th NY's transition from Valley operations to the eastern theater. Alexander still thinks they're heading for Richmond; they're actually heading toward the Second Bull Run campaign. His phrase "one educated soldier in the family" captures the siblings' collective investment in James's West Point appointment — the whole family has contributed money and encouragement. Alexander's teasing about Frances's short letters and threatening to "pull her ears" is the most lighthearted passage in his recent correspondence.
