## Full Transcription

> April 2, 1862
> Hampton — Near Fortress Monroe
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> As I expect Henry Cooper leaves in the morning, I thought I would write a few lines to let you know that I am well. I am writing this letter by candle light, as it is near time for roll call. I cannot write much, or but a few lines. As I am writing I can hear the tree toads. It makes me think of home very much.
>
> 3rd. Today is quite mild, we shall have very warm weather before long. I cannot turn around, or go three or four miles but what there are soldiers. I expect we shall make an onward movement towards Richmond before long. Wood is very scarce around here. The rebels have made a clean sweep of everything. Our Regt. will have a good name if they only keep on as they have. Yesterday a peddler came into camp, but he was no sooner in sight, when he was stripped of everything he had — a barrel of pies taken besides a barrel of apples. All those that were in the scrape have been put under arrest. I have nothing more to write this time.
>
> I remain your Aff. Son,
>
> Henry.
>
> P.S. I have been keeping a Journal some time which I forgot to mention. I shall copy it off one of these days and send it home. C. Regt. leaves this place tomorrow morning for York Town, twenty-four miles from here. I expect we shall march it on foot. I shall hand this letter to Cooper. I expect he will mail it in Washington.
