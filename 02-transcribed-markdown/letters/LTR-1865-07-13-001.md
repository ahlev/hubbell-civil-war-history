## Full Transcription

> July 13th, 1865
> Savannah, Ga.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> The last letter we received from you said, that at Uncle Henry's request you were going to Charlotte. Perhaps that is the reason we have not received my letter or papers from you lately.
>
> I was sorry to hear that you were not in very good health. Hope that it has improved. James & I are in good health. James says that since we came here it troubles him to see at night, but he can see as well as usual in the day time. Since I last wrote you we have moved into the city near the river. We have a good place for a camp. Are now living in board shanties, four men in each. Our Regt. was paid not long since & James & I sent you by express one hundred & thirty dollars. Martin sent seventy dollars with us, so that the express charges would be less. Please let us know whether you have recd. the money. Since any days a good many have been drunk. A man in our Regt. while drunk one night, messed clothes with a woman and then walked down street with her. There were met by some soldiers who after pounding them, robbed each of them.
>
> You speak of hearing from us in Washington. Who did you hear by. Was it Capt. Harry Hummel? There is more business done here now than when we came here. Considerably cotton is being riled and carted. A wagon loaded with cotton came into the city this morning. The driver told me that they had come one hundred & sixty miles with it.
>
> We take a swim in the river quite often. The tide here rises ten or fifteen feet high. The weather is very hot. I nearly melted in church last Sunday. July 4th there was a review of all the troops here. On the Fourth there was a dress parade of each Regt. at eight in the morning, also firing of cannon & ringing of bells. There was a parade of all the fire companies, all black but one. The darkeys young and old all turned out. Yesterday Dr. John Sweeney called on us. He & his brother James are in charge of the Division Hospital. John graduated at the medical school last winter with Ranch Malcolm. Ranch has been assistant surgeon but is not now as his Regt. is discharged. John inquired about you & Frances.
>
> You speak of tomatoes & green corn. I should like to be home to eat some of them, but guess I will not have a chance. He may not be home before our time is out & then we may — it is hard to tell.
>
> I have been quite negligent about writing, but will try & do better. We have a good deal of duty to do. James is on duty so can't write. Please write often & all the news.
>
> From your Aff. Son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

The first peacetime Fourth of July — and in Savannah, Georgia, the parade is led by all-black fire companies. "The darkeys young and old all turned out." Charles reports it without comment, but the image is extraordinary: freed people celebrating independence in a former Confederate city while Union soldiers stand at review. The war's purpose made visible.

James's night blindness has followed him from the Shenandoah Valley to Savannah. The vitamin deficiency that blinded Alexander in Chattanooga now affects the youngest brother. The war damages bodies long after the shooting stops.
