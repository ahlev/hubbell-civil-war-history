## Full Transcription

> Dec. 5th, 1864
> Nashville, Tenn.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> You perceive I am again in Nashville. We left Chattanooga the same day I wrote you my last letter and were two days in getting to this place. We were obliged to move very cautiously as the telegraph wires had been cut and we were not sure but the enemy were in force upon the road. However, we came through without accident. We have been hard at work for the last few days building breastworks, etc., etc. Hood is now in front of the city. No heavy fighting has yet occurred here. Skirmishing and artillery firing is tolerably brisk. The troops here have been and are still hard at work strengthening the fortifications around the city, and I am thinking that Gen. Hood of the bogus Confederacy will have to whistle for his supper so far as getting it out of the storehouse of Nashville is concerned.
>
> I hope it will not be long before I can get to the regiment. I do not like my place here. The officers drink altogether too much whiskey. Fort Negley, close by, is now using its heavy guns upon the rebs.
>
> Please write as often and direct to A. F. Hubbell, Nashville, Tenn. Do not put on economy and rest. Our present duty comes pretty hard on me, but I will soon get inured to it again I expect.
>
> Love to all. Give my regards to Mrs. B., and Grandmothers family. Tell Frances I want to hear all about the "institute" and if she "knows a great deal more" since she attended. Remember me to James and Charles.
>
> Your aff. Son,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

Alexander is in Nashville for Hood's siege — building breastworks, hearing Fort Negley's guns, watching the army prepare to destroy the last major Confederate force in Tennessee. "Hood of the bogus Confederacy will have to whistle for his supper" — Alexander's confidence is absolute. The Battle of Nashville (December 15-16) will prove him right, shattering Hood's army.

"The officers drink altogether too much whiskey" — the teetotaler's complaint. Alexander, who traded his whiskey ration for hard crackers at Dallas, is surrounded by drinking officers. He doesn't like his place. He wants his regiment.
