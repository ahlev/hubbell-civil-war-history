## Full Transcription

> May 19, 1863
> Alexandria, Va.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I was very much gratified yesterday to receive a letter from Alexander. Have been anxiously expecting a letter from either you or him for some time. He has probably written you that he came safely out of that fight and is back in his old camp. In his letter he makes me to write to you for the money to buy a watch. I will buy him a watch and either take it to him or send it by express. Wish you would send me $11.20 of Alex's money by mail; greenbacks if you can get them, for a watch for him. I hope Alex did not keep you in suspense as long as he kept me about his safety. Don't know why he did not write to me sooner.
>
> Our Lieut. has just made a new law for our Co. It is this: Men absent from roll call to be drilled by the corporal on duty next day. The man, or son,
>
> [page break]
>
> can, for I need it very, very, very much. Give my regards to all the boys from Champlain.
>
> Your aff. Brother,
>
> Alexander F. Hubbell
>
> P.S. We are camping in the same place as when you were here.
>
> Albert Cook was here while I was gone to Aquia C. Am sorry that I did not see him. Burdick is back and is promoted to Captain. Another Capt. is now Lt. Col. and Capt. Strain is Major and the Major is Col.
>
> We expect a cavalry raid in this city. Today is Sunday and all the n****rs in the street were impressed this forenoon and set to work throwing up a breastwork across each street leading to the wharves. A vast amount of government stores are in buildings along the wharves and the rebels would like to destroy them. Our reg't. has been for some time at work digging a trench around the city. ## Editorial Notes

The impressed labor passage is jarring: "all the n****rs in the street were impressed this forenoon and set to work throwing up a breastwork." Four months after the Emancipation Proclamation, free Black people in Alexandria are being forced to build military fortifications without choice or pay. Charles records it without comment — it is simply what happened on a Sunday morning.
