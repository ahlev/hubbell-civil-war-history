## Full Transcription

> Aug. 6th, 1861
> Camp ?
> (While attending West Point)
>
> Dear Frances:
>
> I think it is about time that I answered your letter. I wrote to you and Charles a week ago, but when I came to mail the letters the next morning both were missing. Today I had a general overturning of things in my locker to find my note paper, and up came the letters. I don't know whether to tear them up or send them with those that I am now writing. I think I will send them in order that you may see that I have written.
>
> The locker or chest where we put our clothes, paper, books and everything in general is five feet long, one foot high and one foot broad, and must answer for three persons. Whenever we are in a hurry - which occurs frequently - we jerk out whatever we want, putting everything of course "in a muss". There is a little oil on the corner of my paper; you must excuse it as it is the best I have - my hair oil got upset. It did not get onto my clothes fortunately.
>
> There is a cessation of all business here today on account of Ex President Van Buren's death. A cannon is fired every half hour, the flag hung at half mast, and all the officers have put crape on their right arm, which they are to wear for six months. Thirteen guns were fired this morning before reveille, thirty will be fired tonight at six. This morning, when the third class came in from firing, a little before reveille, they went around to the fourth class' tents and made all the Plebes turn over towards the color line "in honor of Van Buren's death."
>
> If a person wishes to come to West Point "to see the sights", August is the month. There is the most going on at this time. The third class have horses attached to their battery now in drilling. It is a very pretty sight to see them drill. We all drill together during this month in infantry tactics. The fourth class drill by themselves in artillery. The drill is to learn to load and also to mount upon the limber boxes and casions of the piece. It takes seven men to work one piece. After the men understand the duties of the places which they happen to have they change places, so that each person may understand the duties of each number well.
>
> You asked me for my photograph. I cannot conveniently get it taken now, but perhaps I will before long. I think you will get out of patience if I scribble much more, so will close.
>
> Please write soon.
>
> Your aff. brother,
>
> James.
>
> Remember me to Mrs. Brinckerhoff.

The observance James describes (cannon salutes, flags at half mast, crape worn for six months) is consistent with August 1862, not 1861. ## Cross-Reference Notes

### Open Questions
- **RESOLVED — Van Buren dating:** Martin Van Buren died July 24, **1862** (confirmed via external sources). This letter is misdated in the 1861 collection. Correct date: **August 6, 1862**. File should be re-cataloged as `LTR-1862-08-06-001` in future passes.
- **Was James a cadet or attending a training program?** The introduction says he served in Co. I, 153rd NY Volunteers — not a West Point graduate assignment. He may have attended a short officer training course, not the full 4-year academy program. The 153rd NY was organized October 1862, just 2 months after this letter.
- **James's class standing:** He references "we" drilling with "the fourth class" in artillery and "all drill together during this month in infantry tactics" — was he a plebe (fourth class)?

## Editorial Notes

This letter stands out from Henry's letters in tone — James writes with more humor and detail about daily life. The hair oil spill, the lost letters, the description of the shared locker are vivid domestic details of cadet life. The Van Buren death observance provides a dateable event that may help resolve whether this letter is correctly dated to 1861 or should be 1862. If Van Buren died July 24, 1862, then an Aug 6 observance at West Point would make sense for 1862, and the letter heading "1861" may be an error in the original or transcription.
