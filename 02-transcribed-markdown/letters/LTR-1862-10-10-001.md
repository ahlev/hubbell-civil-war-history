## Full Transcription

> ..... 30 .....
>
> Oct. 10, 1862
> Plattsburgh
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> As Alek Douglas is to leave for home tonight I send by him two pairs of socks to have the heels run as they will not last any time as they are. My new grey socks have a hole in the heel already. Perhaps the socks at home are better than those I send. If so send them. I think two pairs will be all I will have room for or want to carry, besides those I have on.
>
> Alek D. leaves because his father has been severely hurt by a falling timber. It was not easy for him to get a short furlough. We have double guards posted inside the fences. Part of them have loaded muskets. We received a check for $25 today. There is $67 due us yet including one months pay. The men say they will not leave until they are paid up. But I think they will.
>
> I arrived safely about nine oclock at the barracks the night I left home. Called at Mr. Savages store & at Mrs. R. M. Hubbells. No one at home except Sid & wife when I was. I hope that you will treat the Savages well as they always treated me well. I did not see David S. the other day. I was sorry that I had no time to call at Mr. Savages house. We expected to leave in the morning but I think we will leave Monday.
>
> One of the men told me that he spent $11.00 for beer yesterday treating any one that happened to be on the [?] spot. The grocery is on the grounds. Honesty lasts some of the men no time. Have you heard from Henry? I suppose not as very likely he is a prisoner. Hoping that you & Frances will make the best of things, I will close with this wish.
>
> Your affectionate son, ~~subscriber~~ Charles F. Hubbell
> ..... 30 .....

## Editorial Notes

A prosaic letter dominated by socks and money, but the desertion detail is telling: loaded muskets on the guards inside the fences. Plattsburgh Barracks has become a low-security prison for its own recruits. Charles mentions the $11 beer spree without judgment but it paints a picture of men burning through bounty money on the barracks grounds while the grocery does brisk business. The casual postscript about Henry — "I suppose not as very likely he is a prisoner" — shows how Charles has settled into the prisoner theory as a manageable belief. He doesn't know yet that this belief will be shattered.
