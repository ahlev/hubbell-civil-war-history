## Full Transcription

> July 20th, 1863
> Alexandria, Va.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I have not received a letter since July 8th and I am a little vexed at your long silence. But I try to excuse you by thinking that you and Frances are both busy. I have not written because I have been waiting to hear from you. Have not heard from James for some time. Have you heard from him? I have not heard a word about him since the army left Aquia Creek. He must have been nearly worn out after Hooker's forced march.
>
> Capt. Rich left yesterday for Champlain. I heard that he is going to get the band of the 26th N.Y. Reg't. for Gen. Slough; also that he is going to try to get the drafted men from Champlain and vicinity to come into the three Plattsburgh companies in our reg't.
>
> Steais and Douglas have had a list from home of the big bugs drafted in Champlain and Alex D. heard that there were some long faces there. Sid and Silas are lucky to escape, for $300 doesn't grow on every bush. There was a Plattsburgh paper here with a printed list of the drafted men in our county. Nothing has pleased the soldiers so for a long time as the draft. The only objection is the law that the soldiers have that men can get off by paying for it.
>
> Tomorrow there is to be a general review of all the troops in Alexandria. We don't like it much as we marched in the hot sun on the 4th of July enough to satisfy us. Our woolen clothes are not cool, you know. We expect to be paid today. Our company is having a good time and we don't expect to leave here very soon. We would not have much a good time if we were with the reg'ts. Nine o'clock Sunday morning we have inspection. We get up at five and are all the morning getting ready. Each man unslips knapsack and comes it to show his kit. It takes a great deal of rubbing and brushing to get ready for inspection.
>
> How is your and Frances health? Hope it is good, also hope that you both are prospering finely. My health is good. How is Grandmother McNeill? and all the rest in Charlotte? Is Frances gone visiting? Guess she has, as she doesn't write, but then she would have more leisure on a visit than she has at home. What is the news from Champlain? Have you heard from James lately? If you have will you send me his letters and I will return it. I saw a day or two since that there are now twenty vacancies in West Point owing to that number not passing the examination.
>
> Don't you think New York has been a pleasant place to live in the last few days? I mob rules as well. I guess a good many citizens skedaddled from the city. I think a good many that are drafted in Champlain will pay $300 before they will come out as soldiers. Has Frances put out any of my money at Savages since the first time I sat fall? How much did she get out then? Have you done anything further about Henry's money? Well, that claim agent did you go to?
>
> We have had a good deal of rain here lately but now the water is hot. I never saw such a place for blackberries as it is here. Have been over the river to M., one after berries. Eight or ten of us got a pass and borrowed a boat and then after showing our pass at the gunboat in the river we were soon across. Staid an hour or two and came back with over half a bushel of berries. They are very thick towards the convalescent camp two or three miles from here.
>
> Hope that you and Frances will both write soon. Hoping that you both are trying to enjoy yourselves and don't worry about us boys. I will close by saying that I remain
>
> Your Aff. Son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell

## Cross-Reference Notes

### Connections to Other Letters in Collection
- **Draft riots:** Charles's oblique reference to the New York City Draft Riots (July 13-16, 1863) — the deadliest civil disturbance in American history — is characteristic. "Don't you think New York has been a pleasant place to live in the last few days?" is dry understatement about events that killed over 100 people.
- **$300 commutation:** The soldiers' objection to the $300 buyout provision was widespread — wealthy men could avoid service while poor men could not. This was the same resentment that fueled the draft riots.
