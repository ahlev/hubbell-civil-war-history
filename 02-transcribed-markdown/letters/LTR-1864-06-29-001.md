## Full Transcription

> [Beginning missing]
>
> If any additional charge is made please let me know the amount, so I can collect of Martin his share of the freight. He has paid his share of the $5.00 which was 30¢.
>
> Our Chaplain told me a few days since that he thought that we would not stay here a great while. I don't know where we will go, but probably we will garrison some place near the Mississippi River. Butter are plenty here but they sell very high. Flour they sell from 10 to 12½¢ a pound. Dried apples — 25¢ a lb. Dried apple pies sell at 50¢ each and small short cakes 5¢ each. The pies and flour cakes are made by the men. They are baked in a kettle with a cover.
>
> James yesterday made his first attempt at making doughnuts. He fried the grease out of a lot of pork so that he had plenty of grease. As he put no yeast powder in the dough the cakes were heavy. He wanted sugar or molasses, but sugar costs too much, 50¢ or over at the sutlers. We draw flour once in a while. New York papers sell here for 25¢. I should like to receive some papers from you. How do you and Frances prosper? Have you heard from Alex lately? I have not heard from him nor written to him since I was at N. Orleans. Please write soon.
>
> From your aff. son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell
