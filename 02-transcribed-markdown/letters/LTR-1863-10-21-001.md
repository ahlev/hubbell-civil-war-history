## Full Transcription

> Oct. 21st, 1863
> Capitol Hill
> Washington, D.C.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I read a letter from you a few days since, also one yesterday from James, which I will enclose with this. I guess a man at Point Lookout will have to be very economical not to be in debt at the end of the course.
>
> A good many regts. have been arriving here from the north lately and going straight to the front. They only stopped long enough to take in rations and then left. A guard from our regt. was at the depot three days to keep the men from straggling from these regts. There were several cavalry regts. There were several new cavalry regts.
>
> Some time since I was on duty at Carrol Prison when I saw something walking in all there. I looked at it sharp for a minute. It was a young lady with broadcloth pants and the same kind of material for the short frock. The superintendent of the prison called her "Foster" and had quite a chat with her. Joked her about her breeches, also told her women had no business to be in the army. She called to see Belle Boyd who is confined there and is a rebel and has small secesh flag which she shows to the guard. The Bloomer doctor is said bold feisty could not be looked nor talked down. She is a unionist and is employed in our army. Quite a number of rebel women are confined in that prison.
>
> It is very uncertain whether we stay here this winter. We may leave any day. Two days since our reg't. was out on general inspection. I got excused that morning by the doctor for this first time, so I missed going on that inspection. Nothing was the matter but a cold. I have bought a vest, price $3.00, so you see I intend to keep warm.
>
> Our 1st Lieut. has within a few days married a lady from Alexandria. They live in a house close to the barracks.
>
> One of our Co. went home with his discharge last night. He had the cabosibles. I think that spells Silas. Is Mr. Boyle going to pay you a cow for yours? I think it will be better for you to buy a cow in the spring unless you are sure you have fodder enough to winter one. But you can't have enough, so you had better buy your milk and butter this winter. What is going on in the quiet village? Does Alex Cash expects to be married or I don't think Sid would move out from the old homestead.
>
> Please write as often as you can. Does the President's new call for men cause any excitement in C?
>
> From your aff. son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell

## Cross-Reference Notes

### Connections to Other Letters in Collection
- **Belle Boyd:** One of the war's most famous Confederate spies, Belle Boyd was imprisoned multiple times. Charles's matter-of-fact description — she "has a small secesh flag which she shows to the guard" — treats her as a prison curiosity rather than a celebrity.
- **Female soldier/doctor:** The "Bloomer doctor" in broadcloth pants is likely Dr. Mary Walker, who served as a Union surgeon and was the only woman to receive the Medal of Honor. Walker was known for wearing men's clothing and was employed by the Union army in the Washington area in 1863.

## Editorial Notes

Charles stumbles onto Civil War celebrity: Belle Boyd in Carroll Prison, and almost certainly Dr. Mary Walker visiting her in broadcloth pants. The superintendent calls Walker "Foster" (possibly a misremembering), joked about her breeches, and told her "women had no business to be in the army." Walker was indeed employed by the Union army and wore men's clothing throughout the war. Charles's description — "said bold feisty could not be looked nor talked down" — captures Walker's famous defiance perfectly, even through the garbled transcription.

Belle Boyd showing her Confederate flag to prison guards is the kind of theatrical defiance that made her famous. Charles records it without realizing he's witnessing two of the war's most remarkable women in the same building.
