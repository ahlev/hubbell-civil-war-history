## Full Transcription

> May 17th, 1864
> Camp South of the Oostanaula River
> Georgia
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I thank God that I am able to inform you that I came out of the battle on Sunday the 15th inst. unscathed. The enemy has been defeated and we are now in close pursuit. The loss of our army is comparatively small, I think. The troops in this fight went in in a way that I never saw before being formed in solid column. We were under a heavy fire from eleven A.M. Sunday, until long after dark, but owing to the nature of the ground (being a succession of ridges) our loss was very slight. I am happy to inform you that not a man of Co. H was hurt. I dislike very much spinning yarns on paper and will wait until I see you, which I trust will not be very long, to tell you all about the fight.
>
> I made a mistake in my last letter in stating that it was Buzzard's Roost that the 2nd Div. attempted to storm. The Ridge is called, I believe, Mills Ridge. Love to all. Please write often. We shall have to march some.
>
> Your aff. son,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

"The troops in this fight went in in a way that I never saw before being formed in solid column." Resaca was fought differently from the earlier battles Alexander knew — solid column assault rather than the open-order skirmishing of the Eastern theater. The succession of ridges that saved lives is the Georgia terrain that will define the entire Atlanta Campaign — ridge after ridge, each a defensive position, each requiring a flanking movement.

"Not a man of Co. H was hurt." After Gettysburg's 52 casualties and Lookout Mountain's minie ball, this is the relief of survival spoken in its most compressed form.
