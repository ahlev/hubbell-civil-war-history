## Full Transcription

> May 1st, 1862
> Camp Niles, Relay House
>
> Dear Mother and Brothers:
>
> I am well, Luther is well and all is well. Our Colonel has been promoted to Brigadier General of Volunteers. His name is General Green, a desendent of old revolutionary hero General Green. He certainly is a good officer and a strict disciplinarian. I like him very much. We are still under marching orders but they have been countermanded again. There has been a good deal of excitement in camp today. New Orleans has been captured. It is the heaviest blow that has been struck at the rebels since the war commenced.
>
> You say in your letter Mother that you "repent by this time that you ever went to the war." Why Mother, I am astonished and ashamed that you have so poor an opinion of me. I enlisted to serve three years or during the war. My enlistment was not the act of a moment. I gave the matter days and weeks of consideration and since I have been a soldier I have never for an instant regretted the step I took. On the contrary, I glory in it. I should not be ashamed to die in such a cause. I do not want you to feel that way about it.
>
> One of the prisoners was shot through the leg the other day by one of the sentinels accidentally. It was also reported that one of Co. A's men was shot and instantly killed by one of his friends accidentally. The man who shot him tried to kill himself. There is a good deal of such carelessness in the army.
>
> I will give you an account of what I have spent since I have been in the army:
>
> Sutler — $5.00
> Luther — $3.00
> Washing — 40¢
> Ayes — 50¢
> Bond — 27¢
> Stems — 25¢
> Powers — 10¢
> Myers — 10¢
> White — 10¢
> Bascomb — 10¢
> Long — 5¢
> Vaughan — 3¢
>
> That is every cent I have spent or loaned in six months. I should not have spent a cent at the sutler's if the rations had been sufficient but they were not. I gave Luther three dollars — he was in need of it. I think I have done pretty well.
>
> Frances — Luther's letter is enclosed. Write often.
>
> Your affectionate Son and Brother,
>
> Alexander

## Editorial Notes

Alexander's rebuke of his mother is the emotional peak of the collection so far. Her suggestion that he "repents" enlisting draws a passionate defense: "I enlisted to serve three years or during the war. My enlistment was not the act of a moment." At 18, he frames his service as a deliberate moral choice: "I should not be ashamed to die in such a cause." The complete six-month expense account ($9.90 total) is remarkable for its precision and frugality — an 18-year-old corporal who tracked every penny down to 3¢ loaned to Vaughan. The accidental killings reveal the ever-present danger of camp life: untrained men with loaded weapons. General Green is almost certainly Col. George Sears Greene, 60th NY, whose claim to descend from Nathanael Greene was well known — he would later command at Culp's Hill, Gettysburg, at age 62.
