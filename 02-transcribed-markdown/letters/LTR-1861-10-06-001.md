## Full Transcription

> October 6th, 61
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I received your letter last eve. Very glad that you are all well. We are having very warm weather now. Has been quite cold. I came off from picket yesterday. I enjoyed it very much. The pickets are stationed ½ mile and some a whole mile apart. I with three others had to walk three miles from Camp to our picket. The hardest of it is to carry our overcoat and two blankets, but I have got use to it and don't mind it much.
>
> I have bought a frying pan. It cost 50¢. I shall take it home when I go. Our Company are all under arrest so I have heard. It seems one of our boys left his picket before his time, had some high words with Lieut. Scott, was quite abusive to him, when he was put under arrest. Six dollars is taken off his next wages and a knapsack of 30 lbs. for two days, and his hands tide over his head. Well the boys thought that was to much so one of them went and cut the rope that was on his hands. Our whole Co. is under arrest to find out who it is.
>
> I have got all the clothing I want. Since I have been in camp in M. have got a pr. of shoes, pr. socks and one shirt. That will be all I will want for the present.
>
> Oct. 7th
>
> Today is quite cloudy. Looks like rain. There is a guard stationed around our tents and all on account of that occurance yesterday. I am sorry for the man that cut the rope. It will go hard with him. Have not found out who it is yet. There is no card playing permitted now. If any of the boys are found at it they are court martialed and have a knapsack of 30 or 40 lbs. to carry besides a heavy fine which I think is alright. I for one try to do what is right, and know that you would approve of it if I was at home. I could get into a quarrel every day if I should try to, but I keep out of it, and get along very well.
>
> I am very glad that you correct the bad spelling in my letters. I hope in time to become a perfect speller. I neglected it to much at school, or at least the teachers did. We have a prayer meeting every Sabbath evening, had no preaching yesterday as the Chaplain was sick. Had a very dull day of it. I see Charles is anxious to enlist, but I think he is doing very well where he is, but if he intends or makes up his mind to go he must not enlist into another Regt. but come into our Co. It would be very pleasant to have him here. I have no more time to write.
>
> I remain your aff. Son,
>
> Henry

## Editorial Notes

This letter captures a disciplinary crisis in Co. D. A soldier left his picket early, clashed with Lt. Scott, received severe punishment (pay docked, knapsack weight, hands tied overhead), and an unknown comrade cut his bonds — prompting the entire company's arrest. Henry positions himself as morally upright ("I for one try to do what is right") while showing sympathy for the situation. The chaplain's illness on Sunday and the card-playing escalation to court martial show a regiment tightening discipline as it settles into routine. Most significantly for the family story, Charles is now "anxious to enlist" — the third Hubbell brother to consider or join the war.
