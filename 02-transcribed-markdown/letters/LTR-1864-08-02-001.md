## Full Transcription

> Aug. 2, 1864
> Near Atlanta, Ga.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I received your letter of July 17th and 18th a day or two since and was pleased to hear that you are all well and considerably surprised to hear the 153d was in Maryland. I knew that Gen'l. Canby's troops were there, but did not suppose that the 153d was among them.
>
> Quite a heavy cannonade has been going on today to the extreme left of the line. The 23d Corps (Gen'l. Schofield) was moving to the right yesterday and the day before. I suppose it is them engaged. The distance is so great that I cannot tell whether or not it is accompanied with musketry. The object of the move is, I think, to strike out the Macon and Atlanta railroad. Our lines, I understand, were within a half mile of the road previous to the 23d Corps going there.
>
> Nothing of special interest has transpired since last I wrote you. Sunday night we commenced constructing a new line of work. We were obliged to work in the night on account of the rebel sharpshooters. They plunged two or three balls into the works the next day but did no damage. Last night while the mail was being distributed (a large crowd always collects around the mail) a rebel sharpshooter, I should think nearly a mile away, sent a ball "spot" into the center of the circle. It made them jump a little but they did not scatter until they had got their letters. The ball passed through a tent in its course. The mail carrier is collecting the mail. I must hurry. I want to write James and Charles, but will not until I learn surely whether they are in Maryland. Why does not James write! I feel quite neglected. There surely cannot be one who has a heartier interest in his welfare than I. I would do almost anything for him. Remember me to Charles and James when you next write, and ask them why they do not write me. Please write often. I have — Love to all.
>
> Your aff. son,
>
> Alexander.

## Editorial Notes

The sharpshooter hitting the mail call is one of the collection's most vivid siege details. A ball from nearly a mile away, "spot" into the center of the crowd — and "they did not scatter until they had got their letters." Mail was more important than safety. The ball passed through a tent on its way. Alexander is writing while the mail carrier collects outgoing letters — the same mail system that just survived a sniper shot.

The flanking movement toward the Macon & Atlanta railroad is Sherman's endgame strategy: cut every railroad feeding Atlanta and the city must fall. The 23rd Corps (Schofield) joins the 15th, 16th, and 17th in the rightward sweep. Lines within half a mile of the railroad.

"Why does not James write!" — the youngest brother's silence hurts. "I would do almost anything for him." Alexander, under daily fire outside Atlanta, worries about his teenage brother's feelings more than his own safety.
