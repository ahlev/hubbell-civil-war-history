## Full Transcription

> March 12, 1863
> Alexandria
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I sit down to write wondering what the reason is that I don't receive a letter. Wrote you nearly three weeks since requesting that you would send me two dollars as I wished to buy a pair of boots. I then borrowed $2.00 and bought the boots and have been looking for a letter with the money, but have received no letter since one dated Feb. 23rd. I think that your letters may have miscarried, if not, it is strange that you don't write.
>
> I went to Aquia Creek Monday and saw Alex. Went on purpose to see him; got there at 3:00 P.M. and staid till after dinner next day. He was surprised to see me; was well and very much pleased with his visit home. I did not think it would cost so much to go home and back. He says it cost him nearly $40.00 to go home. Alex received a letter from Aunt Laura while I was there. She says Aunt has bought Henry's farm and she doesn't know what will become of the homestead; is afraid Cleveland will have it. What do you think about matters at Charlotte? One of Alex's new boots is running down at the heel; the shoemaker is to blame. Alex gave me two pairs of socks he bought. They look like good ones and came just in time. Where did you get them?
>
> The log huts that the 60th reg't. live in are quite comfortable. I did not arrive at a good time to see Alex alone as he was getting ready for inspection and after inspection was on guard so that our private conversation was limited. But I had a good visit and Alex was pleased. He says he is now third sergeant and when their orderly is promoted to Lieut. he will be second sergeant. Luther concurs; he is sergeant so there will be a vacancy. He looks just as he used to, big fat face and hearty as a buck; is anxious to have a furlough and will not fail to call upon you. No furloughs are now granted in that reg't, on account of not making a good advance at inspection, but they are to have another inspection and hope to have furloughs granted again. The men of Capt. Ransom's Co. complain that he took home with him his Co.'s money that he received for rations. I don't know as it is true. I heard some good singing in Alex's tent. The orderly and sergeant Howes and Luther were the singers.
>
> Today the weather is cold, and there is a flurry of snow once in a while. Will you tell me about Alex's visit, what he did, etc? Do you know I think some one is sick or dead over at Charlotte and that you and Frances are there; is the reason why you have not written. I hope that I will receive a letter soon and also that you and Frances are both well.
>
> There have been a number of resignations in our reg. One of the Capts. who used to be a minister has resigned. I heard that Lieut. Burdick is going to do the same thing, but I don't know as he will. I wish you would send me some m. coupons, if convenient. Ron does the pony get along?
>
> Does Richard (pronounced Resham) talk of making a line fence between us this summer? He has plenty of wood of his own to sell off his lot. Who do you think will take the farm next year? How would Resham do? Are you going to have sugar made this year? Where does Mrs. Brinkerhoff live this winter? There, I think I have asked questions enough. Will you write immediately if you have not written as I would like to know how you and Frances are and I would like $2.00 to pay that money that I borrowed.
>
> Hoping that you will take good care of yourself. I remain
>
> Your aff. son
>
> C. F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

The Ransom accusation is a shock after his sympathetic Jan 14 letter. The man who wrote "I realize in a certain measure your trying anxiety" about Mother's sons is now accused of taking his company's ration money. Charles hedges — "I don't know as it is true" — but the complaint is recorded. Henry's farm being sold by Aunt Laura is another quiet milestone in the family's acceptance of loss.
