## Full Transcription

> July 24, 1864
> Near Atlanta, Ga.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> How different this Sabbath morning is with you to what it is with us. Instead of the chime of the church bells our ears are saluted with the roar of cannon and bursting shell. (I commenced since one burst close by) and made your humble servant dodge a little, as the pieces flew uncomfortably nigh, so if my writing is unusually bad you will know to what to attribute it — reb shells — not me. (There goes another shot.) "We had a most terrific battle the 20th inst. (Another shell made us jump a little just now.) I wrote you a letter the morning of that day and told you that I thought we should move upon the enemy that day, and we did and met the enemy moving upon us and the battle came off. I will not try to describe the battle to you. I could not do it justice. Suffice it to say that the rebs were defeated with great loss, leaving their dead and a large number of wounded upon the field. None of the boys from Champlain were hurt. Captain Delaney was wounded in the arm, a pretty bad wound but not dangerous. He will not be able to be with us probably for two or three months. The 60th Vets covered themselves with glory. We have the credit of saving the 13th N.Y. Independent Battery. (The rebs are shooting uncomfortably close. I must hurry up and say what I have to say as it is a little dangerous writing out here.) Love to all. You see the rebs have spoiled your letter. Please write often.
>
> Your aff. son,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell
>
> P.S. The rebs are shooting very close. We are within 1¼ miles of Atlanta and our long range guns are shelling the city. How good God has been to me.

## Editorial Notes

The parenthetical interruptions for incoming shells are among the collection's most extraordinary passages — "(I commenced since one burst close by)" "(There goes another shot.)" "(Another shell made us jump a little just now.)" "(The rebs are shooting uncomfortably close.)" Alexander writes through a bombardment, his letter interrupted by artillery, the war literally breaking into his sentences.

The Battle of Peachtree Creek (July 20) was Hood's first attack after replacing Johnston — a major assault on the 20th Corps that was repulsed with heavy Confederate losses. Alexander can't describe it and doesn't try: "I could not do it justice." But the 60th Vets "covered themselves with glory" and saved the 13th N.Y. Battery.

"Within 1¼ miles of Atlanta and our long range guns are shelling the city." The campaign's goal is now under bombardment. "How good God has been to me."
