## Full Transcription

> Sept — 28th, 1862
> Champlain, N.Y.
>
> My darling boys — (Alex)
>
> I was shocked this morning by seeing Henry's name on the list of wounded, it did not state whether slightly or severely. I cannot tell you of the agony of mind I am in. , that I could fly to the darling boy, dear noble, brave, Henry. Want of means alone prevents my going starting for Washington. I do not know where he is — but I should find him. I do not know anyone in his regt. to whom I should write — Scott & Ashmond are at the hospitals. Of course I can get no information. My object in writing you now is to urge you to see him if possible immediately and administer to his wants. See that he is provided with every comfort. Write me
>
> [page break]
>
> immediately & relieve me from the agony of suspense. O, the dreadful minutes are hours to me — I do not know what to do with myself — and shall be miserable until I hear from you. I wish you would interest some one to get a furlough for him, if he is able to travel. O, if I could only go down and take care of him. Dear, say precious boy. Tell me what he needs. Get a furlough for yourself if possible — and come home with him. Tell me if I can come as a nurse. O, I wish I could, until he can come. My heart is aching to bursting, and I can write no more. I expect Charles tomorrow. It is heart breaking to think that I must so soon part with him. May God bless & preserve you, my precious boy, is the constant prayer of your aff.
>
> Mother

## Editorial Notes

This letter is barely holding together. The punctuation fractures, the thoughts pile up without resolution, the sentences break off and restart. "I cannot tell you of the agony of mind I am in. , that I could fly to the darling boy" — that comma after the period is the grammar of a woman whose hands are shaking. She asks if she can come as a nurse — the only way a woman in 1862 could justify traveling to a war zone. She has no money, no contacts in Henry's regiment, no information beyond a name on a list. And tomorrow she must smile at Charles and then let him go too.

The parenthetical "(Alex)" after "My darling boys" is telling — she is writing to Alexander specifically because he is the one who might be able to physically reach Henry. But Alexander is himself in a hospital with diarrhea, asking Mother for five dollars.
