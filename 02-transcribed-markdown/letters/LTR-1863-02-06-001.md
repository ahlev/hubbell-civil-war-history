## Full Transcription

> Feb. 6, 1863
> Camp near Stafford House
>
> My dearest Mother and Sister:
>
> Wonder if it is raining up there today as hard as it is here. It began snowing quite furiously early yesterday morning — settled down into a steady rain — rained hard a good part of yesterday, all last night and is raining hard still. I expect the whole State of Virginia will float away pretty soon. One thing is quite evident — the army will have to lay still quite a little while longer. Capt. Ransom has gone — much to my sorrow and the company's. I had hoped to have gone with him, but Gen Siegel would not sign the furlough and it has gone to the War Department, and it will be some little time before I get it, if I ever do. I went down to the cane with the Capt. If I get this furlough what a time I shall have. I shall not know whether I am on my head or heals for the space of ten minutes. I cannot spare more time than that, for I shall have ten days furlough and shall want all the time possible to see you. You must excuse this short letter for I have no fire and the day is raw and chilly. Luther sends regards. He is well and I am very well. Love to all. Remember me to Mr. and Mrs. Copeland and all they boys.
>
> Your affectionate son
>
> Alexander F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

A short, cold letter — Alexander literally has no fire and the day is raw. The furlough denial stings: Sigel refused, the application has been forwarded to Washington, and Alexander knows it may never come. "What a time I shall have" — the conditional tense carrying all the weight of uncertainty. Ten days of furlough, and he wouldn't know whether he was on his head or heels for the first ten minutes.
