## Full Transcription

> Dec. 13th, 1863
> Wauhatchie, East Tennessee
>
> Dear Charley:
>
> I presume you have about come to the conclusion that I have left the land of the living or else entirely forgotten you, but it is no such thing. I have not forgotten you, though I would not blame you for thinking so. I know I have been sadly negligent, but still a great many things have conspired to prevent my writing — the want of materials for the main. I am now writing upon paper I received today. Now for business!
>
> We had a great time coming out here. Were stationed for some time upon the Nashville and Chattanooga railroad, which the 1st Division of our Corps still guards. We (the 2nd Div.) went with Gen. Hooker to open the road to Chattanooga. Five regts. of the Division had a big fight with Longstreet, the night of the 28th of Oct, and thrashed him soundly. I — hours previous we were sent to hold a gap in the mountains on the border of Georgia at a place called Whiteside. So you see we just missed the fight. Three regts of our brigade were engaged. Gen'l Greene, commander of brigade, was wounded. Gen. himself slightly wounded in the toe.
>
> The 60th has been in three fights since then. Our Division was the one chosen to storm Lookout Mountain. I hope you may never be called on to climb such a hill as that. We were often required to halt ourselves on with our hands, which you know, in the face of the enemy is not over pleasant. We sit and look at each other very composedly as we have both learned better manners than to fire at each other. The rebels are very anxious to exchange papers with us, but we have strict orders not to do it. If the settling of the war was left to the soldiers we would do it in short order. Some of the Rebs are pretty nice fellows, much to my surprise.
>
> My wound caused me to miss the fights of Missionary Ridge and Ringgold. The loss of the Co. stands as follows: Lemage, Tees, Rider, Small, Hubbell, wounded; Majo, Doten, Housedean, killed. The last I saw or heard of him. We made a grand bayonet charge at Lookout. The rebs ran like good fellows. He took the whole posse (1800) prisoner.
>
> I shall endeavor to be more punctual in writing hereafter. Do not write until you hear from me again as we expect to make a move soon and I am afraid I would not receive the letter.
>
> Your aff. Brother,
>
> Alexander Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

"We were often required to halt ourselves on with our hands, which you know, in the face of the enemy is not over pleasant." Alexander describes climbing Lookout Mountain under fire — pulling himself up the steep slope by hand while rebels fired down. The understatement is Alexander at his most characteristic: a near-fatal assault described as "not over pleasant."

This letter to Charles is more militarily detailed than any letter to Mother — troop movements, unit designations, engagement sequences. Alexander gives Charles the soldier's version: Hooker opening the road, Longstreet at Wauhatchie, the Division chosen for Lookout Mountain. The casualty list is more complete here than in the Dec. 1 letter to Mother — adding Lemage, Tees, Majo, Doten, and Housedean.

Note: Some text in the middle of this letter appears to be accidentally duplicated from the September 3 letter (the passage about rebels exchanging papers and being "pretty nice fellows"). This is likely a typist error in the original transcription.
