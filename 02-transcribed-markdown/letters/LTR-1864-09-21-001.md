## Full Transcription

> Sept. 21st, 1864
> In the field near Strasburg
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I sit down to write you a few lines, feeling very thankful that James came out of the battle near Winchester two days since unhurt. There were four killed and twelve wounded in our Co. James is looking very well, he got nicely recruited at the hospital. A day or two after Dr. Sweeney wrote James rejoined the regt. Dr. Sweeney is very kind to James and me, and some time since wished me to transfer him to you. I am still with the Brigade Commissary and am getting fat, the result of good living. I have to walk on the march but carry no load.
>
> I rec'd your letter with the $2.00 and stamps, also James rec'd three newspapers with Mr. Hoyle's name on them. James got our Capt. to write to Albany for his state bounty which is twenty-five dollars and ordered it sent to you. He should have had it long since. The letter was sent a few days since. I think the money you invested in the "2d hands" may well pay. It may be well to invest some of our money in the capital of the C. Bank, but will leave you to do as you think best.
>
> We were camped near Berryville two weeks and while there our Regt. drilled twice a day, leaving hardly time to cook. That is the reason James has not written. We lost a good many men in the fight two days since, but whipped the rebs and drove them to this place where they are making a stand, but they will soon have to leave. Our regt. lost 55 wounded and 10 killed. We are now about twelve miles this side of Winchester. Am glad to hear that you and Frances are well. We are pleased to hear from you so often, hope that you will continue to write often.
>
> From your aff. Son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell
>
> (Written on back of this letter — a note from Frances.)

## Editorial Notes

Third Winchester — Sheridan's first great victory in the Shenandoah Valley. The 153rd NY lost 65 men (55 wounded, 10 killed) — and James, just back from the hospital at Frederick, came through unhurt. Charles, at the Brigade Commissary, is "getting fat" while his regiment bleeds. The irony is not lost, but Charles doesn't dwell on it.

The rebs are "making a stand" at Strasburg — Fisher's Hill. Sheridan will attack the next day (September 22) and rout them again. Charles doesn't know it yet, but he's describing the setup for another victory.
