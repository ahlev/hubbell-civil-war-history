## Full Transcription

> May 1st, 1862 — Thursday
> Fortress Monroe
> Camp Windfield Scott
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> We have just been paid off for two months. I shall send twenty dollars home. Peter Guyan tapped my boots — one dollar. Sutler — one dollar. Cook — fifty cents. That leaves me three dollars and fifty cents to spend as I please. Emerson Smith has used all his wages up. I have drawn one pair of shoes and one pair of drawers.
>
> There is a report in camp that there are fifteen thousand prisoners near here. A rebel fort is near by. Our men are building a tower right opposite the rebel fort. Shells are thrown into it every day. There is a great deal of excitement in camp. I think there will be a move before long.
>
> Saturday — I mailed the twenty dollars by express. The express man charged fifty cents. I was on special duty today felling timber. We do that and everything else that comes along. I have been thinking of subscribing for the Plattsburgh paper — it is only ten cents a week. As far as I can see the Sabbath is not kept any more than any other day. I suppose God will not hold us accountable for what we cannot help. We are up before daylight every morning ready for an attack. Nothing more this time.
>
> Your aff. Son,
>
> Henry

## Editorial Notes

Henry's financial precision matches Alexander's — both brothers tracked every penny. Henry's $20 home versus Alexander's $10 reflects the difference between a private's pay in an active theater and a corporal's in garrison. The Sabbath reflection — "I suppose God will not hold us accountable for what we cannot help" — shows Henry's quiet religiosity wrestling with military necessity. The rebel fort with a Union tower being built opposite and daily shelling indicates the siege intensifying around Yorktown. "Camp Windfield Scott" is Henry's spelling of Winfield Scott.
