## Full Transcription

> Dec. 9, 1863
> Capitol Hill
> Washington, D.C.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> Our Regt. commenced doing duty this morning again. We now guard Carrol Prison again, relieving the Invalid Corps from that duty. We all now expect to stay here this winter, because we are doing duty again. Sure nobody is sorry, although some have said they wanted to go to the front. I think that it was intended to send us to the front, because we changed our guns for new ones, but for some reason we were not sent. Our regt. was called by some, while in Alexandria, Gen. Slough's band box regt. A good many wear white shirts collars cost five cents each. I think I shall let the collars alone.
>
> The theaters are pretty well attended by our regt., our officers giving a pass till 12 P.M. and the men going to the provost-marshal to get it countersigned by him.
>
> The patrol have out several of our men who had no pass in the Central Guard House. A pass to be good after 8 o'clock has to be signed by the Provost Marshal.
>
> Congress is now in session. I intend to go soon and listen to the proceedings. The Capitol is about two blocks from our barracks so that we have a fine chance to see it. The grounds around the Capitol are large and kept in good order. Iron seats, shape of sofas, are placed all along near the broad stone walks where one can rest in the shade and enjoy himself.
>
> Our Chaplain has formed a union church in this regt. Men of all denominations can belong to it, but I think the majority are Methodists. Last Sunday the sacrament was administered by the Chaplain to our church in the Methodist church close by. A large number were admitted into the church on probation and a good many more, formerly members, were admitted to full communion. One man from each Co. is chosen once in three months to assist the Chaplain. The man chosen on our Co. gave in four names. The Chaplain asked me if I was a member of the church; that was about all he asked me. He is a polite, quiet man and is liked by all the men. I think he is not a very good speaker. Prayer meetings are held every evening. There were quite a few new converts that joined the church on probation. Some members thought they were double I about joined in the same way. A name was read off us joining on full communion. The Chaplain is a Presbyterian, I think.
>
> Have you heard from Alex? I am anxious to hear from him, but don't borrow trouble, as that doesn't pay. How does James get along? I have not heard from him for a long time. I suppose you have his last reports. Hope that Frances is enjoying herself, now that she is out of school. How does Pet Steais get along? I guess he is well enough now he is home, but don't tell that I said it. Must close, and I expect that long letter from Frances that she promised.
>
> From your aff. Son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

Charles and Mother write on the same day with opposite emotional temperatures. Mother: "Where are you? Oh, where are you?" Charles: "I don't borrow trouble, as that doesn't pay." The difference is partly temperament, partly gender role — but also partly that Charles has learned, through two years of war, that worrying about what you can't control is a luxury soldiers can't afford.

His plan to visit Congress — two blocks from his barracks — is the kind of detail that makes garrison duty in Washington unique. Soldiers guarding Carroll Prison could walk to the Capitol and listen to the debates that shaped the war they were fighting.
