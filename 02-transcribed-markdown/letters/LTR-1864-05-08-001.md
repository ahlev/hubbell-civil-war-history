## Full Transcription

> May 8th, 1864
> Camp near Altoona Creek, Ga.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> The mail leaves in a few minutes, and I must hasten and have my say for I know you are anxious to hear from your boys in these perilous times. Well, I am happy to say that I am still all right and trust that I shall come out of this campaign so. Culver Bostwick is also well and sends much love to his friends. He has had a chance to try his sharpshooting qualities upon the rebs but with what success I am unable to say — though I expect he made them think a little at least. Of the movements of the army I am unable to inform you. They are so continued I cannot make heads or tails of them. I have rec'd no word from home since I left Bridgeport, Ala., and I feel, oh, so anxious about you all. How you heard from James and was he in that terrible fighting in Va.? I hope and pray he is all right, and Charley. How is he? I see Gen. Banks has moved forward. It is a great comfort to me, Mother, to know that there is One above who knows all things and does all things for the best. It is such a relief to have One to whom we can go and confide all our griefs and trials.
>
> If there is any one that needs God's help I am sure it must be a soldier. I put you all in His hands daily and feel comforted. Give my regards to all friends. With God's help we hope to crush this rebellion this summer. Love to all.
>
> Your aff. son,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell
>
> P.S. Luther is well. We have only two men of Co. H injured. Please send me some stamps. I borrowed one of Luther to send this letter. Write often and send the Tribune as often as possible. I believe we are in Cobb Co., Ga, so that if you look us in the map you may know where to find us.

## Editorial Notes

Three Hubbell brothers are now in combat simultaneously — Alexander in Georgia, Charles on the Red River, James in the Wilderness. Alexander doesn't know the details of James's fight, only that it was "terrible." His religious feeling, always present, deepens under fire into something approaching mysticism: "It is such a relief to have One to whom we can go and confide all our griefs and trials." The soldier's God is not theological but practical — someone to talk to when the bullets are close.
