## Full Transcription

> March 16, 1863
> Aquia Landing
>
> My dear Mother and Sister:
>
> How I should like to see you both today — to be seated in your comfortable sitting room and have it snowing "pitchforks" so that we could have a nice cozy visit — something I hardly had while there. How I wish I had had time to visit the Academy while in session. One of the most pleasant points of my visit for me to look back upon is the lecture in the old Academy. Please to tell Mr. Simons, Miss Fannie, that we hope to have an opportunity soon to vindicate our honor before the world, and to show that we have brains and in the right place too.
>
> Now, dear Mother, a very singular circumstance has occurred. The let or you wrote to Charles containing money by some means or other came to me. The letter was directed to 60th Regt., company I. The sixtieth was crossed out, and in place of it, the 153d placed before it. This was also crossed out, and on top of the envelope it was directed by the postmaster to the "sixtieth N.Y. Volunteers." It was very fortunate, was it not, that I got it? I mailed the letter with one from myself to Charles today. Charles was up to see me a day or two since and I gave him two pairs of socks, as you directed. I am glad he has got them. He said they were just what he needed. He has got himself a first rate pair of boots and has paid $4.75 for them. Said he had written you were the age for money and wondered why you did not send it. Said he was afraid that all was not right up there (i.e., you were sick) ... We have finished the forts and now the air is a daily detail to work upon the wharves unloading boats, etc. We are very pleasantly situated, and I hope we shall remain here for some time to come. How are you getting along with that Rye affair? The old rascal will receive his deserts yet!
>
> You will receive a call from Lieut. Ingram are long. He was very much gratified by my visit to his wife. I received a letter from him a few days since. I wish, Mother, if you can, you would send me Albert Cook's address. I should like very much to hear from him. There is a signal station down at the landing and it makes me think of him. Remember me to Mr. and Mrs. Copeland and their children and to all the ladies of C.
>
> I was very much shocked a few days since by receiving a paper from Mrs. Hoyle with a notice of Dana Lyman's death posted upon it. How much her and you in Champlain will miss her. I have not the slightest doubt but what her prayer for her brother's conversion will be answered. Love to all.
>
> Yours affectionately,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

The mail misdirection is a minor comedy with practical consequences — Mother's money for Charles ended up with Alexander because the postmaster couldn't sort out which Hubbell in which New York regiment. Alexander forwarded it, but the incident illustrates how the Civil War postal system worked (and failed) with millions of letters moving between the front and home.
