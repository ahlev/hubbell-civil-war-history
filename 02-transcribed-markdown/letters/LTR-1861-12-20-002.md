## Full Transcription

> Dec. 20th, 1861
> Camp McClellan
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> We have had a Brigade drill today. Gen. Gorman told our Regiment that they did the best of them all. We felt quite proud, I tell you. I have found a dictionary here. Some of the boys bought one in Washington. I think I shall buy one and send home. It is a very good one. I have written to Alex, but directed to Washington, that was the direction he gave me. I have not heard from him. We hear that Washington is the wrong direction. I do not know his Regt. I shall write again soon. It seems bad that we cannot correspond. A man cannot live in Camp and maintain good morals unless he is very strict and firm. There is so much swearing and gambling that it is enough to ruin any young man. Four men in our company were caught playing cards the other night. They will have to stand on a barrel six hours and two or four dollars taken from their wages. A man in the Minnesota Regt. shot himself through the arm the other day accidentally. How it was done I have not learned. I am well and hope this will find you the same. Write often.
>
> I am your aff. son,
>
> Henry

## Editorial Notes

This letter pairs with LTR-1861-12-20-001 (same day, to Frances) — both carried by B. Miner to Champlain. The pride in Gorman's compliment shows unit cohesion building during winter quarters drilling. Henry's moral commentary — "a man cannot live in Camp and maintain good morals unless he is very strict and firm" — echoes his Dec 19 letter about gambling punishment and the sutler drummed out for liquor. Henry is positioning himself as the upright soldier, distinct from the card-players and swearers.

The Alexander address problem is poignant: Henry wrote to Washington because "that was the direction he gave me," but Alexander is at Camp Jackson near Baltimore. Alexander's Nov 13 letter gave the direction as "Washington, D.C., Sixtieth Regt." — which was apparently wrong or outdated by the time mail could reach him. The brothers are roughly 40 miles apart (Poolesville to Baltimore) but cannot connect. Meanwhile, Alexander's Dec 8 letter confirms he received a letter from Henry mailed Dec 4 — so at least one letter got through, though Henry apparently hasn't received Alexander's reply.
