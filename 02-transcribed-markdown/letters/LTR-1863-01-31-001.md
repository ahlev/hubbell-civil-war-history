## Full Transcription

> Jan. 31, 1863
> Alexandria, Va.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I received your letter today and was surprised to learn that you thought I was sick. My health is very good; have got over my cough and hoarseness. Have not missed duty once on account of sickness since I enlisted.
>
> We have just received our pay for two months. Three months pay is now due us. Nearly the whole Co. this evening as soon as paid, which was about 7:00 A.M., went into the city. I guess there will be no roll call tonight as it is pay night, but the men will have a little liberty. I think we will have some drunken men in our Co. for a few days.
>
> Enclosed I send you a $20.00 allotment check. I suppose it will pass as well as money. Tomorrow morning the whole Co. is ordered to fall out with their guns. Nearly the whole Co. will be on duty tomorrow. I will go to Aquia Creek to guard some soldiers from here and some to the convalescent camp to bring them here. I do not go tomorrow to Aquia Creek, but may go to the same. We have a good time here; have straw ticks to sleep on, and eat much to do. Once in a while we have to guard soldiers to Aquia Creek. Can go into the city when we want to, but must look clean, and be here to roll call which we have three times a day, 6:30 A.M., 5 P.M., and 8:30 P.M. At 8 P.M. we have roll call and dress parade. A boy in our Co. about 16 years old, a hard boy, was absent at roll call several nights running. He was sent to the slave pen and showered. He will behave himself now, I guess.
>
> I wish you would not worry about me, Mother. When I am not well I will let you know. I will keep you posted about myself. You will be much happier if you look on the bright side and expect to see Alex and I safe and sound at home some day, than if you fancy we are sick, etc.
>
> I am glad to hear that you and Frances are well again. How you will take the world easy and try to enjoy yourself. I must close as I have no more time. I wish Frances would write. Has she had a letter from C. Fullerton? The newspaper wrapper on the Observer yesterday was written on the outside. Frances had commenced a letter to her cousin on the wrapper. I don't know who the cousin on the wrapper. I don't know who the cousin is, but she received an unexpected letter from the cousin.
>
> Aff. Son
>
> C. F. Hubbell
>
> ..... 30 .....
>
> P.S. Feb. 1/63 Today is Sunday and we had no inspection this morning because so many men were on duty. I am not on duty today and I am glad of it because the mud is deep, I have not been to Aquia Creek yet. I received an Observer yesterday and one today. It much obliged. Then I saw Alex he had not received any paper for two or three weeks. Perhaps he does now. Letters and papers for us all come just as well without addressing them to care of any one. Last Sunday I attended church twice. It was the Episcopal church where quite a number of shoulder straps and privates were in attendance. I went in the morning and evening and intend to go this evening. The church is a very old one of stone. In the church yard around it are buried persons who died in 1700s. The grave stones are weather worn. Our men kept coming in all last night, some drunk, of course, but they were quiet. There is a theatre and circus here. How do you get along with Mr. Rye about the land? Please write me all about business matters.
>
> Give my respects to all inquiring friends, as the boys tell me to say when I write for them. As this is written in a hurry, please excuse misspelling, which I wish you would correct.
>
> Aff. Son
>
> C. F. Hubbell

## Cross-Reference Notes

### Connections to Other Letters in Collection
- **Slave pen punishment again:** Third mention of the slave pen as a military disciplinary tool in Alexandria (also Dec 8, Dec 26). The showering punishment applied to a 16-year-old boy.
- **Pay finally arrives:** After months of complaints from both brothers about unpaid wages, Charles receives two months' pay. Alexander (six months owed as of Jan 2) may still be waiting.
- **Episcopal church:** The stone church with 1700s graves is almost certainly Christ Church Alexandria (built 1773), where George Washington worshipped. Charles doesn't mention the Washington connection.

## Editorial Notes

Charles's reassurance to his mother — "You will be much happier if you look on the bright side" — is the advice of a young man who has learned that worry is contagious across the mail. The P.S. about the Episcopal church with its 1700s gravestones is a lovely archaeological detail: Charles sitting in a pew that George Washington sat in, surrounded by graves older than the Republic, never mentioning it.
