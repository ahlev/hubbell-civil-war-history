## Full Transcription

> Camp near Aquia Creek Landing
> Feb. 21, 1863
>
> My dear Mother and Sister:
>
> I arrived here safe and sound at about six P.M. yesterday. You pressed me then, that I was but fortyeight hours coming and was here six hours prior to the expiration of my furlough. Found everything here about as I had left it. We are building fortifications. Now as you know how matters and things are going on here, I presume you would like to know something concerning my journey. I went to New York by the N.Y., New Haven railroad. Reached White River Junction that night about two A.M. and remained there until six. Reached New York that day, passing through Springfield, Herts., and Hartford and New Haven, Conn. Crossed the Jersey Ferry and took the first train for Washington which I reached about seven A.M. Friday morning. Left there at twelve M. and reached here as stated. My fare from Rouse's Point to Washington was $17.75. I hope, Mother, you keep up a stout heart. I believe this war cannot last much longer and trust I shall be home again soon.
>
> Love to all, and tell the Capt. I shall write him soon. Luther is well and much pleased with his present.
>
> Your affectionate son,
>
> Alexander

## Editorial Notes

Alexander went home. The furlough he barely dared hope for in the Feb 6 letter came through, and he traveled 48 hours each way for what was probably 6-7 days at home in Champlain. He saw his mother, his sister, the stone castle, the farm — everything the war had taken from him for over a year. The letter on return is brief and optimistic: "I believe this war cannot last much longer." He has no way of knowing that the worst is still ahead — Chancellorsville, Gettysburg, the Wilderness, all still to come.
