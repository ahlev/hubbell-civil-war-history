## Full Transcription

> April 23, 1863
> Aquia Landing
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I have sent you today by express forty dollars and enclosed you will find a receipt of seven dollars and ninety cents which Ingram will call and take up which I have no fear he will do. If he does not, all that I shall have to do will be to return the receipt to Frilly and he will refund my money to me again. In all his (Ingram's) dealing with the Co. he has shown himself to be a man of honor. I received a letter from him a short time since. He requested me to do this as he was afraid of trusting his money in the mail. I always liked Ingram from the time I first saw him and would do anything in my power to oblige him. He has been a good friend to me. Ask him about the time when he and I camped under the apple tree and I pulled the pickets off the fence to make a fire. Ask him about the Crimean War. He can tell you some funny stories. A soldier is the man for me anywhere. If ever I come home you must go with me to Montreal and see the soldiers drill.
>
> I received the money you sent me yesterday. I have paid Vaughan so you need not send any more money. Adding this to what little I have sent home before, it makes one hundred and fifty dollars and ninety cents, though the money you have sent me would draw considerably from this amount. Now, Mother, I do not want you to lay this money up for me. I want you to take what little comfort you can with it. Tell Frances to take out for those boots. Wonder if Jim don't want some money. Should hate to have Jim be behind any of the rest of them for want of it. How I should like to see Master James. Received a real good letter from him today. I wrote him today. How I wish I could see him.
>
> I almost forgot to mention that Luther has sent twenty dollars in the same package with mine. Some of the family will call for it. Must be brief for I have another letter to write tonight.
>
> Your aff. son,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

Alexander sends nearly $48 home, tells Mother not to save it but to use it for comfort, and pays off his debts. A week before Chancellorsville, a man who has refused to seek safety and predicted the war's end in 90 days is quietly putting his affairs in order. The apple tree story and Montreal dream are the thoughts of a man who knows where he's going and wants to leave behind the good memories.
