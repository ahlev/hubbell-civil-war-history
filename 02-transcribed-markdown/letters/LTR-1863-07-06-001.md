## Full Transcription

> July 6, 1863
> Alexandria, Va.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I read a letter from you a few days since and was glad to learn that you were enjoying yourself. Am glad that Grandmother recommended you hope for I run one you are at home by, this time. Think her visit will be pleasant for you.
>
> There was a grand parade of all the troops in this place on the fourth of July. Our Brig. Gen'l. and staff rode at the head of the column. The soldiers did not find it much fun to march around the streets all the forenoon with the sweet streaming off from us. But the men did not grumble much because they had some whiskey given them before we started and after we got back. We marched with our reg'ts. There was a band of music and a short speech was made from the platform side of the street, but very few heard it.
>
> I should like to hear from Alex. Have not heard from his for a long time. He must have had a hard time in his march to Penna. Perhaps the want of writing materials is the reason he doesn't write. A deserter from our Co. on furlough was caught and brought back here and is now in jail. I don't know what will be done with him. He was living in St. Lawrence County and wrote to his wife at Plattsburgh. She answered the letter, and as they now knew where he was, the deserter was nabbed. He has a brother in our Co.
>
> I send you James' letter with mine. He deserves credit for his application. I answered his letter this morning. Am glad that you are attending to Henry's bounty and back pay. Suppose you apply late you will have to wait some time for it. Have you seen anything of Brend Miner and A. Cook? How do the Savage family get along? Tell Frances to give my regards to David Savage. Blackberries are now very thick here. In our company alone is near the Potomac, some of our men have obtained a pass from our Lieut. and borrowed a boat and then crossed the river to Maryland after blackberries and cherries. The pass has to be shown at the gunboat in the river. I have not been yet as I have been on duty when they went, but I intend to go over the river berrying soon.
>
> What do you think of the war now since our great victory over Lee? I have not seen today's paper, but I will get one. Hope Alex is all right.
>
> Is the pony getting fat and what do you think he will bring when sold? How does our deer of a cow get along? Does she trouble Sid? If she does I could poke her as she does not tear down a fence but merely jumps it. A poke will stop that.
>
> You had better sell my old clothes if you have not done so, or the moths will eat them up. A good many in our Co. have sent clothing home by express. Mr. Steais wrote to ask to send a box if he could. A soldier when he has too much of a load will sell cheap or give away his overcoat of blanket and our men get them and send them home. Have you paid that church tax? It ought to be paid. Take some of my money and pay it. What do you think of Mrs Cynthia Bourns's husband? Jim Lucas heard that the Savages said he had only one fault and that was he was not a Christian.
>
> Does Silas get any business? I don't believe he will emigrate to Chicago. There is Mrs. Brink passing the summer. I think I have asked a good many questions, but you must ask if you would know. Today it rained nearly all the forenoon very hard. How does Frances go to Charlotte? I received the two papers that she sent. Wish she would write. Must close as I am in haste.
>
> Your aff. Son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

Charles writes from Alexandria on July 6, unaware that Alexander survived Gettysburg two days earlier. "I should like to hear from Alex. Have not heard from him for a long time." The dramatic irony is total — while Charles worries about the silence, Alexander is burying the dead at Gettysburg.

The July 4th parade in Alexandria — soldiers marching in the heat with whiskey rations — is the garrison experience of the war, a world away from the slaughter in Pennsylvania. The deserter caught through his wife's letter is a small, revealing detail: he wrote home, she answered, and the address betrayed him.
