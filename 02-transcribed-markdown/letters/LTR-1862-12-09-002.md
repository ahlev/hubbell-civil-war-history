## Full Transcription

> Dec. 9th, 1862
> In camp near
> Falmouth, V.A.
>
> Dear Madam:
>
> I received your letter of the 2d inst making enquiry as to your Son, Henry, asking me whether he is dead or alive and how I came by the knowledge of his being in Richmond a prisoner with others of a minor nature, and his answer my first that the day I left Harpers Ferry last, I seen and talked with his Brother Charles of the 60th Regt. and he told me there and then that he had got word from Annapolis from Albert Cook who stated that he had seen and talked with Henry, and that his wound was very slight & in the leg. This is all the information I ever got of his that I placed any reliance on since the memorable field of Antietam. I therefore believe him alive & a prisoner with a great many more. As to the men all being exchanged, it is a mistake and as to Henry being able to write to you, it is not wonderful at
>
> [page break]
>
> all, as the bushes that he could creep into and hide himself, it was utterly impossible for him to do so. The woods in which the enemy as well as ourselves were in was clear from brush or any other speciem of vegetation that would hide a man from view. The large trees were far enough apart that on a level you could see from 1/4 to 1/2 mile without interruption.
>
> Hoping that I have candidly answered you all your questions, will close with the wish and hope that you still hear of your son more definitely. "Will close and say that should I hear of anything in relation to him it will be communicated to you.
>
> Sympathizing and obedient servant
>
> R. McDonald
> Orderly Sergt. Co. D, 34 Regt. N.Y.

### Connections to Other Letters in Collection
- **CRITICAL CONTRADICTION:** McDonald says Albert Cook "stated that he had seen and talked with Henry" and that his wound was "very slight & in the leg." But B. Misner told Mother (Oct 29, LTR-1862-10-29-001) that he AND Albert "saw all the prisoners taken at Antietam, and Henry was not among them." These cannot both be true. Either Cook told McDonald/Alexander one thing and Misner another, or the information was garbled through the chain: Cook → Alexander ("Charles of the 60th") → McDonald → Mother.
- **"Charles of the 60th":** McDonald refers to Alexander as "Charles." He met Alexander at Harpers Ferry and confused the name — or the transcriber did. This is clearly Alexander (who was at Harpers Ferry with the 60th NY), not brother Charles (who was with the 153rd NY near Alexandria).
- **Terrain description:** McDonald's account that the Antietam woods were "clear from brush" with visibility of 1/4 to 1/2 mile REFUTES Mother's fear that Henry "died in the bushes." There were no bushes to die in. If Henry fell, he fell in the open.
- **McDonald promoted:** From unnamed private (Oct 1) to Orderly Sergeant — a significant promotion over two months.
- **Eve of Fredericksburg:** McDonald is writing from Falmouth, two days before the catastrophic assault on Marye's Heights. The 34th NY will cross the Rappahannock on Dec 11.

## Editorial Notes

McDonald's second letter is a detective document — he traces his information chain precisely: Albert Cook at Annapolis → Alexander ("Charles of the 60th") at Harpers Ferry → McDonald. This careful sourcing is the work of an orderly sergeant who has learned that rumors kill families. But the chain is broken: B. Misner says Albert Cook saw all the prisoners and Henry wasn't there. Someone in this chain is wrong, or the same man (Cook) said different things to different people.

The terrain description is the letter's most important factual contribution. The West Woods at Antietam were open, mature forest — no underbrush, no bushes, visibility a quarter to half mile. If Henry fell there, he fell in plain sight. He could not have crawled into brush to die unnoticed. This means either: (a) Henry was taken from the field by the Confederates, (b) he was buried by someone before his company returned, or (c) he managed to leave under his own power and went somewhere unknown.

McDonald writes from the eve of Fredericksburg without mentioning it. In two days the 34th NY will cross the river into one of the war's worst battles. McDonald's fate is unknown.
