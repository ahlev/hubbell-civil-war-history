## Full Transcription

> Sept. 15, 1863
> Camp Davis, Capitol Hill
> Washington, D.C.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I received the Tribune two or three days since, and yesterday rec'd. a letter from Alex. He was well and having a pleasant time.
>
> I think you have the wrong idea about the no. of demerits that the cadets can get before they are found. I came across one of James' old letters and in it he said that they can had 150 demerits the first six months, also the same for the second six months of the first year. After that 100 demerits in six months will discharge them. It takes 150 demerits in six months to discharge them the first year. Have you heard from James lately? How is he getting along and how did he dispose of all his photographs? I think James will get along well enough, but don't expect he will graduate very high. Has Hiram I. got his appointment yet?
>
> There is a good deal of sickness in this regiment. A corporal from our Co. died a few days since. He was apparently a stout, rugged fellow, about twenty-one years old, I should think. Alex Douglas is not very well he is this finest, but you will have a chance to see him for yourself, as he expects to go home soon on a furlough. Pet Steais is in the hospital which is close to the barracks. They live well in the hospital and he comes over to the barracks sometimes. Something is the matter with his chest, I believe.
>
> The boys in our Co. laugh a great deal at one of our men, also pity him because he was such a fool as to do as he did. It seems he was engaged to a girl before he enlisted and he has sent her pay, bounty, and other money to the amount of $200. A few days since he heard that she was married and had spent all the money. He is an ignorant fellow and can neither read nor write. The men told him often that she would have served him so and that he had better send it to his father. When asked how his girl is, he replies that she has gone to Hell. This word is rather common here used in this way "keep on if you think there is no hell."
>
> There is a good deal of talk about our leaving this place soon. I don't know whether we will or not. We are to receive our pay today. If we do leave here I shall be sorry, for it is a good place. A short time since we learned that we could draw books from the soldier's free library in the city. Since then we have drawn books.
