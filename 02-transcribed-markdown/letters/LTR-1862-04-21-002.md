## Full Transcription

> April 21st, 1862
> Camp of the 34th Near York Town
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I received your very welcome letter and was glad to hear from home. We belong to Sedgwick's Division, Gorman Brigade. We have had orders to be ready for marching at a moment's notice. Two days rations in our haversacks. All has been still and quite for a day or two. Our Regt. was on picket the night before last. It was a dark rainy night, could hardly see my hand before me. I stood three hours on my post with gun in hand. We could hear the rebels at work, pounding and sawing, probably strengthening their works. How lonesome, dark and dreary, standing on guard in such a night.
>
> I have seen Gen. McClellan several times since we have been here. He is a splendid looking fellow, smiling countenance, rides a fine horse. He has a body guard of about two hundred cavalry. A large balloon has been going up every day to overlook the rebels. We live pretty well now but the cooking does not go right. Our cook is not just exactly the article. I received the fine comb from Charles. I received a paper from Alexander but no letter.
>
> I had four letters to read the other day that came altogether. Plumer would do to hire for a few days but I would not put much confidence in him for a farm. Would it not be well to see about hiring? I fear the farm will suffer on account of no one to see to things. I hope the horses and cattle are well. I worry about these things a good deal. It is all right about the sugar bush. I hope you will not worry about me for I am well and have been since I have been in Virginia and have not taken any cold. It is rather warm here. I wish I could be at home to help you but I can't be everywhere. When I think of the good times I have had at home it seems like a dream. Write often for I like to have a letter from home to read very much.
>
> Your aff. Son,
>
> Henry
>
> April 24th — There is every appearance that we shall have a move before long. We are now cooking by Co. which is a good thing. The Brigade goes on picket tomorrow. How soon after that we shall march is uncertain.
>
> April 26th — Our Co. was on picket yesterday. We were relieved this morning. A little before day there was some firing, probably half an hour brisk. I could see the flash of the cannon — close enough for all practical purposes. They threw some shells — one burst quite near. Write often.

## Editorial Notes

The observation balloon — Prof. Thaddeus Lowe's corps — is the first mention of aerial reconnaissance in the collection. Henry's description of McClellan with his 200-cavalry bodyguard and "smiling countenance" contrasts with the general's later reputation for excessive caution. The April 26 picket experience is Henry's closest combat yet: "I could see the flash of the cannon — close enough for all practical purposes. They threw some shells — one burst quite near." This is the same morning described from a slightly different angle in LTR-1862-04-18-001's April 26 entry. Henry's farm anxiety — worrying about Plumer's reliability, the horses, the cattle — shows him carrying responsibilities at home even while on the siege lines.
