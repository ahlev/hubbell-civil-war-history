## Full Transcription

> March 21st, 1862
> Harpers Ferry
> Bolivor Heights
> Camp Gormon
>
> Dear James:
>
> I received your letter and was very glad to hear from you. I hope you will write as often as you can. We had a slight fall of snow last night, about three inches. We have more rain and high wind than anything else. Last night two tents blew down. There are sixteen in our tent. Have a stove in the middle in the shape of a bell. The tent is also in the shape of a bell, open at the top to let the smoke out. Have a pole in the middle to hold the tent.
>
> It is raining today. The roads between here and Winchester are very good if the weather is ever so bad. They are made of very small stone. Now and then have to cross a brook. The rebles have distroyed the bridges. The bridge across the Shenandoah River is repaired so that the cars can run between here and Winchester.
>
> I expect our brigade will leave before long for Washington. Gen. McClellan praises up the Army of the Potomac very much, thinks we have done good service in picket duty last summer. I don't care how soon this war closes. If it was next week I would not object. I have had my boots tapped and a patch put on for one dollar. Saves me getting a pr. of shoes from the Government. Peter Guyan did the job. That bag of flour I got from Mrs. Douglas I never paid for. This has been neglected to long. Please take some of my money and pay it. I had the money twice to pay it but didn't pay it. I never could find him. His men would not take it.
>
> I have nothing more to write this time. I expect our Brigade will move before long for Washington.
>
> I remain your aff. Brother,
>
> Henry
