## Full Transcription

> Dec. 12th, 1864
> Nashville, Tenn.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> Nothing of special interest has transpired here since I last wrote. Since then the weather has been intensely cold. Today the weather is clear, but very keen with about two inches of snow on the ground. Just before the cold weather set in I'd set up in a shanty and fire place and am therefore quite comfortable. I was out this morning beyond the pickets after rails for fire wood, but was not fired at. The firing just to the right was quite sharp. I got into the pit of one of the advanced pickets (a colored soldier) and reconnoitered the ground pretty thoroughly before going out. The colored gentleman told me as if he could "only les get his eye onto one of dem rebs he would fetch him sure" and I rather think he would. These colored soldiers fight well.
>
> There has been very little firing for the last few days. Both sides had all they could attend to in keeping warm. I hardly think the Johnny Rebs will venture to attack us in our present position. I expect Thomas will soon take the offensive. Today's paper seems to think Sherman will be at the coast in a day or two.
>
> I hope we shall be sent on as soon as he gets here. I am heartily sick of my present position. Have the "Raiders" yet appeared in "Northern New York?" The Nashville papers are making considerably fun of the recent fright in Burlington, Vt. Let me hear particulars in your next letter. Is the Militia and Home Guard still kept up and do they still stand guard night and day?
>
> Love to all. Kind regards to Mrs. B. Tell Frances I shall endeavor to write her soon.
>
> Your aff Son,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell
>
> P.S. You may direct as usual (Nashville, Tenn.) but be careful and not write contraband intelligence as I may not get the letter. How are Charles and James. Please remember me to them when you write.

## Editorial Notes

Three days before the Battle of Nashville, Alexander goes beyond the pickets for firewood and meets a Black soldier in a pit. "Only les get his eye onto one of dem rebs he would fetch him sure." Alexander renders the dialect with respect, not mockery, and adds: "These colored soldiers fight well." The man who wrote that Providence never intended the races to mingle now admires Black soldiers' fighting spirit. The contradiction is the Union army's defining paradox: men who fight alongside Black soldiers and respect their courage while opposing their equality.

Thomas's offensive is three days away. Sherman is near Savannah. The war's endgame is converging from multiple directions. Alexander, "heartily sick" of Nashville, will witness one of the war's most decisive battles.
