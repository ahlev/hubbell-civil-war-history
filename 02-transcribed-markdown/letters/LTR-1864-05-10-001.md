## Full Transcription

> May 10th, 1864
> Camp near Buzzard's Roost, Ga.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I wrote you a few days since but will not write you again that you may not be over anxious about me in these perilous times. The 1st & 2nd brigades of our Div. were engaged with the enemy at this place Sunday. We should have been in but early that morning we (3rd Brig.) were sent out as a support to a Div. of Cavalry on a reconnaissance and did not rejoin our Div. until late in the evening. The firing had just ceased when we came up. Buzzard's Roost is a high rocky ridge and where we are is only accessible by a narrow gap through the high rocky ledge with which its sides are covered. Our men drove the rebels nearly to the crest of the hill when they came upon them in great force posted behind rocks and trees and in the ledges from which they fired upon our men such a withering fire that they were obliged to halt, but managed to hold their ground until all our wounded were removed and, I believe, left the dead, when they fell back to their original position. The Third Div. of our Corps made a similar attempt lower down with like effect. I think both the attacks were but feints to attract the attention of the enemy from other portions of the army. A large force of our army is now in rear of the enemy. I think the Johnnies will have to "up and git" before a great while. Quite a heavy cannonade has been going on this afternoon on the left of our line. We were working all last night building breastworks, as it was feared the enemy might make a break this way and thus get between the two main portions of our army. I think you will hear glorious news from this army before a great while — even before this letter reaches you.
>
> A dispatch was read to the troops today stating that the Army of the Potomac had thrashed the rebs and driven them ten miles and Gen. Sherman added "let us do likewise." I vote and trust we shall drive them ten times ten miles.
>
> Do not be over anxious about me, dear Mother. I am in God's hands. I hope I shall come out of these battles all right. All the boys from our way are well. Culver Bostwick stands the marches like a little hero. The first day he joined us we marched twenty odd miles. It was a pretty good beginning, I thought. Those new boots used up my feet so that I was obliged to march two days in rear of the column and one day was carried in an ambulance. I am happy to say that I am all right now. Our men are busy today strengthening the breastworks. The rebs would find it hard if they attacked us here. We are somewhat isolated from the rest of the army and this is a very important position. That is the reason of our strengthening it so. I will write you all the news as soon as I can find out anything definite.
>
> Please write often. Give my regards to Grandmother and Silas and all friends. Tell Miss Fannie I am patiently awaiting a letter from her. I will write her as soon as I can find opportunity. The cannonade is quite brisk on the left. I expect they are having a big fight. God grant that we have the victory. Love to all.
>
> Your aff. son,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell
>
> P.S. Have you heard from James and is Charley still at New Orleans? I have two postage stamps left.

## Editorial Notes

Alexander's battle report from Buzzard's Roost is his most tactical yet — the narrow gap, the rocky ledge, the rebels behind rocks and trees firing down with "withering fire," the managed withdrawal with wounded removed but dead left behind. He correctly identifies the attacks as feints — "to attract the attention of the enemy from other portions of the army." The soldier understands the strategy.

Sherman reading Grant's dispatch to the troops — "the Army of the Potomac had thrashed the rebs and driven them ten miles" with Sherman's addition "let us do likewise" — connects the two great simultaneous campaigns of 1864. Alexander's response: "I vote and trust we shall drive them ten times ten miles."

The cannonade is audible as he writes — "quite brisk on the left. I expect they are having a big fight." He writes through it.
