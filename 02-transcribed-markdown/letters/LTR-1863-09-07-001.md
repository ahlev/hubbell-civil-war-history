## Full Transcription

> Sept. 7, 1863
> Capitol Hill Barracks
> Washington, D.C.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I received your welcome letter of Aug. 28th in due time. Was very glad to receive so long a letter. I did not know as four weeks had gone by without my writing, but I suppose I was waiting for you to write. You know you were not very prompt in writing at that time. My health is very good. Have nothing to bother me except bed bugs and other animals, but as I am using unguentine and hot water, expect to be rid of them soon.
>
> Have you seen Mr. Burdick yet? I saw him the night he left, but did not feel very well then as I had a headache. Was all right the next morning, though. Mr. Burdick was in very high spirits at the thought of going home for good. When he was home before, he said that he pushed my letter under the door as no one was in. I sent no letter by him this time as I had no time to write. I am sorry that he resigned.
>
> Received a letter from James Sunday with your last letter. In it was his picture which I think looks very well. I think you are mistaken about Mr. B. trying to injure James. He stands on his own merits there. James says there are to be 65 new cadets to fill vacancies caused by the south seceding. Says the plates are used rather rough.
>
> Frances wants to know about that fair correspondent. Well, she is anything but fair. Among a lot of Beardslee's pictures I saw none here, therefore I know. I guess Corporal Beardslee wants the papers that she sent nearly a month old. I suppose that was the reason, but as I did not do it, had only two or three papers from her. He is saving of his money and would like some one to do it to save him the expenses.
>
> Yesterday I attended the Methodist church which is close to our camp. One of our Co. was there last evening and he was a disgrace to the regiment, as he visited his whiskey in the church. The ladies looked blank and turned up their noses. One of our men called it a religious drunk. He had preaching a last evening in the yard. Benches were brought out of the mess rooms so that we sat down in a square. The Chaplain is sick, but a preacher with newspapers of different denominations gave us a good sermon; also the newspapers.
>
> There are a lot of old Harpers Magazines and some books forming a small library which we can draw from every week, so that we have plenty of reading. Our 1st Lieut. has come home on a furlough. Capt. Rich is somewhere near Fonda attending to conscripts, I believe.
