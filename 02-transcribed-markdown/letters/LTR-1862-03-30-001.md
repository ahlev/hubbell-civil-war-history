## Full Transcription

> March 30th, 1862
> Relay House
>
> Dispatch No. 2
>
> My Dear Mother:
>
> We have again "struck tents" as you will see by the heading of my dispatch. Last Friday morning about quarter after eight orders came for us to be ready to march by nine. I had been detailed for guard duty that day and was waiting for the bugle to sound for guard mounting. I immediately went to work and "packed up" and was soon ready. I endured the march of nine miles very well.
>
> It is no use for me to try to write there is so much noise and jarring. I am enjoying excellent health. There is a rumor afloat that we are to go to Harpers Ferry to guard railroad. Guarding railroads will probably be our business during the war as our Regt. is brigaded "Railroad Brigade".
>
> I shall try and write you a long letter soon. We are moving about so that I can get but few opportunities of writing. Direct your letters as usual. My eighteenth birthday I shall remember as a day of exceeding hard duty. I feel old. I have begun life's battle for myself. I am thrown upon my own resources. It will probably be an advantage to me. "The secret of the Lord is with them that fear him; and he will show them his covenant". I feel that God is near me. My trust is in him and in him alone. I am surrounded with sin and vice, but I feel strong in the Lord.
>
> Sargeant Ingram of our Company will probably be home soon on furlough. He will call upon you.
>
> Your affectionate son,
>
> Alexander

## Editorial Notes

Alexander turned 18 on a day of "exceeding hard duty" — a 9-mile march on his birthday. His reflection is the most mature passage in the collection: "I feel old. I have begun life's battle for myself. I am thrown upon my own resources." The Psalm 25:14 quotation and his awareness of being "surrounded with sin and vice" while feeling "strong in the Lord" show a young man who has found spiritual anchoring amid chaos. The "Dispatch No. 2" label (following the "dispatch" from Camp Preston King) shows Alexander adapting his letter-writing style to rapid movement — short dispatches instead of long letters. The Railroad Brigade designation confirms the 60th NY's wartime mission: guarding the B&O Railroad, the Union's critical east-west transportation link.
