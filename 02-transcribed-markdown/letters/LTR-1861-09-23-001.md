## Full Transcription

> Sept. 23, 1861
> Camp Wheeler
> Ogdensburg
>
> My Dear Sister:
>
> I am contented and happy, though I long to see you all. Our fare is excellent. We had for breakfast this morning coffee, beefsteak, potatoes and bread. Each man has a quart cup of coffee and the privilege of having it sweetened. This is a specimen of our fare. Reveille beats at six o'clock A.M. We have the Corporal's roll call at seven when we form in double line and march to breakfast. After breakfast comes drill. At one dinner. Drill again at three. Retreat beats at six P.M. Roll call again. Supper at seven. Tattoo at nine. Roll call again when all retire.
>
> Our Company was examined and sworn into the States service on Saturday. The examination was very rigid. The surgeon told me that I was a fine boy. There are now over seven hundred men in the Regt. It will soon be full.
>
> 5 P.M. I have just come from drill. Our time is pretty well occupied. You can have no idea how difficult it is to write here. There is a bugle playing not fifty feet from me. Can you send me an overcoat if it is not to much trouble. I have needed one very much. If you send Henrys, please tuck it up so that it will not be too long for me. Will you also send me Charleys wide-awake suit. I shall need it when I stand on guard. If you can get a satchel with a key please send it with said articles. My satchel is xxxx at headquarters and I am obliged to go to the Captain whenever I wish anything. It is very inconvenient.
>
> We have a wash house where all the Regt. wash in long troughs. There is a large twelve pound brass field piece attached to the Regiment, which is discharged at sunrise and sunset. The Captain is very kind to me. We have elected our Capt. and first Lieutenant. Loring E. White, a pious young man, is Lieut. I think we have the best officers of any company in the Regt. Each company thinks they have. I have no more time. The drums are beating for supper. Tell Mother, James and Charles x that this letter is as much for them as you. The band has just truck. Oh how I wish I could see you all. Tell C. I will try to write better next time. Give my love to Mrs. Brink and tell her I want to see her very much.
>
> Your affectionate Brother,
>
> Alexander F. Hubbell.
>
> P.S. Direct your letters to (it is almost dark) Camp Wheeler, Ogdensburgh, New York, care Capt. Ransom.

## Editorial Notes

Alexander's most detailed letter yet — a vivid portrait of Camp Wheeler's daily rhythm. The detail about requesting Henry's overcoat "tucked up so that it will not be too long" gives us a physical detail: Alexander was shorter than Henry. The election of officers by company vote was standard practice in early-war volunteer regiments. Lt. Loring E. White being described as "a pious young man" aligns with Alexander's own religious sensibility (prayer meeting attendance in his Sept 27 letter). The letter is explicitly communal — "Tell Mother, James and Charles that this letter is as much for them as you" — reflecting paper economy and the family's shared reading of correspondence.
