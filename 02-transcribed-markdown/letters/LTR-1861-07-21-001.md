## Full Transcription

> July 21st, 1861
> Washington D.C.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> Although I wrote a day or two before I received this letter, I thought I would write a few lines. I am very glad you are going to Charlotte. I hope you will make a long visit. I have received the two papers. Very glad to get them. While I am writing there is a battle going on. Can here cannons firing not over twenty-eight miles from here. Am not certain when our Regt. will go. Did here something about our Regt. being City guard. Am not certain. Wherever we go I am ready. Know I shall not be forsaken if I put my trust in the all seeing power.
>
> I am very glad you have corrected the bad spelling. It is more careless than any thing else.
>
> We had service this morning at six o'clock by the Chaplain reading a portion of scripture and prayer. Shall probably have service again this afternoon. The Capt. is very fond of singing, have it very often. I shall write more next time.
>
> I remain your Aff. Son,
>
> Henry

## Editorial Notes

This is one of the most historically significant letters in the collection. Henry Hubbell is sitting in Washington on a Sunday morning, having just attended chapel service, and can hear the cannons of the First Battle of Bull Run — the war's first major engagement. The battle, fought near Manassas Junction approximately 28 miles from Washington (Henry's estimate is remarkably accurate), ended in a Union rout. Henry doesn't know the outcome as he writes; his tone is calm and faithful. His companion letter to Frances (same day) includes a dramatic "Later" postscript: "We are ordered off. Shall probably leave in the morning. Ready tonight. May leave before morning. There has been a hard battle today. Have not heard much about it yet." — written as news of the Union defeat reaches Washington.
