## Full Transcription

> [**First part of letter missing — page 1 of PDF is blank scan**]
>
> Continuation — June 6, 1862
>
> be very pleasant for me if I should have to go, to be under your care, but I do not think you could stand it. You would be obliged to be on your feet most of the time, and I am afraid it would bring you down sick. There are others better able than you are.
>
> George Dodds has run down so that you would not know him. He is in the hospital. I think he ought to have his discharge. I believe he has applied for it. I have stood the march since I have been sick better than I thought I would. I should be very glad to have a furlough, but I think it would be difficult to get one just now. I shall try to stand it through now. I hope Cyrus and Jayson will be back.
>
> In regard to my having an instrument of music. It would be very pleasant, but I have no way of carrying it, and of course it would be in my way. We are not sure of staying in one place long. I have received no letter from James or Charles in this letter as you wrote.
>
> Please excuse all mistakes and bad spelling. The pen is the best that I can have just now. Tell Frances I shall write when I have a good pen. I have not quite forgotten her yet. I hope James will pass. I shall send ten dollars in the letter and if it is all right send the rest next time. Please let me know who has the keeping of my money. I should like a few postage stamps if it would be convenient to send.
>
> I remain your aff. Son,
>
> Henry
