## Full Transcription

> January 9th, 1862
> Camp McClellan
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I was very glad to get a letter from you. Glad to know that you are all well. If you are sick I want you should write and let me know. Brink said he had a very pleasant visit in Champlain, only his visit was not long enough. I should like to get a furlough and make a visit home, but I do not think I could, for I understand that only the married men can go at present. I shall have to make up my mind to wait another time.
>
> We are having very pleasant weather now. We had some snow, but it is all off. New Years was quite a merry time with some of the boys. We all received a good glass of beer from the Capt. Some of the boys got drunk, or drank a little more than was necessary. Our Company has presented Capt. Reich with a very nice sword. I gave a dollar towards it. Some gave more and some less. The sword cost $125. It is a very handsome sword.
>
> The Minnesota band played most all night New Years. They came over into our Camp and played some very good tunes before the Colonel's tent. The box of socks has not arrived yet. I expect the box is in Washington.
>
> I have nothing more to write at present. Write often. I get very lonesome if I do not get a paper or something else to read. Excuse this writing.
>
> I remain your aff. Son,
>
> Henry
>
> P.S. Capt. Reich brother is in as sutler for this Regt.

## Editorial Notes

The sword presentation for Capt. Reich is a significant unit-cohesion ritual — companies frequently pooled funds to buy their officers ornamental swords as tokens of loyalty. At $125, this was a major expense; Henry's $1 contribution was modest but meaningful on private's pay. The Minnesota band playing at the Colonel's tent on New Year's night confirms the social bonds between regiments in Gorman's brigade. The detail about Capt. Reich's brother becoming sutler — right after the previous sutler was drummed out for selling liquor — suggests a patronage network within the regiment.
