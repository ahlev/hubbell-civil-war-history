## Full Transcription

> October 29th, 1864
> Patterson Park Hospital
> Baltimore, Md.
>
> Dear Alex:
>
> I had a letter written and sealed which I carried in my pocket for a long time. I have now determined to add another before I send it to you. I was wounded in the left thigh at Cedar Run. It is nothing but a flesh wound and is doing well. I walked several miles after being hit in order not to fall into the Johnnies hands. They were then driving our forces, but things changed before night. Our forces were driving them full well. Their army was completely demoralized. They threw away everything. We got their wagon train, artillery and almost everything they had. The Johnnies attacked and routed the eighth Corps before daylight. This rather took our force by surprise and compelled them to retreat as they were flanked and the breastworks would be of no use. I do not believe the Rebs will try it again in the Valley — three times terribly whipped.
>
> Our Co. was very much cut up. It was also badly cut up at Winchester, so that there is scarcely anything left of it. Lucas was badly wounded. He is the only one you know in the company. Three fellows assisted me to the hospital which was situated in a barn several miles back. I had gone part of the way myself, but was about played out when they came along. I remained at the Hospital a day and was then sent on here. I had thirty miles to ride in an army wagon to get to a railroad station, and then one hundred and twenty in a freight car. That journey was a hard one.
>
> The hospital here is a very comfortable one and the fare very good. Baltimore seems to be the first great place to which our wounded and sick are sent. They are transferred from here to Philadelphia. You were right when I heard from you last. I hope you have got well ere this. I look for a speedy termination of the war. I think we shall be able to judge a good deal come morning it is after the Presidential election. I shall be able to vote here and shall cast my vote for Abraham Lincoln. I hope he will be elected.
>
> We have not been paid for six months. I think it is about time for the Paymaster to visit us. I do not know how long I will be here but will send you my address; it is Patterson Park Hospital, Baltimore, M.D.
>
> Your affectionate Brother,
>
> James.

## Editorial Notes

James's Cedar Creek is told by a wounded man who walked for miles to avoid capture, then rode thirty miles in an army wagon and 120 in a freight car. "That journey was a hard one." The understatement of a teenager who has been shot, walked on it, and ridden 150 miles to a hospital.

"I do not believe the Rebs will try it again in the Valley — three times terribly whipped." James counts the victories: Winchester, Fisher's Hill, Cedar Creek. The Shenandoah is done. At 18 or 19, he analyzes the campaign like a veteran.

"I shall be able to vote here and shall cast my vote for Abraham Lincoln." All three Hubbell brothers — Alexander in Nashville, James in Baltimore, Charles in the Shenandoah — will vote or would vote for Lincoln. The family that was pro-McClellan in April is Lincoln by November.
