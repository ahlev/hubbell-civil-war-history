## Full Transcription

> April 1, 1864
> Head Quarters
> Department of the Gulf
> New Orleans, La.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> Although I have rec'd no letter for so long a time, I think I ought to write once in a while as I have been doing. I think the fault must be in the mail. Today I am to receive over $11.00 extra pay. I have not earned it as I have done hardly anything. The extra pay is 40¢ a day and as I came here March 3rd, will receive nearly a month's pay. "Will have to buy me a blouse which will cost about $10.00." This month another clerk is going to be caterer for the mess. Last month we paid $3.10, but this month we pay five. Last month part of the time we did not have butter or sugar so we pay more this month. The clerks here have sent in a petition for commutation of rations and quarters. If it is granted, it will be a good thing for the clerks, as they can then board where they please. Now the pay is not enough for that. A good many of us clerks sleep in the different offices on the floor. Commutation of rations and quarters is receiving the money for rations and a place to live. I wish I was a better writer; don't know whether I will stay here or not; at my rate you had better not say much about it.
>
> Should like very much to hear how you all get along, especially James and Alex. I wonder if James has enlisted. Gen Banks and most of his staff are in the field, and some clerks with them, but part of his staff stay here to attend to business.
>
> Our troops on the Red River are doing very well and we expect to clear the rebels out of western Louisiana and Texas before long. I have not heard a word from the regt. since I left it. Should think if there was any letter for me there that it would be sent me here. If I do not receive a letter soon I shall write there enquiring about it. I am having a good time here, live well, and can sit and read all night in the office if I choose. The office is my home; there in one corner I keep my knapsack and when night comes spread my blankets on the floor and sleep soundly. One clerk always sleeps with me, but I always go to bed first as he spends his evenings in the city somewhere. We have a carpeted room with a marble mantle, also marble washstand with a spigot of water leading to it. We are in the third story. I hope that you all are well. My health is good. Please write often. The weather is not very warm here yet, still flowers are in bloom. I have seen roses and other flowers in bloom in a park in this city. The mail is about a week for the north. It goes out tomorrow morning. Sometimes the mail is oftener.
>
> From your aff. son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

Charles's description of his office home is quintessentially Charles — precise inventory of comfort. Marble mantle, marble washstand with a spigot, carpeted room, knapsack in the corner, blankets on the floor. "I always go to bed first" while his office-mate spends evenings in the city. The clerk who doesn't go to theatres, doesn't drink beer, goes to bed early. His war has become remarkably comfortable, and he knows it — "I have not earned it as I have done hardly anything."

The Red River optimism will prove catastrophically wrong within a week. Banks's army is heading toward Sabine Crossroads and disaster.
