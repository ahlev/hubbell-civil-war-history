## Full Transcription

> May 27, 1862
> Harpers Ferry
>
> My dear Mother —
>
> I must write you at least two or three words. I am now sitting writing on my knapsack sitting on the ground. We crossed the river this morning and for the first time planted our feet on the sacred soil of Virginia. We are in some expectation of a battle. The rebels are not far distant. We have captured a number of spies. I am in excellent health and spirits. I have sent James 20 dollars. Love to all.
>
> Your affectionate son
>
> Alexander

## Editorial Notes

After months of railroad guard duty and frustrated pleas to see combat, Alexander has finally crossed into Virginia. The brevity — writing on his knapsack on the ground — signals genuine movement. The $20 to James represents Alexander's promise kept: he pledged everything for his brother's West Point outfit, and he's delivering. "Sacred soil of Virginia" was a phrase often used with dark irony by Union soldiers — the soil they were about to bleed on. Alexander's arrival at Harpers Ferry places the 60th NY in the Shenandoah Valley just as Stonewall Jackson's Valley Campaign was reaching its climax.
