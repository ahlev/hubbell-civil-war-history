## Full Transcription

> April 25, 1864
> Hd. Qtrs. Dept of the Gulf
> New Orleans, La.
>
> Dear Alex:
>
> I rec'd your letter of Apr. 10th yesterday and was very glad to hear from you. Have been waiting to hear from you before I wrote, so that I could have your direction. It is a very long direction. Would you not get your letters as quickly if it was shortened?
>
> I am relieved from duty at these Hd. Qtrs, and ordered to rejoin my regt., but have been waiting the last two or three days for the boat. Will probably go off tomorrow. I am to carry the mail on my way back to Gen. Banks Hd. Qtrs. in the field, as by so doing I will receive subsistence on the boat and a state room. This is better than carrying my rations with me and sleeping anywhere. I will have a long ride up the R. River, which is better than footing it as the army have done. Do you see my being here has done me so much good. There has been a great fight near R. River in which we lost a great many men and I am not sorry I was not in that fight. Another battle is expected soon. The army is amassed on R. River at Grand Encore so then I will ride all the way. My penmanship is not good enough is the reason of my going back to the regt. But I feel well enough about going back as others have failed to suit as well as me. Just before I came two men were relieved and now another new clerk that has come, I think, will be relieved as he does not write very well and he has not been set to work.
>
> A clerk here was killed a short time since. He came in drunk one night about 12 o'clock and after spreading his mattress on the floor of the office where he slept, went out on the gallery on the third story, and as he was about todescend a few steps that led to a lower part of the gallery he pitched headlong over the bannister and struck the stone pavement on his head in the yard below. He never moved. A lot of us soon gathered around to see him. He was in his drawers and shirt as he was just going to bed. All the clerks attended at his burial. He was buried by the hospitals. A wagon from the hospital containing eight bodies followed by another wagon containing a corporal and eight men drove into the cemetery. As the chaplain could not be there that day one of the clerks made a prayer, the volleys were soon fired, and we got into the street car and came home.
>
> I am sorry that James does not let us know where he is. Hope he will come out all right. How do you prosper? Are you in good health? Hope you will try and take good care of yourself. Give my regards to Luther. Have you got your watch yet and does it keep good time? I have rec'd but two letters, yours and Mothers, since I left Washington. Expect to find several with the regt.
>
> The street cars here are drawn by one mule and some of them have two stories, that is, people ride on top of the cars and in it. The honesty of the passengers is trusted to put the fare in the box, as there is no conductor.
>
> The Army on the R. River under Gen. Banks expects to soon drive the rebs out of Louisiana and I think will do it. I will be there to help, if not sick. Did you ever hear anything about the dry Tortugas? It is a small island on the coast of Florida where Uncle Sam is building an immense fort. A great many soldiers are sentenced to hard labor on this island for various crimes. A good many are sent from this city, for five or ten years.
>
> I hope you will prove a better correspondent than you have been so far. Do try and write often and I will answer them. Will close by wishing you good luck.
>
> From your aff. brother,
>
> C. F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

The burial scene is Charles at his most cinematic — the wagon with eight bodies from the hospital, another wagon with a corporal and eight-man burial detail, the absent chaplain, a clerk's improvised prayer, the volleys, and then "we got into the street car and came home." The collision of military death ritual and urban convenience — a funeral and a streetcar ride — is pure New Orleans in wartime.

Charles writes to Alexander for the first time in months. "It is a very long direction. Would you not get your letters as quickly if it was shortened?" — the practical brother commenting on military addressing conventions while heading back to a regiment that just fought a battle on the Red River.
