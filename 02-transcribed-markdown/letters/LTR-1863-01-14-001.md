## Full Transcription

> Jan. 14, 1863
> 12th Army Corps, 2nd Division
> 2nd Brigade, In camp near
> Fairfax Station, Va.
>
> Mrs. F. M. Hubbell:
>
> My Dear Friend:
>
> Your letter to hand making inquiry concerning Alexander's going home. I immediately applied to the commanding officer of the Regiment to have him sent home on recruiting service if any sergeant went from the regiment. The matter was favorably considered by him after reading your letter and an explanation by myself. I thought the request was good for him to go home on duty and thus save expense to himself. If that failed, I was determined to apply for a furlough for him, as our circumstances daily looked more and more like "going into winter quarters" here, I though soon to effect one or the other efforts for him to go home. But yesterday we were ordered to be ready to march at 12 hours notice and with eleven days rations. We do not know where we are to go, of course, some think to join Rosecrans, others think towards Fredericksburg, and others still think we will go back to Harper's Ferry. No one knows exactly; it may be the generals and I don't very much that they know. So you see matters of furlough or recruiting service is all done away with for the present least, by orders to march.
>
> I feel confident that I realize in a certain measure your trying anxiety of mind about your sons, and sympathize most sincerely with you and would be glad to do something to aid in relieving your anxiety. But can at present only assure you that Alexander is very well indeed and never looked so finely and well as at the present time. On Friday last I was down to the 153d Reg't. and say your son Charles, who looked well, too, and is in good health, he says, except a cold. Having many things to attend to in order to be fully ready to march when positively ordered, I write in haste. Please excuse. With much love to yourself and Frances, I am
>
> Your friend,
>
> James H. Ransom
>
> P.S. Since writing the above, Charles arrived in camp to visit us. Is well and looks finely. Will return tomorrow at 7:00 A.M.

## Editorial Notes

This is the only letter in the collection from an officer writing directly to the Hubbell mother. Captain Ransom's tone is remarkable — compassionate, respectful, actively trying to help. He applied to the commanding officer on Alexander's behalf, considered both recruiting service and furlough options, and writes with genuine sympathy. The P.S. — "Charles arrived in camp to visit us" — catches the moment in real time, a snapshot of the brothers reuniting that both Charles and Ransom documented from different angles.

The date discrepancy between Charles's letter (dated Jan 2 but describing a Jan 14 visit) likely means Charles wrote the letter over multiple days or misdated it. Ransom's Jan 14 date, with the P.S. about Charles's arrival, is probably the accurate timeline.
