## Full Transcription

> May 3rd, 1864
> Alexandria, La.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I arrived here yesterday and am now back with the regt. Went on board of a steamer, Monday, April 25th at N. Orleans, but after staying some time on her she was ordered not to leave so I went on board of another steamer which left Wednesday afternoon. I carried despatches for Gen. Banks. I occupied a state room with two mail carriers, and we ate at the table with those that paid for a meal. The soldiers on the boat drew their rations and cooked them themselves. The living at the table was nothing extra. We stopped at Port Hudson a short time. The boat was a slow one and it was on the 4th day after we left N. Orleans that we arrived at this place. We ought to have gotten here in two or three days. There were two or three hundred soldiers on board all armed to be prepared for the guerrillas. Only a short time before a steamer on the Mississippi was fired into and two men were killed and 11 wounded. After getting into Red River we were told to report to the gunboat at the mouth of Black River and if necessary a convoy would go with us. On arriving at the mouth of Black River we were told to wait for a convoy which we did one day and night and then as no gunboat was ready to go with us we started without any. There was a great difference in the color of the Red & Black rivers where the Black flows into the Red. The water of the Red is reddish as if mixed with red clay. We passed by some cattle on the shore when the Lieut. Col. commanding ordered the boat to be run ashore and a beef to be shot and dragged on board. One beef was shot and dragged on board with a rope. But three soldiers that went into the woods chasing the cattle after another beef did not come back. The boat whistled and waited for them, but as they did not come back we finally came off without them. It is supposed they were shot or taken prisoners by the guerrillas. This was on the bank of the Red River.
>
> We passed the night at Fort DeRussy and the next morning in convoy with a transport that had three cannon and with a gunboat in the rear we started for this place. When about thirty-five miles from here we had a fight. There was one man shot dead and one wounded on our boat. The rebs had one cannon and the gunboat soon dismantled it. The guerrillas kept up a musketry fire on us some time. As I had my gun I fired it into the woods but did not see any rebs except one who came out of the woods and fired at the gunboat. He had a breastwork on the boat made of boxes of hardtack. As the banks of the river here high we could not see much but those on the hurricane deck had a good view of the rebs. It was there the men were shot on our boat. Two or three bullets came near where I was.
>
> I found four letters for me with the Regt., one from Alex. Alex Douglass sent three to N. Orleans, but as he directed them to the "Provost Marshall" of course I did not receive them. Our Captain thought I was there, and as I did not write to the regt. it was partly on one fault. The regt. look thin in flesh. A. Douglass is not very well. I never saw so much dust as there is here. Then I arrived the regt. were out on a foraging expedition and I never saw such looking men. They were covered with dust half an inch thick when they came in.
>
> I write this sitting on the ground so you must excuse the penmanship. Please write soon. I wish you would send me a newspaper more in a while as there is nothing to read here. Direct your letters to N. Orleans, La., Co. and Regt., of course. The reason why I did not receive the letters you directed to Hd. Qtrs. was because the Co. and Regt. were on the letters.
>
> From your aff. son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell

## Cross-Reference Notes

### Connections to Other Letters in Collection
- **Charles under fire:** After seven months of garrison duty and two months as a clerk, Charles is finally in combat — firing his gun from behind hardtack boxes at guerrillas on the Red River. The contrast with his comfortable office is stark.
- **Three soldiers lost:** The cattle-chasing soldiers who didn't come back — "it is supposed they were shot or taken prisoners" — are among the war's invisible casualties, lost not in battle but in a foraging incident on a riverbank.
- **Red River Campaign:** Banks's army is retreating to Alexandria after Sabine Crossroads. The 153rd NY is there when Charles arrives — foraging, dusty, thin.

## Editorial Notes

Charles's Red River journey is the most action-packed passage he's written — steamer fired upon, men killed, guerrillas in the woods, three soldiers vanished chasing cattle, a rebel cannon dismantled by a gunboat, bullets near his position. "As I had my gun I fired it into the woods" — the clerk-turned-soldier fires his weapon in combat for what may be the first time. He fires from behind boxes of hardtack.

The three soldiers who went into the woods after cattle and never came back are one of the collection's most haunting small moments. The boat whistled and waited. They didn't come. "It is supposed they were shot or taken prisoners by the guerrillas." The Red River swallowed them.
