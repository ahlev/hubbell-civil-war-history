## Full Transcription

> Dec. 26, 1861
> Camp McClellan
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I received a letter from you Christmas Eve. Glad to know that you are all well. We are having cold weather, but no snow. Our Co. came off from picket this morning, was on for twenty-four hours. Went on Christmas morning.
>
> Dec. 27th. Today is quite warm from what it has been. I have bought a stove for our tent. It cost $3.50. I advanced the money. It was so cold that I could not get along without it. The stove is made of sheet iron. There will be twenty-one shillings coming to me for the stove next pay day. It cost seven shillings apiece for four of us. I shall try to send home next pay day $25.00. We get paid off by the 15th of Jan. We are having barracks built for our Regt. There are so many men detailed for making them, but I have not been yet. They get extra pay for it. The soldiers do all the wood cutting. The teamsters draw it. It takes a great quantity to supply the Regt.
>
> We have the same Chaplain now that we had at first. Have a prayer meeting every Sunday evening, but I have not been yet, for it so happened, that most every Sabbath I have been on guard. There is not much of any Sabbath here.
>
> I find our stove very comfortable. You need have no fear but what I shall get my money back. I have received a letter from Alex. He seems to like soldiering very well. He has received that letter I wrote him although the direction was not quite correct. He wrote that Capt. Ransom had a narrow escape. I suppose he has written you all about it. I have not much of news to write you. You get it up North faster than we do. I hope Silas will get that accordian. I shall be very careful of it.
>
> I have nothing more to write. I shall be more careful after this and not have so much bad spelling. Sometimes I have to write in a hurry and do not take so much pains as I might or as I should. We have in our Camp a man who takes pictures. He has got the whole rigging.
>
> I do my own washing every week and try to keep myself as clean as I can. Most all the soldiers do their washing. Last summer I washed in cold water, but now I have it warmed.
>
> I remain your aff. Son,
>
> Henry

## Editorial Notes

This letter closes a major narrative thread: Henry finally received a letter from Alexander. The brothers, who have been unable to correspond since September despite being 40 miles apart, have reconnected — the "not quite correct" direction worked after all. Henry's relay of Alexander's news about Ransom's "narrow escape" confirms the boiler explosion story traveled through the family network. The stove purchase ($3.50, split four ways) and barracks construction mark the deepening of winter quarters. Henry's self-consciousness about spelling ("I shall be more careful after this") is endearing — he's writing to his mother, aware that the family reads these letters carefully. The camp photographer detail is evocative: a man with a full wet-plate setup working in camp, making portraits available to soldiers who might not survive to sit for another.
