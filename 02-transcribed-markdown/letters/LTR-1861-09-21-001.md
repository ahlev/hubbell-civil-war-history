## Full Transcription

> Sept. 21, 1861
> Near Washington, D.C.
>
> Dear Frances —
>
> I meant to have written before, but was interrupted, expect of course, you will excuse me this time.
>
> We have had very pleasant weather thus far, except last night and today it has rained very fast. It has been so warm that I have been obliged to keep my coat off in the day time, but it gets cold at night. You wished to know who it was that directed my letter to you. It was Edgar Graves from Chazy Village. Is well acquainted with Mrs. Brinkhoff. Wished to be remembered to her, etc., etc. It was his desire to direct it so I let him.
>
> I have not much news to relate except while on Picket I saw what they call a Signal thrown up over in Virginia — looked somewhat like the evening star, only it was a dark red — looked very fine. Only wish you could have seen us while at supper on picket. Lieutenant Scott was with us, had a merry time of it. Tell Mother I will not need any clothing, I have more than I can carry now. Please write all the news. I will write more next time. I have no more room now.
>
> I remain your aff Brother
>
> Henry
