## Full Transcription

> June 12th, 1865
> Savannah, Ga.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I write in haste so that this letter will go by the steamer that leaves tonight. Our Regt. left Washington June 5th & landed here this morning. We lay all day yesterday at the mouth of the river waiting for the tide to come in & last night just before dark we came to the city. The river has been very well obstructed. At one place piles were driven all the way across the river except a small opening in the center. Near these piles were two or three forts.
>
> Yesterday while lying at the mouth of the river & while in swimming one of our men was caught by the tide & was rapidly being carried out to sea. A life preserver was thrown him & a boat rowed by marines went to his rescue. He was picked up after being badly scared.
>
> The city looks deserted not much business being done now. I don't know whether our regt. will stay here or go into the interior of the state, but I think it will stay here.
>
> Have you heard from Alex lately, how does he get along? Have you paid Mrs. Joseph Martin twenty dollars as I requested. Please let me know. My three years is on the 17th of Oct. Don't know whether we will be mustered out before that or not.
>
> I hope you and Frances are well & that you will both pass a pleasant summer. Please write soon & tell us all the news. James says he will write next time.
>
> From your Aff. Son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell
