# Letter: Henry Hubbell to Mother — July 6, 1861 (mislabeled "June 6")

> Confidence notation: `basis · legibility · source-certainty`. See [CONFIDENCE-GUIDE.md](../CONFIDENCE-GUIDE.md).

> **⚠ DATING CORRECTION:** This letter is dated "June 6th, 1861" on the page, but is almost certainly from **July 6, 1861**. Evidence: (1) The 34th NY left Albany July 3 and arrived Washington July 5 per regimental history — Henry cannot have been in Washington on June 6. (2) Henry writes "arrived Friday eve" — July 5, 1861 was a Friday; June 5 was a Wednesday. (3) Henry's letters dated June 16, 19, and 27 are from Albany, where the regiment was still mustering. He cannot have been in Washington on June 6 and back in Albany on June 16. The likely error: Henry or the 1947-1949 transcriber wrote "June" instead of "July" in the first days after arriving in Washington. File retains original ID for traceability; correct date: **July 6, 1861**.

## Full Transcription

> June 6th, 1861
> Washington, D.C.
>
> Dear Mother -
>
> We arrived at Washington Friday eve about ten. We took the boat at Albany and went down the river some ways past New York, were not allowed to go into the City. It seems to me I never got into such a drunken set as I have here, however there are some that don't drink. After we left the boat we took the cars, and then went to Baltimore. Our regiment was cheered all the way from Albany to Washington. We marched half a mile through Baltimore and then took the train for Washington. It is said our regiment was the best looking that came into Washington.
>
> No one attempted to molest us going through Baltimore, but rather cheered us. In the depot I saw several holes in the roof where there had been a row. There was one volunteer accidentally killed by another letting his gun fall when it was loaded. Twenty of our company had their guns loaded going through Baltimore in case of attack, but it was useless. It took thirty cars to carry our regiment and two engines to draw the train. I have not been around the city yet, hope to before long and get my likeness taken. Had not time in Albany.
>
> I hope when you write you will let me know whether you received that money, was eight dollars. You must excuse bad spelling and improper grammar. I am writing where there is a great noise. Going through Mariland I saw some very fine farms and some fine grain. We had three day rations given us from New York to Washington.
>
> I would have been very glad to have gone home for three or four days, and spoke to Brink about it, but he said it would cost me twelve dollars, thought it would not pay. If I had known it would have cost only five dollars I would have gone. However, perhaps it is all for the best. I know you will need the money at home. Several of our company have spent their money for drinking, some have had it stolen from them.
>
> I hope you will write all the news. I find it very heavy to carry the knapsack, but shall get use to it. I shall not write any more just now. Please write soon.
>
> I remain your aff Son
>
> Henry
>
> P.S. I cannot tell definitely how you should direct your letters, but you might write Care of Capt. Reich, Co. D., 34th Regt., Washington. Can tell more definitely when we get into Camp.

## Editorial Notes

**CORRECTED DATE: July 6, 1861** (not June 6). This letter was written the day after the 34th NY arrived in Washington (Friday, July 5). The regiment left Albany July 3 and traveled via New York and Baltimore. The "June" label is a writing or transcription error — Henry was still in Albany being mustered on June 15-16. See Corrected Chronology table above for full sequence.

The passage through Baltimore is historically significant: approximately 77 days earlier (April 19), the 6th Massachusetts had been attacked by a pro-Confederate mob, killing 4 soldiers and 12 civilians. Henry notes bullet holes in the depot roof and that 20 men had guns loaded "in case of attack" — but Baltimore "rather cheered" the 34th NY. The political/military situation in Baltimore had shifted significantly by July.

Henry's concern about drunkenness, theft, and financial responsibility to his widowed mother is a recurring theme that begins in this first letter. The 34th NY included one company from Clinton County (where Champlain is located) — this was Henry's Co. D.
