## Full Transcription

> Feb. 28, 1862
> Harpers Ferry
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> Our Regt. Left Camp McClellan on the 26th. We marched through Poolesville and from there to Bainsville and from there to Adamstown. We took the cars then to Harpers Ferry. We arrived about three in the afternoon. After a couple of hours we crossed over the river to Harpers Ferry. We went over on a floating bridge of boats. You no doubt remember of the rebles burning the bridge. Nothing but the stone piers remain. It really made me feel bad to see what distruction the rebles have done in this place. Several buildings burnt. Nothing but the brick walls remain. I also saw the Arsenal that was burnt by our boys, four or five thousand stand of Arms distroyed, so that the rebles could not get them I suppose.
>
> The first day we marched 20 miles with our knapsacks. Felt rather tired at night. Next morning we started at seven and marched about five miles to Adams-Town. It is about 30 miles from there to Harpers Ferry. Our Regt. is now in a brick building to recruit for a while. I should judge there was about 60 thousand troops in this place. We may see some fun before long. I do not care how soon, sooner the better. I have got so now I don't mind marching 20 or 30 miles with my knapsack, but of course I get very tired. Some of the boys have thrown away their blankets, but I mean to keep all I have.
>
> When our Regt. crossed the river I heard several remark that our Regt. was the best looking that crossed over. We have a good band to our Regt. I have not been around town much yet, for there are guards posted all around. I am on today. Gen McClellan and staff are here, but I have not seen him yet.
>
> I have nothing more to write at present. I shall write as often as I can. Please excuse all mistakes as I write this in a hurry. Do not feel anxious.
>
> I remain your aff. Son,
>
> Henry

### ⚠ DATING DISCREPANCY — RESOLVED

**This letter resolves the dating discrepancy identified across LTR-1862-02-05-001, -002, -003.**

Henry states: "Our Regt. Left Camp McClellan on the 26th." This is February 26, 1862.

**Timeline reconstruction:**
1. **Feb 24** — Henry at Camp McClellan, orders read at dress parade (LTR-1862-02-24-001)
2. **Feb 26** — Regiment departs Camp McClellan (this letter)
3. **Feb 26-27** — March to Harpers Ferry via Poolesville → Barnesville → Adamstown → freight train
4. **Feb 28** — Henry writes from Harpers Ferry (this letter)

**Conclusion:** The three letters dated "Feb 5" from Bolivar Heights/Camp Gorman (LTR-1862-02-05-001 to Frances, LTR-1862-02-05-002 to Charles, LTR-1862-02-05-003 to Mother) were actually written **after Feb 28** — likely late February or early March. The "Feb 5" dates are transcription errors, possibly misreadings of "Feb 28" or "Mar 5." The original 1947-49 transcriber may have misread Henry's handwriting.

## Editorial Notes

This is the most important letter for establishing the chronology of Henry's movements in early 1862. The unambiguous statement "Our Regt. Left Camp McClellan on the 26th" proves the departure date was Feb 26, 1862. The three Bolivar Heights letters dated "Feb 5" must be misdated — Henry was at Camp McClellan through at least Feb 24 (LTR-1862-02-24-001). The march route matches the other accounts: Poolesville → Barnesville → Adamstown → freight train → Harpers Ferry. Henry's estimate of 60,000 troops is notable — this was a major concentration of Union forces. The Arsenal "burnt by our boys" refers to Lt. Roger Jones's destruction of the armory on April 18, 1861, nearly a year earlier. Henry saw the ruins for the first time.
