## Full Transcription

> May 6, 1863
> Camp somewhere in
> the State of Georgia
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I now hasten to write you a few lines to let you know of my welfare. This is my first opportunity of writing you since I left Stevenson. We have been marching hard — very hard for the last few days.
>
> I rec'd the boots when I reached Bridgeport, Ala. I was very much pleased with them, but most unfortunately they proved a little too small for me (as my used to feet will testify) and I have sold them to young Lafountain from Champlain for $8.50. I have now an excellent pair of Govt. bootees and I find them much better for marching than boots.
>
> The sugar was most excellent and you can well imagine how much I enjoyed it. I am very much obliged to you for sending it. The stripes are very nice indeed.
>
> We are close upon the enemy. Our advance came upon them this morning but they fell back. I hardly think they are in much force. We have between thirty-five and forty thousand men here under Hooker. We are, I believe, on the extreme right of the army.
>
> I will write you as often as possible. Culver Bostwick is here. He reached the regt. yesterday morning and he wishes me to tell his friends that he would write with me but that he has drawn a new gun and is too busy cleaning it.
>
> The mail is going out in a moment and I must close.
>
> Your aff. son,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell
>
> P.S. Keep a stout heart.

## Cross-Reference Notes

### Connections to Other Letters in Collection
- **"State of Georgia" mystery:** The 11th and 12th Corps were transferred from Virginia to Tennessee/Georgia in late September 1863 — NOT in May 1863. The "Georgia" heading is either (a) censorship/misdirection, (b) a typewriter error, or (c) confusion. In May 1863, the 60th NY was at Chancellorsville. Stevenson and Bridgeport (Alabama) suggest the 60th may have passed through the area en route, but this is chronologically problematic.
- **"Keep a stout heart":** Alexander echoes his own advice to Mother. He said "keep up a stout heart" in the Feb 21 letter. Now, near the enemy, he says it again.
- **Alexander survived Chancellorsville:** This letter, written May 6, confirms he came through the May 1-3 battle.

## Editorial Notes

A letter from the field — short, urgent, written as "the mail is going out in a moment." The "State of Georgia" heading is puzzling: the 60th NY was at Chancellorsville in May 1863, not Georgia. The Alabama references (Stevenson, Bridgeport) complicate the picture — these are real places the 12th Corps would pass through, but not until fall 1863. The typewritten transcription may have conflated headers from different letters, or Alexander wrote the wrong state deliberately to confuse Confederate mail interceptors. "Keep a stout heart" — the last two words before battle.
