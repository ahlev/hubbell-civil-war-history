## Full Transcription

> Apr. 28, 1864
> Stevenson, Ala.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> We leave here in a day or two — perhaps tomorrow — for the front. The eastern troops in this Department have too good a name for bold unflinching bravery to allow them to lie still while the rest of the Army is fighting. Battles will probably be fought in a few weeks that will not be a whit behind, and perhaps throw completely in the shade the battles of Antietam and Gettysburg. May God be with us and grant victory to our arms. I hope the people at the North will keep a stout heart. The soldiers in the field will do their best to be equal to the emergency. It is the death struggle of this mighty rebellion. I hope, dear Mother, that you will not worry yourself about me. Our Father in Heaven is watching over us all and nothing will happen to us but what is for the best. I hope I shall come safely out of the struggle — if not, I trust that we shall all meet in the better world.
>
> I rec'd your letter of the 18th inst. day before yesterday and should have answered it immediately but several little matters interspersed and prevented my answering until now. Hope you will pardon the delay. I think if you will examine the letters I have written since I have been here you will find that instead of my being in debt it is a little the other way, see but then I shouldn't care to answer to it. I shall write you as often as possible. I have not as yet rec'd. the boots, but expect they will be along in the course of time. I hope I shall get them before we march. If we do not move soon I will endeavor to write you another letter from Stevenson. Love to all, particularly to Frances.
>
> Your aff. son,
>
> Alexander F. Hubbell
>
> P.S. Have you heard from James? If not, I do not think you need have cause to worry for he probably is waiting until he gets himself well going so that he will be able to mollify in a measure your regret at his failure. I am glad to hear you think Charles will remain at Hd. Qtrs. I hope he will write me soon. I am a little vexed at him. I have not heard from him for seven or eight months. I have written him twice since I have been here. Enclosed I send a slip from a Louisville paper that you may know our Div. and Div. Commander are up noticed out here. You see that it means we shall be in our share to do. Bridgeport is nine miles from here and is the headquarters of the Div.

## Editorial Notes

This is Alexander's testament. "It is the death struggle of this mighty rebellion." He explicitly prepares Mother for his death — "if not, I trust that we shall all meet in the better world" — and then immediately softens it with domestic reassurance about letters and boots. The soldier's art of saying goodbye without saying goodbye.

"Battles will probably be fought in a few weeks that will not be a whit behind, and perhaps throw completely in the shade the battles of Antietam and Gettysburg." He was right. The Atlanta Campaign — Resaca, New Hope Church, Kennesaw Mountain — would be among the war's most sustained and brutal fighting.
