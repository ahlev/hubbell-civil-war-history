## Full Transcription

> May 9th, 1862
> West Point
> Camp Windfield Scott
>
> Dear Frances:
>
> I believe this is the first letter I have written to you since I left home, almost a year ago. I am now at a place called West Point, not West Point where the Military Academy is, but a place on the York River, twenty or thirty miles from York Town and I should think about thirty from Richmond. We left York Town on the seventh on a large boat — the whole Regt. on one boat. We were very much crowded.
>
> York Town has been evacuated by the rebels. Their intrenchments and breastworks are very large and extensive. Our artillery commenced firing on Wednesday the sixteenth of April. I have kept a journal of our doings here and will send you a copy one of these days. It will give you a better idea of what we have been doing than a letter.
>
> Royal Corbin and George are here in the 16th Regt. I saw them the other day. Sid is also here. I was very glad to see them.
>
> Write often. I remain your aff. Brother,
>
> Henry

## Editorial Notes

Henry's arrival at West Point, Virginia — where the Mattaponi and Pamunkey form the York River — coincides with the Battle of Eltham's Landing (May 7), though he doesn't describe combat. His careful distinction between West Point, VA and the Military Academy is poignant given James's West Point ambitions. The artillery date (Wednesday April 16) is a precise anchoring detail for the Yorktown siege timeline. The journal, now mentioned four times with repeated promises to send a copy, is one of the collection's great lost documents — Henry's daily diary of the Peninsula Campaign from approximately March 25 to at least late April. If it existed when Henry fell at Antietam, it may have been returned with his effects or lost on the battlefield.
