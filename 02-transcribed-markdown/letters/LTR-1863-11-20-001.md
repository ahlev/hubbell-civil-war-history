## Full Transcription

> Have you heard from James and Alex lately? James has owed me a letter for some time, but I suppose he is very busy in the reason. My health is good and I hope that I will stand the winter. But we may not have much of one, but may ride. How is your health and Frances and how does the world go with you both? Hope that you have wood enough to keep warm this cold weather. The weather is warm and pleasant here now. The cold rains are to come.
>
> Nov. 21st — 2:00 P.M. Enclosed is a twenty dollar check. I wish you would use my money freely — as much as you want of it. Our Col. got back last night. They say that he is trying to get our reg't. to Philadelphia, but that we will probably go to Alexandria. Also it is said that Major Strange doesn't like Alexandria. No signs of leaving here yet. I don't think we will go to the front this fall or winter, but to some other place. It has rained all day, but a great many of the men are on passes in this city. Our clothing acct's for 14 months was settled with us yesterday when we got our pay. I rec'd. $2.70 due me for clothes, but some of the men were largely in debt for clothes. We receive $3.50 a month from date of enlistment which is $42 a year. Please write soon.
>
> From your aff. son
>
> C. F. Hubbell
>
> Nov. 23rd — as I was about sending my letter off two days since I put my hand in my pocket to get my check out of my pocketbook, but it was not there. It was lost, and this is the reason that I delayed sending the letter till today. Last night the pocket book was returned to me with the check, but the money was taken out of it. They said they found it without any money in it and that the person that first found it had taken the money. I lost $18.78 in all. I wrote in haste so the mail is going out, but will tell you all about it in my next letter. I must have pulled the pocket book out of my pocket with my knife. If you can send me $2 I will be much obliged. I hope you will not tell all creation about my losing this money. It has not bothered me much. My appetite and sleep are as good as usual. It is now after 1 P.M., and we have just returned our guns. Are to get new ones. I don't know the object of the change as we have very good guns. Are to get more new ones. There is a story here that the Col. has his choice to go to Alexandria or Chattanooga and that he does not want to go to Alexandria. I don't know when he will leave here. I don't know what all the soldier's wives will do, if we go far.
>
> Your aff. son,
>
> C. F. Hubbell.
>
> Please let me know soon if you receive the check.
