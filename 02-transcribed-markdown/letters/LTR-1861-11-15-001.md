## Full Transcription

> Nov. 15, 1861
> Camp McClellan
>
> Dear Charles:
>
> As I have a little leisure I thought I would write a few lines. It is cold enough today to wear overcoats. We cannot have much pleasant weather now. It rained all last night. The mud is not so deep here as it is at Champlain when it rains. The soil here is sandy, some of it has a redish color.
>
> I have sent home $20.00 by express. I put it in a letter and directed it to Mother, and then gave it to the Adjutant. He is going to Washington today or tomorrow and will there send it by express. I thought that was the safest way to send it. Please be on the lookout and see that mine is not lost. I suppose the package will be sent either to B. Moore or G. Dunning.
>
> I am glad to know that we own 100 acres near Reiders, as the sugar bush is so far away, 50 acres is enough off there. So Silas and Sid are selling more land. They had better look out or they will want money one of these days.
>
> I cannot tell now where our Regt. will go for winter quarters. We may see a battle before winter. I heard last night that there were five Regts. of the rebels in Georgia that resigned because they could not get their pay and could not get enough to eat. Uncle Sam takes care of his soldiers very well. We have plenty to eat and of the right sort. We have bread every two days. The rest of the time we have crackers. Have very good meta, fresh meat now and then.
>
> The other night our Regt. and the other Regts. were formed in line of battle, and ready to go down to the river, as the e was a report that the rebles were crossing the river, but it was not so. We are liable to be called up any night. We have four in our tent. Pretty well filled up. We have new blankets, very heavy. Sleep quite warm nights.
>
> I am surprised to hear of Albert Savage's death. His father must feel very bad. I have nothing more to write now. When you write, please write all the news. I like to hear of my friends.
>
> I remain your aff. Brother,
>
> Henry
>
> Nov. 16th — I thought I would add a few lines before I seal this up. Last night it froze one or two inches. Quite cold this morning. Very hard wind. My money will not go to Washington till Monday. I suppose it will be about three days going to Champlain from Washington. Tell Mother that those preserves went very well. I fold this letter this way because it is so large. I am going to try and get a pass and go to Poolsville Monday.

## Editorial Notes

Henry has moved from the Washington defenses to Camp McClellan — almost certainly in the Poolesville/Upper Potomac area, exactly as rumored in his Oct 17 letter. The false alarm (rebels crossing the river) reflects the heightened tension after Ball's Bluff (Oct 21), which occurred in this exact area. The land discussion — 100 acres near "Reiders," sugar bush, Silas and Sid selling more land — provides rare insight into the family's economic position: they were landowners with a sugar maple operation, not subsistence farmers. Henry's $20 remittance (following earlier $25 and $5 gold piece) shows steady financial support flowing home despite his private's pay. The contrast with his report of starving Confederate regiments in Georgia serves both morale and propaganda purposes.
