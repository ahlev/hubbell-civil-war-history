## Full Transcription

> Sept. 27th/61
>
> Dear Charles —
>
> I received your letter last evening. I am getting along pretty well now. I have had the rheumatism very bad, am over it now. The reason I have not written about it before is I could not spell the word. It is a long word to spell. I hope you will always correct all bad spelling.
>
> We have got a floor to our tent. It cost 95 cts. — that would make little over 30 cts for three of us. I should probably have the rheumatism now if we had not got the floor. I had it so bad before that, I could hardly walk, but I am all right now. It is raining quite hard today and it has rained all night. We do not have so much of it here as up North.
>
> So Alexander is off for the war. Well, good luck to him. I rather think he will get sick of it when he comes to go on guard duty, but perhaps he wont. I was cut four hours in the rain on guard last night, but I have got so I don't mind it much. I came off from picket day before yesterday, was on with Lieutenant Scott and H. Cooper and Sergeant H. There were also two privates besides myself to stand guard, three hours and a half at a time. Had a very pleasant time while on picket. Our Regiment has not drilled for some time, but we will have three drills every day after this besides dress parade.
>
> So you have heard about the attack over the river, but you did not hear it quite strait. There were not three Comp. of Rebels, but only thirty-five against twelve. I wonder you did not hear there was a thousand. We fare very well now, have bread every day, have a large oven to bake it in. We get our rations every five days. Each man gives two shillings a month for cooking. We have fresh meat quite often.
>
> [page break]
>
> Contd. Sept. 27, 1861
>
> You wished to know how the men pass the time. Thus far they have played cards, but that is not allowed now. The Col. has forbidden it. There was to much gambling going on, so the boys can not play now.
>
> I have nothing more to write now. Please send me a paper if it is convenient.
>
> I remain your aff Brother
>
> Henry

## Editorial Notes

Henry's letter to Charles has a different tone from his letters to Mother and Frances — more casual, more humorous ("the reason I have not written about it before is I could not spell the word"), and more willing to correct exaggerated war reports. The rheumatism episode reveals the health toll of sleeping on bare ground, and the 95-cent tent floor as a medical intervention. The rations detail (bread oven, fresh meat, 2 shillings/month for cooking) and the card-playing ban paint a rich picture of the regiment's daily economy and social regulation.
