## Full Transcription

> May 9th, 1862
> West Point on the York River
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> We are now twenty five or thirty miles from Richmond. We came from York Town to this place on one boat, about six hours. The place where Cornwallis surrendered was pointed out to me. It was a great sight. The 16th Regt. has been in a skirmish. I heard that one of the companies lost sixteen, several were wounded. George Corbin and Royal shook hands with me. I was very glad to see them.
>
> How do things get along at home? I hope the garden and farm will be attended to. I worry about these things. I think the farm had better be let out on shares. If you could spend the summer at Charlotte it would be very pleasant and much better for you. I hope you will make a visit it will do you so much good. How does Sid come on with his farming. I am afraid he will not make much of a farmer. Silas, I suppose, has a good deal of business by this time.
>
> Write all the news and do not feel anxious if you do not happen to hear from me regularly, for the mail doesn't go very regular. I have not received a paper from home since I left Hampton, and that was stolen before I read half of it. I shall send part of my Journal next time.
>
> Your aff. Son,
>
> Henry

## Editorial Notes

Henry seeing the Cornwallis surrender spot from the York River connects this war to the first — a connection Alexander had made intellectually a month earlier ("it seems strange that Yorktown, once invested by the forces under Washington and DeRochambeau, should again be invested by American troops"). Now Henry sees the physical place. The 16th Regiment's losses at the Battle of Eltham's Landing (May 7) — one company losing 16 — bring the war's cost close to home through George Corbin and Royal, friends from Clinton County. Henry's practical advice to let the farm out on shares and send Mother to Charlotte for the summer shows him managing family affairs from 500 miles away. The journal promise — "I shall send part of my Journal next time" — is the fifth mention and the most specific promise yet. The stolen newspaper ("before I read half of it") shows how scarce information was at the front.
