## Full Transcription

> Sept. 2d, 1864
> U. S. General Hospital
> Chattanooga, Tenn.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> The evening of the day I wrote you my last letter, I with a large number of other sick and wounded was put on board a hospital train and sent to this place. We were two nights and a day getting here. We should have gone directly to Nashville, but old Wheeler had made a raid and torn up the road so we were obliged to stop here. I do not know how long we shall remain here. Generally as soon as the men are able to hear it they send them on to Nashville. I was pretty sick yesterday and last night, but am very much better today. I shall be able to join the regt. before a great while, I think. Considerable anxiety is felt to know who the Presidential nominee is at the Chicago convention the 29th inst. We will know in a day or two. There is nothing of interest to write. Send your letters to the regt. as usual. Love to all.
>
> Your aff. son,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell.

## Editorial Notes

September 2, 1864 — the day Atlanta falls. Alexander is in a hospital in Chattanooga, moved there by hospital train because Wheeler's cavalry raiders tore up the Nashville railroad. He writes a brief, tired note about feeling sick and wondering who the Democrats will nominate in Chicago. He doesn't know that on this day, the city he shelled from 1¼ miles away, the city he saw from a ridge with its spires and chimneys, has been entered by his own 20th Corps. The campaign is over. He missed the ending.
