## Full Transcription

> October 11, 1863
> Army of the Cumberland
> Headquarters 60th N.Y. Vol.
> Murfreesboro, Tenn.
>
> Dear Fannie:
>
> I am highly provoked with you! Here you have excited my curiosity to its highest pitch by telling me that something "very, very funny has happened" — and then wind up by telling me that you "dare not trust it to paper." A fine idea, isn't it? But perfectly womanlike. Don't you think I shall have my pay for this? Wait and see. I expect it was something about Captain Ran. The "Baltimore widows" had something to do with it or rather you teased him about them — eh! I wish I knew something you would like to find out. How I would tease you!
>
> You say you received two military novels. What were they and how do you like them? When you find out who they are from, please let me know. My curiosity is strongly excited. You say they were directed on the back to Serg. A. F. Hubbell and then crossed out. Let me know as soon as you can find out.
>
> Well, Miss Fannie, I do not find Tennessee very much different from the rest of the world, only some of the forts here are built partly of cotton bales. I believe they do not use that material very extensively in York State for such purposes. I saw, while coming from Nashville to this place, the first large fields of cotton I ever saw. I never saw but one small patch while I was in Va. I shall endeavor to write again soon. Please write often. Love to all.
>
> Your aff. brother,
>
> Alexander F. Hubbell
>
> I intended to have written Mother in this letter, but it is so late that it will not be possible. I have got to fly around if I intend to have supper before dark. P.S. We are now stationed here as garrison to the town. General Greene (commander of my Brigade) a commandant of all the forces stationed here, infantry, cavalry, and artillery.

## Editorial Notes

Alexander's first letter from Tennessee is pure Frances-teasing — barely a word about the war or the journey from Virginia. The cotton bale forts are his only concession to local color: "I believe they do not use that material very extensively in York State for such purposes." The understatement is characteristically Alexander.

The mysterious military novels directed to "Serg. A. F. Hubbell" and then crossed out suggest a secret admirer — or at least someone in camp sending books to Frances through Alexander's name. The subplot never fully resolves in the collection.
