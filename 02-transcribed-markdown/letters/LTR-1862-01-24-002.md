## Full Transcription

> January 24th, 1862
> Camp McClellan
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> We have been paid off and I shall send home $15.00 this time. I should like to send more but can not very well do so. My bill to the sutler was $5.20. I bought a pr. of boots which came to $4.50 and other necessary's. Then I owe 50 cts. to the band and 50 cts. to the cook and $1.00 for the sword, so that I could not send home as much as I should like to. Next time I shall send home more. In regard to the stove, I shall have to wait till next pay day before I can get paid. I have had several applications to lend money, but I would rather send it home.
>
> We have had pretty warm weather for the last ten days. It is beginning to grow colder now. The mud has been very deep. I hope it will freeze up.
>
> In my last letter I wrote about getting a discharge, but I think this war will be all over before next July from what I have read in the papers and from what I have heard. It seems that we have had a glorious victory in Kentucky. With many such victory's the war can not last long. My health is pretty good now. The barracks have been taken and burnt for fire wood as it was not thought healthy to live in them. We will therefore live in our tents the rest of the winter. I do not know of naything more to write at present. McDonel is in as Corporal.
>
> I remain your aff. Son,
>
> Henry

## Editorial Notes

Henry's financial accounting reveals soldier economics: $13/month pay, minus $5.20 sutler, $1 sword installment, $1 band and cook, leaving $15 to send home. He refuses to lend money in camp — "I would rather send it home." His optimism about the war ending by July (after Mill Springs) is poignant: he had eight months to live. The barracks — recently built — were burnt for firewood because they were unhealthy, putting the men back in tents in January. McDonel's promotion to corporal fills a vacancy in Co. D.
