## Full Transcription

> Feb. 28, 1863
> Aquia Landing
>
> My dear Mother and Sister:
>
> It is raining quite furiously here today and as a natural consequence we are not at work on the fortifications, much to my satisfaction. As I told you when I was at home, all I have to do is to oversee the men. We are erecting several forts (of earth) and when they are all completed this will make quite an impregnable position. We have a half-pint of whiskey every day. I do not drink or profess, but give it to the boys. You need have no fear of my ever drinking it. The very smell sickens me and what is more than all, I am bound by a resolve that I held to be most sacred in the world — a resolve to my mother. Well, about my writing — I am living it all over again in my imagination, which you know is quite vivid, and am enjoying it almost as much as when I was there. So you remember, Miss Fannie, hear these presentations: Frenchmen have out of the road, and your humble servant presents rather an undignified appearance whenever that comes into his mind. Wonder how Charley and the big, fat Frenchman get along — wonder if he been him warm still these cold nights? Rich I could see Charley and give him those socks. They will keep his warm.
>
> How do matters and things progress around Champlain? Has pony recovered from his journey to Rouse's Point? I feel very much provoked when I think of that for I should have had ample time to have obtained my ticket and even had time to spare but I taken the care at Champlain, and you, Mother, might have been spared that miserable ride. But time did you get home that night? Tell Willie to take good care of Pony. I will get him a comb and brush as soon as possible. While in New York, when endeavoring to get a bill out of my wallet to pay an omnibus driver, I accidently tore one in two. I send it enclosed. If you
>
> [page break]
>
> can pass it will you send me another? As I wish to return the dollar to the fellow that owned the watch. You know Scott charged me nothing for fixing it. Love to all. Remember me to all the good people of C.
>
> Yours affectionately,
>
> Alex F. Hubbell.
>
> P.S. Regards to the Capt. Luther is well. Send a U.S. note if possible.

## Editorial Notes

Alexander is savoring the afterglow of home. The furlough has recharged him — he's playful, teasing Frances about the Frenchmen presentation, wondering about Charley and the fat Frenchman. The temperance passage is quietly powerful: a half-pint of whiskey every day, which he gives away because of a promise to his mother. In a war where alcohol was medicine, warmth, and escape, Alexander abstains on the strength of a maternal vow.
