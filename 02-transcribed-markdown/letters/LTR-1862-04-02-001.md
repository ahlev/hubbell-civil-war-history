## Full Transcription

> April 2, 1862
> Hampton, Va.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I received a letter the day before I left Alexandria, eight miles from Washington. We were in camp there two days, left last Sat. morning, and took the steam boat for Fortress Monroe, Va. We were two days coming a distance of nearly two hundred miles. I for one can write that it was anything but a pleasant trip. Hardly had room to lay down at night. In regard to grub, we had hard crackers and no coffee, but then we are soldiers and of course we have to put up with whatever comes in our way.
>
> The Chesapeake Bay is a large body of water. Could not see land on either side of the boat coming through. We are now about four miles from Fortress Monroe. I have not had a chance to see the Fort yet. We are pened up like sheep — cannot get out without a pass signed by the Gen., but then that is all right for a great many go out for the purpose of getting drunk.
>
> The rebels leave their marks everywhere they go. This place, for instance, is worse than Harpers Ferry, every building burnt to the ground. We left our tents at Alexandria, but the Government has supplied us with rubber blankets. By putting four of them together, makes a very good tent. We left our tents at Alexandria, but the Government has supplied us with rubber blankets. By putting four of them together, makes a very good tent. Green grass around here. Very fine weather. Write often. Direct your letters the same as before.
>
> I remain your aff. Son,
>
> Henry.
>
> I received a letter from Aunt Laura and Nellie with her likeness. Have not answered yet. I commenced a letter before we started, but did not have time to finish it.

## Editorial Notes

Henry's arrival at Hampton marks the beginning of the Peninsula Campaign for the 34th NY. His observation that Hampton is "worse than Harpers Ferry, every building burnt" documents the Confederate scorched-earth destruction of August 1861. The rubber blanket tents (four blankets combined) represent field improvisation — the regiment left its proper tents at Alexandria for rapid deployment. The Chesapeake Bay crossing ("could not see land on either side") was likely Henry's first experience of open water.
