## Full Transcription

> Dec. 1st, 1863
> General Hospital
> Tullahoma, Tenn.
>
> Dear Mother:
>
> I presume you will be surprised to hear from me from this place. Well here I am in the hospital. I was wounded at the storming of Lookout Mountain. I am not seriously hurt. I am wounded in my left side by a minie ball. Rather a close call for my life, I think. I shall soon be back to the regiment. God has been very good to me. Company H lost as follows as near as I can learn: George Hays, killed; Sergeants Hubbell and Long, Corporals Ish and Kelly, Private Sidney Rider, wounded. I am not positive whether Sergt. Long was wounded or not. Latter was all right the last I saw or heard of him. We made a grand bayonet charge at Lookout. The rebs ran like good fellows. He took the whole posse (1800) prisoner. Please write often and direct as usual. I think I shall be with the regt. soon. Love to all.
>
> Your affectionate son
>
> Alexander F. Hubbell

## Editorial Notes

Alexander lists his own name on the casualty report: "Sergeants Hubbell and Long, Corporals Ish and Kelly, Private Sidney Rider, wounded." The soldier's habit of recording casualties in orderly military format — even when the soldier himself is among the wounded — is a small act of discipline that reveals everything about who Alexander is.

"Rather a close call for my life, I think." The understatement of a man who has been under fire at Antietam, Chancellorsville, Gettysburg, Wauhatchie, and now Lookout Mountain. A minie ball in the left side could easily have been fatal — the lungs, spleen, and kidneys are all in the impact zone. Alexander downplays it: "I shall soon be back to the regiment." Whether he believes this or is protecting Mother is impossible to know.

The family's worst fear — short of death — has materialized. Mother's efforts to get Alexander discharged were nine days too late.
