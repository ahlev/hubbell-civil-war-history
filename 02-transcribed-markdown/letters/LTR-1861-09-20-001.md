## Full Transcription

> September 20/61
> Ogdensburgh
>
> My Dear Mother —
>
> Here I am safe and sound in the barracks. How strange it does seem. Ah! what a bedlam! You said you wished me to tell you everything. We had a very pleasant journey. The cars were very noisy most of the time. We had at last subsided into something like quiet, when a half tipsy fellow sung out "Strike up there forward (the fifer and drummer) or they will think we are all dead". We had it then in good earnest.
>
> The machine shop (a large building) is used as a dining room. Two other large buildings, as sleeping houses. The buildings are provided with tiers of berths (made of hemlock boards and filled with straw). When we came off the cars (the barracks are close to the railroad) we were formed in double file and marched to the parade ground and dismissed. I was surprised to see the ground surrounded with armed sentinels. (There are some fellows above my head singing "Am I a follower of the lamb.") In a short time we were formed in double file and marched to dinner which consisted of soup, bread, boiled pork, cold slum, I potatoes. Very good, I think. That ginger bread went well. I have no more time. You must excuse this, people knocking my elbows and making noise have quite put me out.
>
> Your affectionate son
>
> F. A. Hubbell (Alex)

## Significance Flags

### Historical Significance: `notable`

> Alexander's first letter home, written from the Ogdensburgh barracks: "Here I am safe and sound... How strange it does seem. Ah! what a bedlam!" He marvels at hemlock-board berths stuffed with straw, the parade ground ringed with armed sentinels, and a half-tipsy soldier on the cars who hollered for the fifer and drummer to "strike up." Dinner was soup, boiled pork, and potatoes — but it was the ginger bread from home that "went well."

### Emotional Intensity: `moderate`

> Mix of wonder ("How strange it does seem"), humor (the tipsy fifer request), and slight overwhelm ("quite put me out").

## Editorial Notes

Alexander's first letter is a vivid arrival narrative — the noisy train, the tipsy soldier demanding music, the straw berths, armed sentinels, and the ginger bread from home. His tone is wide-eyed and energetic, a stark contrast to Henry's increasingly seasoned observations from Washington. The "(Alex)" notation after "F. A. Hubbell" suggests the transcriber added clarification, since the family had multiple sons writing. Alexander enlisted despite Henry's explicit advice against it (LTR-1861-09-11-001), arriving at Ogdensburgh roughly 9 days after Henry's letter was written.
