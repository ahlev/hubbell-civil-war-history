## Full Transcription

> THE FREDERICK A. HUBBELL FAMILY
> June 1861 thru July 1965
>
> Atlanta, Georgia
> October 28, 1996
>
> The following letters were transcribed by my mother, from the original hand written pages, in what was a true labor of love. The originals had been stored in a trunk in a basement, and many of them were somewhat water stained. The transcribing was very tedious in some instances and took over two and a half years, from early 1947 to the latter part of 1949. This was painstakenly accomplished and are true copies of the originals insofar as both spelling and grammatical useage are concerned, along with notations of when they were entirelly illegible, or with portions missing. The Hubbell home was Champlain, upper state New York.
>
> All of these were communications between four brothers, their mother, their sister, and in a few instances, with others; while they were in the Union Army during the civil war.
>
> The U. S. Army identifications were as follows:
>
> Charles F. Hubbell — Co. I, 153rd Reg't, New York Volunteers. Charles died in 1875 as a result of wounds suffered during the war.
>
> Henry Hubbell, Mo C 58119 — Co. D, 34th Reg't, New York Volunteers. Henry was killed at battle of Antietam, Sept. 17, 1862.
>
> James Hubbell — Co. I, 153rd Reg't, New York Volunteers. James died on the way home from Savannah, Ga. Oct. 19, 1865.
>
> Alexander F. Hubbell, WC 658842 — Co. H, 60th Reg't, New York Infantry. Alexander died on his farm at Fonda, Iowa in November 1894.
>
> Alexander Fullerton Hubbell, listed above, was my grandfather. His father (my Great Grand father) Frederick Agustus Hubbell, passed away in Apr. 1853 when Alex was only nine years old.
>
> Trusting that these letters may be of interest to others, as well as with my children, I am,
>
> sincerely,
> Fred Alexander Hubbell, Jr.

## Notes

This document is critical for understanding the provenance chain. We are reading typewritten transcriptions made 1947-1949 from water-damaged handwritten originals, then scanned in 2025. This means: (1) legibility issues in our scans may reflect the typewriter transcription, not the originals; (2) the transcriber noted illegible passages, so `[illegible]` markings come from the 1947-1949 transcriber, not from us; (3) spelling preserved from originals per the transcriber's stated intent.

Notable: Charles and James served in the same regiment (153rd NY Volunteers, Co. I). Henry was in a different regiment (34th NY). Alexander was in yet another (60th NY). This means the letters will track at least three different units through the war.

Three of the four brothers died as a result of the war (Henry KIA 1862, James died returning home 1865, Charles died of wounds 1875). Only Alexander survived to old age.

The father died in 1853 when Alexander was 9, meaning the mother raised these boys alone and then sent them to war. The mother's first name is not given here — a data gap to fill from census or pension records.
