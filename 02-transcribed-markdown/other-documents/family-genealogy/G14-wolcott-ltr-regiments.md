> WOLCOTT W. HUBBELL
> 1000 North [Virgo?] Ave
> Pasadena, Calif. 91107
>
> January 20, 1971.
>
> Mr. Frederick A. Hubbell, Jr.,
> 1847 Ravenswood Way, N. E.,
> Atlanta, Ga.
>
> Dear Fred:
>
> Inclosed in this package are several old photographs of the Hubbell children, your father and uncle and aunts, as they appeared 'way back in the Gay Nineties. These pictures were sent to me by the Mount Dora, Fla. undertaker who handled [someone's] last rites, and that has now been quite a few years past.
>
> I have been intending to send them to you, as rightfully yours, but they have been put away in some obscure place, and the idea to actually get them on their way just has not coincided with having them right at hand to act on. Better late than never, of course, even if it is not the best way of things.
>
> There are two pictures of your father, and I am sending them both, retaining none for myself. Likewise I am sending none of me, as I believe you could not be less interested in them. The age notations were given to me many years ago (except as noted) by my mother, and were written on the back of each photo of my set. During the years my set got mixed in with the others, and when they finally came through to me all were not there. You can be sure, however, that the ages as given are approximately correct.
>
> I am using an old address of yours that I happen to have, and hope that this package will get through to you safe and sound. It will come first class, so should be returned to me in case of non-delivery.
>
> I was 80 years old on last October 24th. I have far-outlived my immediate family and also my generation, and why this should be I knoweth not. Outside of being badly crippled by arthritis, and somewhat handicapped by urology troubles I am sound and hearty, with a sound heart and a young man's blood-pressure. I will be facing that bothersome prostate operation within the next few weeks, and this has hastened my clearing up of all pending matters (such as these photos) that have been hanging over my head like the sword of Democles.

### Page 2

> It will not be necessary to acknowledge this shipment. I do not want to ask for anything that might cause you distress, especially as you seem to have been reared in an unswerving belief that in the case of the differences between your father and me that the two sides of the question were his side and wrong side.
>
> I wish you well, and also your sister. I hope that these old photographs will fill a gap in your records of the Hubbell family.
>
> Along this line, have you ever obtained the Civil War records of your grandfather Hubbell? They are obtainable from the General Services Administration, National Archives and Records Service. Write them for application blanks, and when received fill in and return with $1.00 fee. Make the request out for
>
> Alexander F. Hubbell, WC 658842,
> Co. "H", 60th New York Regiment
> New York Infantry.

## Key Data Extracted

### Wolcott W. Hubbell — Identity
- **Alexander's son** (refers to "your grandfather Hubbell" when writing to Fred Jr.)
- Born October 24, 1890 (age 80 in Jan 1971)
- Living in Pasadena, California
- Married to Priscilla (~1921, "almost fifty years")
- Health: arthritis, urology issues, prostate surgery pending, but "sound heart and young man's blood-pressure"
- Had outlived his entire generation

### Family Dynamics
- **Family rift** between Wolcott and Fred Sr. (Fred Jr.'s father): "the differences between your father and me" — Fred Jr. apparently raised to believe Wolcott was in the wrong
- Despite the rift, Wolcott sends family photographs and Civil War information
- References "your sister" — Fred Jr. had a sister (Emily Winn Hubbell, later married Bruce Levitt)

### Historical Photographs
- Old photographs from "the Gay Nineties" (1890s) of Hubbell children, including Fred Sr.
- Originally held by a Mount Dora, Florida undertaker (who handled someone's last rites)
- Age notations on backs written by Wolcott's mother (Lois Wood Hubbell)

### Civil War Records — Instructions
Wolcott provides precise instructions for obtaining military records from National Archives:
- Application blanks from General Services Administration
- $1.00 fee per request
- Pension and land grant records available
- Additional "jacket-envelope" records from bi-monthly returns

## Notes

This letter is significant beyond the military data. It reveals:
1. Alexander had a son named **Wolcott Wood Hubbell** (named for the Wolcott line and the Wood family — Lois Wood)
2. A family rift existed that apparently lasted decades
3. Wolcott knew the pension/claim numbers and military details from memory at age 80
4. The tone is gracious despite acknowledged estrangement — he sends the photographs as "rightfully yours"
