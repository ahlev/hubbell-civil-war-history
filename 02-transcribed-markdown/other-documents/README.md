## Four Layers of Preservation

This collection exists because four generations of one family refused to let these words disappear.

### Layer 1 — Gladys Sands Hubbell (1947–1949)

Alexander's daughter-in-law. She spent two and a half years painstakingly transcribing deteriorating handwritten letters from a trunk in a basement. Water-stained, fading — she preserved them in typewritten form, faithful to every misspelling, every grammatical choice, noting every passage too damaged to read. A true labor of love.

### Layer 2 — Fred Alexander Hubbell Jr. (1996)

Alexander's grandson. He wrote the [introduction letter](DOC-INTRO-1996-10-28-001.md) — identifying the four brothers, their regiments, their fates. He placed his family's story in context and entrusted it to future generations.

### Layer 3 — Bruce Levitt (2025)

Emily's husband, Alexander Hubbell Levitt's father. He scanned the typewritten collection, preserving it digitally for the next generation.

### Layer 4 — Alexander Hubbell Levitt (2025–2026)

Named for the soldier. Using modern technology to understand the stories and history within these letters in ways no previous generation could — drawing meaning from deep family connection, tracing emotional arcs, mapping movements, building a living archive from 160-year-old words.

## The Four Brothers

All four sons of Frederick Augustus Hubbell (d. Apr 1853) and their mother, raised in Champlain, New York. Their mother sent them all to war.

| Brother | Regiment | Fate |
|---------|----------|------|
| **Henry Hubbell** | Co. D, 34th NY Volunteers | Killed at Antietam, Sep 17, 1862 |
| **Alexander F. Hubbell** | Co. H, 60th NY Infantry | Wounded at Lookout Mountain; survived. Died Nov 1894, Fonda, Iowa |
| **Charles F. Hubbell** | Co. I, 153rd NY Volunteers | Died 1875 of war wounds |
| **James Hubbell** | Co. I, 153rd NY Volunteers | Died Oct 19, 1865 returning from Savannah |

**Frances (Fannie) Hubbell** — their sister — and Mother ran the Champlain household, managed finances, and wrote faithfully throughout the war. Frances is the recipient of 38 letters and co-authored one (LTR-1862-11-10-001).

## Source Provenance

All source documents originate from the Hubbell family collection, scanned and organized by Bruce Levitt (2025). Original materials include:

- Typewritten letter transcriptions (Gladys Sands Hubbell, 1947–1949)
- Official military records (U.S. Army muster rolls, pension files, National Archive)
- Published genealogies and county histories
- Family research notes and presentations
- Cemetery records and vital documents
