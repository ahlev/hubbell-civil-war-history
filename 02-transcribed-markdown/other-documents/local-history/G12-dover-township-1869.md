# G12 — Dover Township, Pocahontas County, Iowa: Early Residents and Settlers

## Transcription

**Source:** http://www.rootsweb.com/~iapocaho/esdover.htm
**Compiled by:** Tim Meyer, copyright 2003-2004

### The Town of Varina

The town of Varina is located on the NE corner of section 31. This land was originally settled by Jacob Hauser. Jacob was born in Germany in 1864, the son of David Hauser. His brothers were named David, Nicholas and Matthew. In 1890 he married Minnie Porath of Buena Vista county. Jacob platted the first addition to Varina in December 1899. In 1901 he was chosen a member of the first town council of Varina.

### First Settlers (1869 and early 1870)

**Blakeslee, Alvin C** — arrived in May of 1870. He located on section 32. He had a family and entered his land as a homestead.

**Hubbell, Alex** — the first settler in the township. He purchased section 26 in May of 1869. He located on his land in April, 1870, with his brother Charles.

**Hubbell, Charles** — brother of Alex.

**O'Niel, James** — and his brother John B. They, along with John's wife, located here in 1870. In 1873 John and his family moved to Nebraska, and in 1874 James died at the home of John Garvey.

**Woodin, D M** — arrived in May of 1870. He had a family and entered his land in section 24 as a homestead.

### Settlers Prior to Township Establishment (Sept 6, 1870)

Carpenter, William M; Closson, A C; O'Niel, James; O'Niel, John B; Rathbun, William Wallace (b. 1843 Lee County Iowa, homestead section 32, m. Harriet Robinson 1871, teacher, removed to Fonda 1882); Reilly, Bernard E; Reilly Sr., Bernard (b. 1816 Louth County Ireland, d. 1887).

### Alexander Fullerton Hubbell — Full Biography

**Hubbell, Alexander Fullerton** — was the first settler in Dover township. Alexander was born on March 28, 1844, the fourth son of Frederick A and Frances L (nee McNeil, born 1808) Hubbell in Champlain, Clinton county, New York. He also had a sister named Julia who died in 1859. His father was a well known attorney. His family was of Welsh descent and can trace their family back over 800 years, as far back as the Danish conquest of England. His mother lived with Alexander in Dover township until her demise on May 29, 1890. She was a native of Charlotte, Vermont, and married Frederick A Hubbell in 1833. Four of her other sons were in the civil war. Henry died at Antietam and James died on his way home. Another son, Charles F, died a few years after the war (1875) from an illness contracted during his service. Alexander joined the war on September 21, 1861 in his 18th year. (Ed note — there is quite an extensive civil war narrative in this biography)

In the spring of 1869 Alexander travelled to Dover township on horseback from Fort Dodge and purchased all of section 26, which he later called the "large pasture". He was very prominent in the township and held many official offices. In 1887 he married Lois A Wood, daughter of A B P Wood. Their children were Frederic Augustus, Affa, Wolcot Wood and Helen.

### Other Notable Settlers (post-1870, selected entries)

**Barnes, John** (b. 1852 Dubuque County Iowa) — arrived 1872, section 27, m. Phoebe Edith Miller 1877.

**Chamberlin, Percius R** (b. 1823 Vermont) — 1873, soldier's claim 160 acres section 20. Civil War: Co. B, 44th Wisconsin Infantry, Sept 26 1864 to July 2 1865.

**Coleman, James Henry** (b. 1850 Derby CT) — 1876, section 13. School treasurer, township assessor.

**Fitzgerald, Daniel** (b. 1844 Ireland) — 1873, section 23. Ten children.

**Fitzgerald, John** (b. 1836 Ireland) — 1873, section 23, farm grew to 340 acres.

**Fitzgerald, Patrick** (b. 1834 Ireland) — 1873, section 23, farm to 320 acres, d. 1898. Eleven children.

**Fitzgerald, William** (b. 1840 Ireland) — 1874, section 13. Civil War: Co. F, 6th Iowa Cavalry, 1862.

**Garton, William Henry** — arrived 1880, m. Sarah Josephine Latin 1865.

**Garvey, John** (b. May 5, 1848 Ireland) — 1873, section 21, m. Sophia Williamson 1873.

**Gombar, Frederick Michael** (b. 1853 Broadhead WI) — 1878, section 30, m. Alice McCarl 1873.

**Hogan, Michael Augustus** (b. 1855 Delaware County Iowa) — 1877, m. Catherine Cullen 1886, County Supervisor 1895-1900.

**McCarty, Ann (Mrs. Michael)** (b. 1815) — 1876, with children William, Michael, Maggie.

**Merchant, Peter** (b. 1818 Pennsylvania) — 1871, section 30, m. Susan Weaver.

**Needham, Horace Moulton** (b. 1849 Massachusetts) — ~1874, section 21, m. Emma C Atwood 1872.

**O'Conner, Patrick** (b. 1850 Ireland) — 1878, m. Ellen Durigan 1874.

**Pinneo, George Oliver** (b. 1838 Yates County NY) — 1871, section 30. Civil War: Co. I, Iowa 46th, May 2 1864. Died 1891.

**Sayre, Mahlon** (b. 1817 New Jersey) — 1873, section 20. Broom maker. Both he and wife Lucinda died in 1890.

**Sayre, Electa (nee Haven)** (b. 1826 Vermont) — 1877, Dover township. Married William Sayre 1844, who died 1861.

**Steiner, David** (b. 1826 Germany) — 1871, section 30. Civil War: Aug 11 1862. M. Sarah (nee Harrison) Thompson.

**Taft, Harrison** (b. 1844 St. Lawrence County NY) — 1872. Civil War: Co. E, 65th Illinois Infantry. M. Martha Melinda Burdick 1872.

## Notes

- **Alexander as first settler:** This source independently confirms Alexander Fullerton Hubbell as the first settler in Dover Township, purchasing all of section 26 in May 1869, arriving on the land in April 1870 with brother Charles. He traveled on horseback from Fort Dodge. The "large pasture" name for section 26 is a family detail.
- **Sister Julia:** This is the only document mentioning a sister named Julia who died in 1859. G03 and G10 list only brothers (Frances, Charles, Henry, James, Alexander). Julia may have been omitted from Emily's line documents because she died young before the Civil War.
- **"Fourth son":** The biography calls Alexander "the fourth son," confirming the birth order. G03 lists him fifth among children but that includes daughter Frances.
- **Welsh descent / 800 years:** The biography states "His family was of Welsh descent and can trace their family back over 800 years, as far back as the Danish conquest of England." This aligns with the etymology in G09 (Hubba, Danish chief + Anglo-Saxon "hill"). The "800 years" from ~1904 would reach back to ~1100 AD.
- **Mother's death confirmed:** Frances L. McNeil Hubbell died May 29, 1890 in Dover Township, living with Alexander. This matches G03 exactly.
- **Children named differently:** This source lists the first son as "Frederic Augustus" and the third as "Wolcot Wood" (single 't'). G03/G10 use "Frederick Alexander" and "Wolcott Wood." The name "Frederic Augustus" here may reflect the original naming after the grandfather Frederick Augustus Hubbell, with "Frederick Alexander" being a later usage or the son's own preference.
- **Brother Charles:** Charles is mentioned both as a fellow settler (arriving April 1870) and as dying in 1875 from Civil War illness. This is Charles F. Hubbell.
- **No death date for Alexander in this source.** The biography was published ~1904, so Alexander was already dead, but his death date is not stated in the surviving text. This may be due to the extent of the civil war narrative that was summarized with an editorial note rather than fully transcribed.
- **Civil War contemporaries:** Several other Dover Township settlers were also Civil War veterans: Chamberlin (44th Wisconsin), Fitzgerald/William (6th Iowa Cavalry), Pinneo (Iowa 46th), Steiner, and Taft (65th Illinois). Alexander served in the 60th New York Infantry.
