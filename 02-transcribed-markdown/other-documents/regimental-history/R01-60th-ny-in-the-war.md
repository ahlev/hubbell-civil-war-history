# 60th New York State Volunteers — Regimental History & Resources

## Regimental Overview

The 60th New York Infantry, known as the **"St. Lawrence Regiment,"** was organized at Ogdensburg, N.Y., and mustered in **October 30, 1861**. The regiment left the state for Baltimore, Md., on November 4, 1861.

## Unit Assignments

- Dix's Division — to March 1862
- Railroad Brigade, Army of the Potomac — to June 1863
- 2nd Brigade, Sigel's Division, Dept. of the Shenandoah — to June 26, 1862
- 2nd Brigade, 2nd Division, 2nd Corps, Pope's Army of Virginia — to August 1862
- 3rd Brigade, 2nd Division, 2nd Corps, Army of Virginia — to September 1862
- 3rd Brigade, 2nd Division, 12th Army Corps, Army of the Potomac — to October 1862
- 2nd Brigade, 2nd Division, 12th Army Corps — to May 1863
- 3rd Brigade, 2nd Division, 12th Army Corps, Army of the Potomac — to October 1863; then Army of the Cumberland to April 1864
- 3rd Brigade, 2nd Division, 20th Army Corps, Army of the Cumberland — to July 1865

## Service Record

**1861-1862:** Duty at Baltimore, Md., and between there and Washington, D.C.; also at Relay House, Md., and Harper's Ferry, W. Va., until June 1862. Defense of Harper's Ferry May 28-30. Operations in the Shenandoah Valley until August.

**Pope's Campaign (August-September 1862):** Northern Virginia August 16 - September 2. Sulphur Springs August 24. Battle of Groveton August 29. Bull Run August 30.

**Maryland Campaign (September 1862):** September 6-22. **Battle of Antietam, Md., September 16-17.** Duty at Bolivar Heights until December. Reconnaissance to Rippon, W. Va., November 9. Expedition to Winchester December 2-6. March to Fredericksburg, Va., December 9-16. Duty at Fairfax until January 20, 1863.

**1863:** "Mud March" January 20-24. Chancellorsville Campaign April 27 - May 6. **Battle of Chancellorsville May 1-5.** Gettysburg Campaign June 11 - July 24. **Battle of Gettysburg, Pa., July 1-3** (Culp's Hill). Pursuit of Lee to Manassas Gap, Va., July 5-24. Duty on line of the Rappahannock until September 24.

**Western Theater (Late 1863):** Movement to Bridgeport, Ala., September 24 - October 3. Duty in Lookout Valley until November. Reopening Tennessee River October 26-29. Chattanooga-Ringgold Campaign November 23-27. **Battles of Lookout Mountain November 23-24.** Mission Ridge November 25. Ringgold Gap, Taylor's Ridge, November 27. Duty at Bridgeport, Ala., until May 1864. Veterans on furlough December 1863 - January 1864.

**Atlanta Campaign (1864):** May 1 - September 8. Operations about Rocky Faced Ridge, Tunnel Hill and Buzzard's Roost May 8-11. Battle of Resaca May 14-15. Near Cassville May 19. New Hope Church May 25. Battles about Dallas, New Hope Church and Allatoona Hills May 26 - June 5. Operations about Marietta and against Kenesaw Mountain June 10 - July 2. Pine Mountain June 11-14. Ackworth June 12. Lost Mountain June 15-17. Gilgal or Golgotha Church June 15. Muddy Creek June 17. Noyes Creek June 19. Kolb's Farm June 22. Assault on Kenesaw June 27. Ruff's Station, Smyrna Camp Ground, July 4. Chattahoochie River July 6-17. Peach Tree Creek July 19-20. Siege of Atlanta July 22 - August 25. Operations at Chattahoochie River Bridge August 26 - September 2. Occupation of Atlanta September 2 to November 15. Expedition from Atlanta to Tuckum's Cross Roads October 26-29. Near Atlanta November 9.

**March to the Sea (1864):** November 15 - December 10. Near Davisboro November 28. Siege of Savannah December 10-21.

**Campaign of the Carolinas (1865):** January to April 1865. North Edisto River, S.C., February 12-13. Battle of Bentonville, N.C., March 19-21. Occupation of Goldsboro March 24. Advance on Raleigh April 9-13. Occupation of Raleigh April 14. Bennett's House April 26. Surrender of Johnston and his army. March to Washington, D.C., via Richmond, Va., April 29 - May 20. Grand Review May 24.

**Mustered out July 17, 1865.**

## Casualties

Regiment lost during service:
- **3 Officers** and **64 Enlisted men** killed and mortally wounded
- **5 Officers** and **96 Enlisted men** by disease
- **Total: 168**

## Published Works on the 60th NY

### Richard Eddy (Chaplain)
**HISTORY OF THE SIXTIETH REGIMENT, NEW YORK STATE VOLUNTEERS**, from the commencement of its organization in July, 1861, to its public reception at Ogdensburgh as a veteran command, January 7th, 1864. Published: Philadelphia, Pub. by the author, 1864. xii, 360 pp., 20 cm.

- LC Call No.: E523.5 60th
- Control No.: 8195080
- Author dates: Richard Eddy, 1828-1906
- **Scarce title** — expect to pay $250-$395 for original. No reprint yet available.
- One known copy inscribed and signed by Chaplain Eddy to A. F. Stickney — priced at $395.
- Note: "This is a good regimental but is very difficult to find... The only flaw I see in this book is that the author was not present at Chancellorsville and Gettysburg. This section appears as a supplement written by the Chaplain."

### R. L. Murray
**A PERFECT STORM OF LEAD: GEORGE SEARS GREENE AND HIS NEW YORK BRIGADE IN DEFENSE OF CULP'S HILL.** Benedum Books, 80 pp. Maps, Photos. Details the battle at Culp's Hill on the federal right on July 2 and 3, 1863. Softcover — $8.95 (from C. Clayton Thompson, Bookseller, Boone, NC).

## Notes

1. **Hubbell Family Connection:** The annotation "BROTHER KILLED: Hubbell, Henry, 22, s,, Champlain, Antietam" appears at the top of the source document, indicating this file was compiled or annotated by a family researcher. Henry Hubbell was the brother of Alexander Fullerton Hubbell (see L09, L10). The double comma after "s" (single) likely represents a blank field (perhaps rank or company).

2. **Source Provenance:** This document is a web compilation — not a single primary source. It aggregates information from Dyer's Compendium, the Library of Congress catalog entry for Eddy's book, and a Long Island genealogy website with extensive Civil War links. The "Stan Maine" and "Michael Nicosia" references suggest it circulated among Civil War researchers via email in the early internet era.

3. **Eddy Book Scarcity:** The 1864 regimental history by Chaplain Eddy is noted as extremely scarce ($250-$395 for originals). The signed/inscribed copy to A. F. Stickney was priced at $395. No reprint was available at the time of this compilation.

4. **Culp's Hill Significance:** The 60th NY played a notable role in the defense of Culp's Hill at Gettysburg under Gen. George Sears Greene, significant enough to warrant a dedicated book by R. L. Murray.

5. **Cross-references:** Henry Hubbell's death at Antietam connects to the McDonald condolence letter (L10), cemetery records (G11), and family lineage documentation (G01).
