## Key Facts

- **Alexander F. Hubbel** [sic — surname misspelled with single 'l'] was enrolled as a **Corporal** at age 18 on September 21, 1861, at Ogdensburgh, by mustering officer David Day
- He traveled 112 miles to reach the mustering point
- His close companion **Amos U. Luther** (mentioned frequently in the 1863 letters as "Luther") was also a Corporal, age 18, enrolled a month later on Oct 19
- **Percy Stacy** (Private #65) is likely the "Stacie" mentioned in Charles's Feb 13 letter when "Lieut. Knapp stopped and shook hands with Mrs. Burdick. He called for Hubbell and Stacie."
- The company was mustered by Capt. (later Lt. Col.) David Day, who appears frequently throughout the muster rolls
- The company had at least 78 privates plus officers/NCOs — a large company
- Two musicians (William and George Masury, ages 17 and 15) were among the youngest

## Notes

- The muster roll spells the name "Hubbel" (single L) — this is a common variant. All other family documents use "Hubbell."
- Alexander was enrolled as a Corporal from the start (Sept 21, 1861). G03 (Emily's line) says he was "Promoted Sgt, Oct 21, 1862" — so he was promoted from Corporal to Sergeant a year into service.
- The muster roll is an official U.S. military document — data quality A for all data it contains.
- The "travel miles" column (112 for Alexander) represents the distance from home to the mustering point at Ogdensburgh.
- Champlain, NY to Ogdensburgh, NY is approximately 112 miles — consistent with Alexander's hometown.
- Cross-references: G01, G03, G11 (all reference military service), L04 (letters mention Capt. Ransom, Luther, Stacy), R01 (regimental history)
- **Company identification**: This is almost certainly **Company H** based on its position in the roll (8th company listed) and the fact that G03/G11 both identify Alexander as "Co. H" while this is Capt. Ransom's company (who writes a letter in L04).
