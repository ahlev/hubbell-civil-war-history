Hubbell Family Archive · 1861–1865

The Hubbell Brothers

Four brothers. Four years of war. Two hundred and seventy-three letters across the distance between them.

“‘Way down South in Dixie.’ That is where I am. And a beautiful country too if it were not cursed by the existence of slavery. I am encamped on the summit of a high hill and the view is very fine. Green fields, meadows, large farms, patches of woods, roads winding here and there… all of this spread out before me like a beautiful painting.

I was sitting on the hill just now watching the sun as it was setting behind the Blue Ridge. The soft notes of the bugle were floating through the air when I almost forgot I was a soldier…”

Alexander Hubbell Pine Mountain, VA · July 14, 1862 · (Age 18)

“…But if the drum should beat to arms tomorrow, there will be a different scene. Troops will be forming in line of battle… the skirmishers will be thrown out, and then the earth will fairly rock.”

Alexander Hubbell Pine Mountain, VA · July 14, 1862

The Letters

What they wrote home.

Not the heroic correspondence of myth — young men writing to their mother about being sick among strangers, about money, about the long grinding weeks between battles. Read together, an unedited record of what the war felt like from inside it.

The Preservation

A trunk in a basement. Five generations of care.

Bundled away after the war, water-stained and nearly lost — then transcribed, introduced, digitized, and finally analyzed, each by a different descendant, each in a different decade.
