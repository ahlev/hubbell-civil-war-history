# Site v2 Graphic Revamp — Prompting Jump-Off

> **Purpose of this file:** This is the next prompt to run. It lays the groundwork for extending the v2 landing page's visual style, art direction, and storytelling into the rest of the site's pages — while preserving each page's data-rich substance as the focus. Read it in full before scoping any work.

---

## The Ask (Overview)

Since our v2 landing page is looking so good, I want to implement some of the style, art, and storytelling and compelling visual imagery that lends itself well to enriching a narrative on some of our other pages. I have a few thoughts on what that should look like, but I want to lay some groundwork in terms of how we should approach it.

I really like the **impressionist oil painting style** that we've been using in our images, and I think we should keep that consistent and steady for the future images that we may generate and animate. The brush technique, general artistic and paint technique, and medium should follow a similar visual style.

The **animation** should also be at a similar level of depth, where only certain textures and hero figures or elements really receive animation. Lighting is something that also is often given a little bit of subtle life through animation. Movement of wind, etc.

The general ethos and artistic approach we've used so far is what we should continue for new images. **We may want to reference some of the existing imagery we've created already.** The likenesses of each brother should potentially serve as a personified reference for **character consistency** if we choose to later depict those same brothers at different points of their journeys in different settings with different energy or just in general setting. We might want to show those same individuals depicted in different situations relevant to our narrative.

## 1. The Preservation Narrative (Final v2 Scene → Dedicated Images)

In terms of some of the preservation imagery, we may want to take the image on the **last scene of the v2 landing page** — which speaks to the different layers of preservation that have happened — and break it into dedicated, individually depicted elements. Those layers:

- **The family trunk** that contained the deteriorating letters
- **Great-grandmother typing them** lovingly and painstakingly on a typewriter to preserve their contents
- **A future ancestor digitizing** through a scanner to capture PDF versions of those typewritten letters
- **A more modern digital / computer technological layer** represented by the laptop

There's imagery for each of those elements that already exists within that larger animation splash background for our final scene. We should use that montage as a **direct reference** for more individually depicting those same elements. We want visual consistency, a little bit of dynamic context, and placement of those same scenes and key objects and themes — fine to keep them in the same style.

If we want to render each of the computers separately to represent the **scanner stage** as its own image, and the **modern technology stage** as its own image, a similar graphic depiction to what we have in that one montage image — but pulled into its own image for each section — may be what we want. Again, **use the existing montage imagery that references those things as a direct source-material reference image**, at least that specific piece of it.

### The Missing Step — Grandfather's Letter

There's one step in the preservation narrative that is **not explicitly depicted** in that montage scene: **my grandfather writing a letter and passing the collection on.** I believe the context of that letter is available in the larger collection within our data sources, and that's something we should look for — to explore further how we might bring that to life or include it in the collection in some capacity for readers to see, interact with, and emphasize.

I think that would be an interesting way to approach the narrative of preservation: produce dedicated images representing **each one of those preservation stages**, woven in, again using that one preservation montage from the v2 landing page as a jumping-off point.

### Design Help Needed (Preservation Section)

I'll look for your design help on UI layout and direction:
- Whether to include those images **unobstructed**, or **overlay text** onto them, and to what degree
- How much they should be **animated** — fade in/out, etc.
- How exactly we **position them in space** and arrange around them
- Whether their placement/positioning impacts our approach to **typography layout** or the way the page renders (column width, margins)

All of that we'll want to revisit, but I think that approach could really make sense.

## 3. Data Pages — Imagery as Immersive Lead-In (Substance Stays the Focus)

For our other pages, there's an opportunity to develop compelling imagery that either:
- Serves as an **interactive, artistic landing-page introduction** to the actual interactive charts and data visualizations that are the core substance of this collection and its learnings, **or**
- A **visual nod** that draws the user in and adds to the consistency of visual immersion and portrayal of this *Brothers in the War* narrative.

### Example — The Wellness Ledger

On the wellness ledger, we could have a scene or multiple scenes (maybe just one) that is either:
- A **hero image at the top of the page**, animated, for a headline or key information — a different way to head and introduce the page
- A scene/animation that **fades out before showing the chart** and the data visualization

I'm not sure exactly what I'd look for in your recommendations, but worthwhile imagery could include:
- A **field hospital**
- Soldiers in a more **formal military hospital**
- **Field treatment** of soldiers in a battle or skirmish
- A **post-battle setting**

Military scenes from the era — realistic and relevant to the brothers — that reflect the illness, wellness, medical settings, and health/well-being of the story. Imagery that represents that, brings it to life, and animates it to draw the user in and transition into the substance in an aesthetically pleasing way (with thoughtful layout and design).

### Example — The Map That Moves (Comic-Style Transitioning Scenes)

The idea of almost a **comic style**, but with transitioning scenes and images, could be compelling. For example:
- A **close-up of a soldier marching**
- A **zoom out on the column of soldiers** from an aerial view, showing many acres of organized march in which that close-up was taken — also animated
- A **further zoom out** to show a whole region and the relative small proximity of the Army centered within that much larger Northeastern United States aerial topographic map view — to show the scale of the map and the troop movement

Adding to the narrative that way, through visuals that animate.
