## Notes

Charles Fullerton Hubbell — older than Alexander, younger than Henry — enlisted at Champlain in August 1862 into Company I of the 153rd NY, the company his youngest brother **James** would join in 1864. The official record confirms him cleanly despite the regiment carrying **four** Hubbells (two of them named Charles): he is the Co. I corporal from Champlain, mustered out at Savannah in October 1865. His company commander was **Capt. Davis J. Rich** — the very officer who had led Henry's Company D in the 34th NY (Henry's phonetic "Capt. Reich"), a thread that ties two brothers' regiments together. Charles's letters are among the collection's most observant — he served as a regimental HQ/commissary clerk (a post Lt. Col. Strain arranged) and was remembered as a "newspaper correspondent." The 153rd lost four times as many men to disease as to battle, and it was disease, not a bullet, that followed Charles home: he survived the war, migrated with Alexander to Dover Township, Iowa, and died there in **March 1875** of the illness he had contracted in service — the only brother whose death the war caused but did not witness. His 1875 grave is, for now, undocumented; the pension file that would name his disease is the most valuable thread still to pull.
