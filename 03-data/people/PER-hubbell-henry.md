## Notes

Henry Hubbell is the first voice heard in the collection — his June 6, 1861 letter describes the 34th NY's journey from Albany to Washington via Baltimore in the first weeks of the war. He comes across as earnest, responsible, and eager to reassure his mother. He mentions sending money home ($8), asks about the farm, reassures her about his morals ("you need have no fear of my drinking or smoking or any other vice"), and writes to both his mother and his younger brother Alex and sister Frances on the same day. He is killed 15 months later at Antietam — the bloodiest single day of the Civil War. The introduction notes his pension claim number as Mo C 58119, suggesting a pension was filed (likely by his mother as a dependent).

His commanding officer in the early letters is **Capt. Reich**, Co. D, 34th Regt. — worth verifying against regimental records.
