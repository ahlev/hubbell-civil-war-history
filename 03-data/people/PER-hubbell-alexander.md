## Notes

Alexander Fullerton Hubbell is the collection's anchor voice: the only brother to write across the whole war and survive it. He enlisted at 17–18 as a corporal in Company H of the 60th NY — the "St. Lawrence Regiment" — recruited from his own Clinton County. The official record confirms every load-bearing fact the family preserved: his Champlain enlistment, his rise to sergeant, his veteran reenlistment, and his wounding at **Lookout Mountain** (named in Eddy's 1864 casualty list, "Sergeant A. F. Hubbell, Co. H"). He mustered out July 17, 1865, rode horseback from Fort Dodge to Dover Township, Iowa, bought all of Section 26, and held township office until his death — recorded by the external record as **December 7, 1894** (resolving a family-internal conflict with an alternative 1899 date). The two duty details the letters cherish — that he was a **color bearer** and later served in the **ambulance corps** — are the only service claims the rosters do not yet corroborate; the likeliest external witnesses are a Clinton County newspaper notice (NYS Historic Newspapers) or his pension file. Cross-referencing his letters recovered his whole company circle: Capt. Ransom, his friend Amos Luther, Sgt. Howes, the "orderly" Dickinson, and comrades whose fates the letters never knew — Lt. White dead of typhoid, Pvt. Porter killed by railroad cars near Baltimore, Pvt. Lablue a deserter.
